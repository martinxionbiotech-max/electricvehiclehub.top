## TL;DR

Importing a Chinese EV to Austria means paying the full EU customs stack — **10% standard duty plus a brand-specific countervailing duty of 17% to 35.3%** — and then **20% VAT** on the running total. What Austria does not add is a purchase tax of its own: **NoVA, the registration tax, is fully exempt for battery-electric cars**, so for a BEV the consumer-side stack stays at customs + VAT + a small registration line. On our model a BYD Seal lands at about **$44,431 (+80.0%)** — within $18 of the same car into Germany — while the identical car lands **$10,624 less** after crossing into non-EU Switzerland, whose customs world looks nothing like the EU's.

## Key numbers

- **Import duty:** 10% — the EU's common external tariff on electric passenger cars (TARIC heading 8703.80), identical at every EU border
- **Countervailing duty (CVD):** brand-specific — 17.0% BYD · 18.8% Geely group (Zeekr, Lynk & Co) · 20.7% most other cooperating producers (Neta, Xiaomi, Changan, Leapmotor and others) · 35.3% SAIC brands (MG, Wuling, Maxus) · 7.8% Tesla Shanghai
- **VAT:** 20% on the duty-paid value — recoverable for VAT-registered businesses only within price caps: full deduction up to €40,000, partial between €40,000 and €80,000, none above
- **NoVA:** 0% for BEVs — Austria's *Normverbrauchsabgabe* registration tax fully exempts battery-electric cars
- **Worked example:** BYD Seal $24,690 ex-factory → **$44,431 landed (+80.0%)**; the same car lands at $44,413 in Germany, $45,890 in Sweden and $33,807 in Switzerland
- **Market context:** BEVs took **25.2% of Austrian new-car registrations year-to-date through August 2026** — 53,449 cars, up 31.1% on the same period a year earlier [Source: BEÖ, September 2026]
- **No offsetting subsidy:** the national E-Mobilität 2024 purchase subsidy ended early on 7 February 2025 — nothing on the domestic side absorbs the tariff stack today [Source: European Commission / EAFO]

## The terms that decide this market

- **Landed cost** — the importer-side cost to bring a China-market car to an Austrian buyer, registered and compliant. Base price → import duty → countervailing duty → VAT → freight → clearance → certification → registration → inland transport. The full formula is documented in our [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).
- **MFN duty** — the EU's "most-favoured-nation" tariff of 10% that applies to cars from any country without a free trade agreement covering them. China has no EU car FTA, so 10% is the floor at every EU entry point.
- **Countervailing duty (CVD)** — an anti-subsidy tariff layered on top of the MFN duty. For Chinese BEVs, the EU set definitive, brand-specific rates in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, effective 31 October 2024 for five years.
- **NoVA** — Austria's *Normverbrauchsabgabe*, a registration-stage acquisition tax that most cars pay and that BEVs are **fully exempt from** [Source: European Commission / EAFO]. For an electric import, the line most EU member states use to load a purchase — Ireland's VRT, the Netherlands' bpm — reads zero in Vienna.
- **CIF** — cost, insurance, freight; the customs value. In our model duty applies to the ex-factory value, with freight, clearance and certification carried as a fixed block per market.
- **Compounding, not adding** — the stack multiplies. Duty comes first, then the CVD is calculated on the duty-paid value, then VAT on the running total. Never sum 10 + 35.3 + 20 = 65.3%; the line-item math is the only math that gets it right.
- **The Swiss mirror** — Austria and Switzerland share a border and a language, not a customs union. The same structural divide that separates Sweden from Norway sits $10,624 apart on a BYD Seal here.

## What a Chinese EV pays at the Austrian border

| Layer | Rate for a China-origin BEV | Levied on | In our landed model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Import duty (MFN) | **10%** | CIF value | Yes |
| Countervailing duty | **17% BYD / 18.8% Geely group / 20.7% default / 35.3% SAIC / 7.8% Tesla Shanghai** | duty-paid value | Yes |
| VAT | **20%** | running total (value + duty + CVD) | Yes |
| NoVA | **0% for BEVs** | — | Yes (zero) |
| Registration & plates | ~$200 flat in our records | — | Yes |

Read the table top to bottom and Austria is one of the cleanest stacks in Europe: two customs layers set in Brussels, one VAT rate set in Vienna, a small registration line, and a NoVA of zero. Any EU entry point produces the same customs bill — Hamburg, Rotterdam or an Adriatic port — because the EU is one customs territory; being a landlocked, German-speaking market in Central Europe changes the route, not the rate. That is why "which EU country is cheapest" collapses to the VAT rate plus whatever national registration taxes exist — a spread our [Germany vs Netherlands vs France comparison](/blog/germany-netherlands-france-import-comparison/) works through in detail, and one where Austria wins a point most peers lose: its registration-stage tax is zero for exactly the cars this article is about.

| Neta V | $8,320 | $19,556 | +135.0% |
| MG 4 | $9,690 | $23,606 | +143.6% |

| Xiaomi SU7 | $30,320 | $54,607 | +80.1% |

Two patterns jump out, and both are structural.

First, **the brand you pick matters more than anything else you control.** The MG 4 and the Neta V sit only $1,370 apart ex-factory, yet the MG 4 pays a 35.3% CVD (SAIC) against Neta's 20.7% and lands more than **$4,050** above it. Our catalog maps each brand to its statutory rate — BYD 17%, Geely group 18.8%, SAIC 35.3%, everyone else 20.7% — and on a $25,000 car the gap between the cheapest and the most expensive tier is worth roughly **$6,039** in Austria once VAT compounds on top. That spread is set in Brussels, not Vienna.

Second, **cheap cars wear the premium worst.** A $5,000 Wuling pays the same freight, clearance, certification and registration block as a $60,000 Zeekr — about **$6,300** in our model — so its percentage premium runs to +204.6% while the Zeekr 009 "only" pays +67.0%. Percentage comparisons between a microcar and a luxury MPV tell you almost nothing about either import decision. Absolute landed cost and incremental tax do.

### Worked example: a BYD Seal in Austria

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (ex-factory China) | $24,690 |
| Import duty (10% of base) | $2,469 |
| Countervailing duty (17% of base + duty) | $4,617 |
| VAT (20% of base + duty + CVD) | $6,355 |
| Freight, clearance, certification, registration, inland | $6,300 |
| **Total landed** | **$44,431** |

Formula: `base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs`. Every input comes from EV Hub's tariff and landed-cost records dated 2026-09-15.

One caution on the registration line: our model folds registration into a flat $200 for Austria for comparability, but the official Austrian registration and NoVA filing fees could not be verified from primary sources at the time of writing — treat those specifics as UNKNOWN and confirm with Austrian authorities or a customs broker. Treat $44,431 as a floor, not a showroom estimate.

## Where Austria sits: the German twin and the Swiss mirror

Austria's number reads best against three reference points: the identical customs stack in Germany, the higher-VAT regimes elsewhere in the EU, and the non-union world across the Swiss border. Same BYD Seal, same dataset, different tax regimes:

| Market | Stack | Seal landed | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Switzerland (non-EU) | 4% duty + 0% CVD + 8.1% VAT | $33,807 | +36.9% |
| Norway (non-EU) | 0% duty + 0% CVD + 25% VAT | $38,112 | +54.4% |
| Germany (EU) | 10% + 17% + 19% VAT | $44,413 | +79.9% |
| **Austria (EU)** | **10% + 17% + 20% VAT** | **$44,431** | **+80.0%** |
| Poland (EU) | 10% + 17% + 23% VAT | $45,240 | +83.2% |
| Sweden (EU) | 10% + 17% + 25% VAT | $45,890 | +85.9% |

Denmark's 25% VAT plus a $3,500 registration line pushes the same car to $49,320 (+99.8%), the EU high mark in our dataset. The two comparisons that matter most:

**Germany is a near-twin, not a rival.** The customs bill is identical — same 10%, same 17% for BYD — and the VAT difference of a single point (20% vs 19%) adds $318 on the duty-paid value, while our model's Austrian registration line sits $300 below Germany's. Net difference: **$18**. For a German-speaking buyer, Austria and Germany are effectively the same import market; choosing between them is a logistics-and-service question, not a tariff one.

**Switzerland is the mirror.** Same language, different customs universe: no CVD, a 4% duty, 8.1% VAT. The Swiss result is **$10,624 below** Austria's for the same car. The entire gap is the customs stack — the multiplier Austria applies before VAT is **1.287** (1.10 × 1.17) versus Switzerland's **1.04**, and Swiss VAT applies to a much smaller base. It is the Sweden–Norway story with Alps instead of fjords.

Two footnotes keep the comparison honest. Norway's model applies 25% VAT to the full import value, but the real 2026 rule exempts the first NOK 300,000 — so the Norwegian number shown is a ceiling, not a floor; our [Norway import guide](/blog/norway-import-guide/) covers those mechanics in detail. And every row carries the same fixed import block structure with each market's flat registration line — the customs multiplier is the part that is policy, not modeling. For a fuller EU price ladder, see the [Sweden](/blog/sweden-import-guide/) and [Poland](/blog/poland-import-guide/) guides.

## Why Austria still matters: a NoVA of zero and demand without subsidies

Austria's local tax code is, for once, a non-event for BEV importers — and the demand data says the market rewarded it anyway.

- **NoVA (registration tax):** fully exempt for BEVs [Source: European Commission / EAFO]. One registration-stage tax, zero for the cars this article covers. For non-electric powertrains NoVA is a consumption-based charge; the exact formula for those is outside our dataset and unverified here (UNKNOWN).
- **Purchase subsidy:** the national "E-Mobilität 2024" scheme ended early on 7 February 2025 due to budget limits, with no national replacement announced since [Source: European Commission / EAFO].
- **Ownership tax:** since 1 April 2025, BEVs are no longer exempt from the motor-related insurance tax and are taxed similarly to combustion cars [Source: European Commission / EAFO]. The NoVA exemption remains.
- **Company rules:** zero-emission cars are exempt from ownership and pollution taxes; companies can apply a 10% investment premium, use 30% declining-balance depreciation, and run BEVs as company cars without benefit-in-kind taxation [Source: European Commission / EAFO].
- **VAT recovery caps:** companies may deduct input VAT fully only for BEVs priced up to €40,000; between €40,000 and €80,000 the deduction is partial, and above €80,000 it disappears [Source: European Commission / EAFO]. This is the Austrian rule that decides whether your "net" cost is 20% lower or not.
- **Local layer:** several federal states run their own programmes — Lower Austria has offered €1,000 toward private BEV purchases, and Burgenland up to 50% for private home chargers [Source: European Commission / EAFO].
- **Charging:** Austria counted almost 40,000 public charging points in April 2026, with 4,000 ultrafast points (150 kW or more) — double a year earlier [Source: BMIMI, April 2026].

The market result: 2025 closed with BEVs at 21.3% of new registrations, up 35.9% year-on-year; 2026 through August reached **25.2%** on 53,449 cars, up 31.1% [Source: BEÖ / Statistik Austria]. March 2026 was the record month at 8,206 cars — a quarter of all new registrations [Source: BMIMI]. That growth happened after the purchase subsidy died, which is the Austrian version of the Nordic signal: the demand is structural, and the import wedge is entirely a border-and-VAT story.

## What importers should check before committing

1. **Which CVD tier does your brand sit in?** The difference between 17% and 35.3% is worth about $6,039 on a $25,000 car in Austria. Confirm the rate for the exact exporting producer, not just the badge.
2. **Are you importing as a business or a private individual?** Austrian input-VAT deduction is capped by price band (full to €40,000, partial to €80,000, none above) — a different shape from most EU markets, and private buyers cannot deduct at all. The same car has two different effective costs depending on who registers it.
3. **Budget the registration steps properly.** Our model carries a flat $200; the official Austrian registration and NoVA filing fees could not be verified from primary sources (UNKNOWN) — confirm with Austrian authorities or a broker before wiring money.
4. **Check the homologation route.** A car with EU whole-vehicle type approval can be approved without extra technical documentation; a China-only homologation needs single-vehicle approval, which is slower and costlier — see our [WVTA vs single-vehicle approval](/blog/wvta-vs-single-vehicle-approval/) explainer.
5. **Left-hand drive and CCS2.** Austria is a left-hand-drive market with CCS2 charging; a China-market car needs the right port and charging configuration — our [CCS2 vs GB/T note](/blog/ccs2-vs-gbt-charging/) covers the compatibility cost.
6. **Watch the regulation, not the noise.** The CVD regime is set at EU level (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754). Check EUR-Lex for amendments before committing; any changes beyond the 2024 regulation could not be verified at the time of writing (UNKNOWN).

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view, Austria is the quiet case that proves the point: a German-speaking EU market can sit on one of Europe's heavier customs stacks, tax consumption at 20%, and still watch EV share climb past 25% — because the block-level tariff, not the national tax code, sets the import math. The NoVA zero is the one local decision that matters for importers, and it cuts in their favor; everything else about the Austrian wedge is Brussels plus the standard VAT rate.

**Reasoning.** First, the numbers are tight where it counts: within $18 of Germany on the same car despite different tax codes — so Austria's "cost position" is effectively Germany's, and comparison shopping across the Alps is a service-and-logistics question, not a tariff one. Second, the Swiss mirror shows the size of the structural trade: $10,624 on a single BYD Seal, purely because one side of the border is in the customs union — the same lesson as Sweden vs Norway, in a second language. Third, Austria's demand is genuine and post-subsidy: 21.3% BEV share in 2025 and 25.2% through August 2026 with the purchase bonus gone, a charging network approaching 40,000 points, and company-car rules that keep electric attractive for fleets. The private-buyer math — full 20% VAT, no deduction — stays the harsh part; the business math is where the caps at €40,000 and €80,000 decide real money.
