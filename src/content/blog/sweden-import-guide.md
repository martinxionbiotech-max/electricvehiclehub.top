## TL;DR

Importing a Chinese EV to Sweden means paying the full EU customs stack: **10% standard duty, a brand-specific countervailing duty of 17% to 35.3%, and 25% VAT** on the running total. On our model a BYD Seal lands at about **$45,890 (+85.9%)** — roughly **$7,778 more than the same car into Norway**, which sits outside the EU customs union and levies no duty at all. Sweden is the cleanest mirror in Europe: two Nordic markets, the same 25% headline VAT, one inside the customs union and one outside — and the difference is entirely the tariff stack.

## Key numbers

- **Import duty:** 10% — the EU's common external tariff on electric passenger cars, identical at every EU border (TARIC heading 8703.80)
- **Countervailing duty (CVD):** brand-specific — 17.0% BYD · 18.8% Geely, Zeekr and Lynk & Co · 20.7% most other cooperating producers (Neta, Xiaomi, Changan and others) · 35.3% SAIC brands (MG, Maxus, Roewe) · 7.8% Tesla Shanghai
- **VAT:** 25% on the duty-paid value — the Nordic standard and among the highest rates in the EU; recoverable for VAT-registered businesses, terminal for private buyers
- **Worked example:** BYD Seal $24,690 ex-factory → **$45,890 landed (+85.9%)**; the same car lands at $38,112 in Norway and $44,413 in Germany
- **Market context:** EVs took **45% of Swedish new-car registrations in August 2026** and 42% year-to-date, up from 35% a year earlier [Source: Mobility Sweden, September 2026]
- **No offsetting subsidy:** Sweden scrapped its *klimatbonus* purchase subsidy on 8 November 2022 — nothing on the domestic side absorbs the tariff stack today [Source: European Commission / EAFO]

## The terms that decide this market

- **Landed cost** — the importer-side cost to bring a China-market car to a Swedish buyer, registered and compliant. Base price → import duty → countervailing duty → VAT → freight → clearance → certification → registration → inland transport. The full formula is documented in our [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).
- **MFN duty** — the EU's "most-favoured-nation" tariff of 10% that applies to cars from any country without a free trade agreement covering them. China has no EU car FTA, so 10% is the floor.
- **Countervailing duty (CVD)** — an anti-subsidy tariff layered on top of the MFN duty. For Chinese BEVs, the EU set definitive, brand-specific rates in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, effective 31 October 2024 for five years.
- **CIF** — cost, insurance, freight; the customs value. In our model duty applies to the ex-factory value, with freight, clearance and certification carried as a fixed block per market.
- **Compounding, not adding** — the stack multiplies. Duty comes first, then the CVD is calculated on the duty-paid value, then VAT on the running total. Never sum 10 + 35.3 + 25 = 70.3%; the real number is worse in one way and smaller in another, and only the line-item math gets it right.
- **EU vs EEA** — Sweden is inside both the EU and its customs union; Norway is in the EEA and the single market but outside the customs union. That single structural fact is why a Chinese EV pays duty in Gothenburg and none in Oslo.
- **Bonus-malus** — Sweden's vehicle tax system. The "bonus" (a purchase subsidy) is gone; the "malus" (a higher annual tax on high emitters) remains. Sweden charges no registration tax on imports in the way, say, Ireland's VRT or the Netherlands' bpm do [Source: European Commission / EAFO].

## What a Chinese EV pays at the Swedish border

| Layer | Rate for a China-origin BEV | Levied on | In our landed model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Import duty (MFN) | **10%** | CIF value | Yes |
| Countervailing duty | **17% BYD / 18.8% Geely group / 20.7% default / 35.3% SAIC / 7.8% Tesla Shanghai** | duty-paid value | Yes |
| VAT | **25%** | running total (value + duty + CVD) | Yes |
| Registration & plates | ~$70 flat in our records; the Transport Agency's verification-of-origin fee alone is SEK 1,240 | — | Yes |

Read the table top to bottom and the structure is obvious: Sweden has no domestic purchase tax on cars beyond VAT, but the customs layer is the heaviest in Europe. Any EU entry point produces the same customs bill — Hamburg, Rotterdam or Gothenburg — because the EU is one customs territory. That is why "which EU country is cheapest" collapses to the VAT rate, a point our [Germany vs Netherlands vs France comparison](/blog/germany-netherlands-france-import-comparison/) works through in detail. Sweden just happens to top the VAT league among the big car markets.

## Landed cost by model (Sweden)

Figures from EV Hub's vehicle records: ex-factory China price, plus the brand's CVD rate, 10% duty, 25% VAT, and the fixed import block (freight, clearance, compliance, registration, inland).

| Model | Base (China) | Sweden landed | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wuling Hongguang MINI EV | $5,000 | $15,472 | +209.4% |
| Neta V | $8,320 | $19,978 | +140.1% |
| MG 4 | $9,690 | $24,197 | +149.7% |
| BYD Atto 3 | $16,260 | $32,328 | +98.8% |
| BYD Seal | $24,690 | $45,890 | +85.9% |
| Xiaomi SU7 | $30,320 | $56,490 | +86.3% |
| Zeekr 009 | $61,830 | $107,169 | +73.3% |

Two patterns jump out, and both are structural.

First, **the brand you pick matters more than anything else you control.** The MG 4 and the Neta V sit only $1,370 apart ex-factory, yet the MG 4 pays a 35.3% CVD (SAIC) against Neta's 20.7%, and lands more than $4,200 above it. Our catalog maps each brand to its statutory rate — BYD 17%, Geely group 18.8%, SAIC 35.3%, everyone else 20.7% — and on a $25,000 car the gap between the cheapest and most expensive tier is worth roughly **$6,290** in Sweden once VAT compounds on top. That spread is set in Brussels, not Stockholm.

Second, **cheap cars wear the premium worst.** A $5,000 Wuling pays the same freight, clearance, certification and registration block as a $60,000 Zeekr — about $6,170 in our model — so its percentage premium runs to +209.4% while the Zeekr 009 "only" pays +73.3%. Percentage comparisons between a microcar and a luxury MPV tell you almost nothing about either import decision. Absolute landed cost and incremental tax do.

### Worked example: a BYD Seal in Sweden

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (ex-factory China) | $24,690 |
| Import duty (10% of base) | $2,469 |
| Countervailing duty (17% of base + duty) | $4,617 |
| VAT (25% of base + duty + CVD) | $7,944 |
| Freight, clearance, certification, registration, inland | $6,170 |
| **Total landed** | **$45,890** |

Formula: `base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs`. Every input comes from EV Hub's tariff and landed-cost records dated 2026-09-15.

One caution on the registration line: our model folds registration into a flat $70 per market for comparability, but the Transport Agency's verification-of-origin fee for an imported car is SEK 1,240 on its own, and the registration inspection costs extra. Treat $45,890 as a floor, not a showroom estimate [Source: Swedish Transport Agency].

## Sweden vs Norway: one VAT rate, two customs worlds

This is the comparison the Swedish number is built to make. Norway and Sweden share a 25% headline VAT, a Nordic cost base and a customer who is comfortable with electric cars. What they don't share is a customs union.

| Model | Sweden (10% + CVD + 25% VAT) | Norway (0% + 0% + 25% VAT) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wuling Hongguang MINI EV | $15,472 | $13,500 | +$1,972 |
| MG 4 | $24,197 | $19,363 | +$4,835 |
| BYD Seal | $45,890 | $38,112 | +$7,778 |
| Zeekr 009 | $107,169 | $84,538 | +$22,632 |

The entire gap is the customs stack. For a BYD, the 10% duty and 17% CVD multiply the ex-factory price by **1.287 before VAT is even applied**; for an MG, the SAIC rate of 35.3% pushes that multiplier to **1.488**. In Norway the same multiplier is 1.000 — zero duty, zero CVD. [Source: EU Implementing Regulation 2024/2754; EV Hub records]

Two footnotes keep the comparison honest. Norway's model in our catalogue applies the full 25% VAT to the import value, but the real 2026 rule exempts the first NOK 300,000 — so for cheaper cars the Norwegian number shown is a ceiling, not a floor; our [Norway import guide](/blog/norway-import-guide/) covers those mechanics in detail. And the Swedish figures exclude nothing: what you see is the full stack. For a German reference point, the same Seal lands at $44,413 — $1,477 below Sweden, almost entirely because Germany's VAT is 19% rather than 25%.

## Why Sweden still matters: strong EV demand, unchanged customs bill

Sweden is not a marginal EV market with an import problem. It is one of the EU's most electrified car markets — 45% of new registrations were battery-electric in August 2026, 42% year-to-date, with the private share of EV registrations climbing from 32% to 39% and two in three of those financed through private leasing [Source: Mobility Sweden, September 2026]. That adoption happened without a purchase subsidy: Sweden abolished the *klimatbonus* in November 2022 and never replaced it [Source: European Commission / EAFO].

What the Swedish system still offers leans toward ownership and fleets rather than purchase:

- **Annual vehicle tax:** BEVs pay a base rate of SEK 360 per year and avoid the malus surcharge that higher-emitting cars carry for three years [Source: European Commission / EAFO].
- **No registration tax:** Sweden imposes no CO₂-based registration tax on cars; administrative fees are uniform [Source: European Commission / EAFO].
- **Company cars:** since 2025, BEVs used as company cars get a SEK 350,000 discount on the taxable benefit value (capped at 50% of the car's price), plus a further 40% reduction on the remaining amount (max SEK 14,000/year); tax-free workplace charging runs until June 2026 [Source: European Commission / EAFO].
- **Scrapping premium:** a SEK 10,000 payment for individuals replacing a 15-year-old car with a BEV ran through the end of 2025 and has now expired [Source: European Commission / EAFO].

For an importer the practical reading is this: the demand is real, the market is educated, and the tax code nudges electric — but none of it discounts your customs bill. If you are a VAT-registered business, though, the 25% VAT is recoverable, which pulls a large chunk of the modeled cost back off the table. For fleets, that changes the math materially; for private buyers, it does not.

## What importers should check before committing

1. **Which CVD tier does your brand sit in?** The difference between 17% and 35.3% on the same car is worth about $6,290 on a $25,000 import. Confirm the rate for the exact exporting producer, not just the brand on the badge.
2. **Are you buying as a business or a private individual?** VAT-registered businesses can deduct the 25% import VAT on commercial use; private buyers cannot. The same car has two different effective costs depending on who registers it.
3. **Budget the registration steps properly.** After arrival, the owner applies to the Swedish Transport Agency for verification of origin (SEK 1,240, paid in advance), then books a registration inspection or individual approval test with an inspection company [Source: Swedish Transport Agency].
4. **Check the homologation route.** A car with EU whole-vehicle type approval can be approved without extra technical documentation; a China-only homologation needs single-vehicle approval, which is slower and costlier — see our [WVTA vs single-vehicle approval](/blog/wvta-vs-single-vehicle-approval/) explainer.
5. **Left-hand drive and CCS2.** Sweden is a left-hand-drive market with CCS2 charging; a China-market car needs the right port and charging configuration — our [CCS2 vs GB/T note](/blog/ccs2-vs-gbt-charging/) covers the compatibility cost.
6. **Confirm current customs practice locally.** The exact fee schedule and processing details for private vehicle import at Swedish Customs (Tullverket) could not be verified from primary sources at the time of writing — treat those specifics as UNKNOWN and confirm with a customs broker before wiring money.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view, Sweden is the clearest evidence that China-EV tariff policy inside the EU is done at the bloc level, not the national one: two Nordic neighbours can run the same 25% VAT and the same EV-loving customer base, yet the same car costs $7,778 more in Sweden than in Norway purely because Norway is not in the customs union. The EU-vs-EEA structural gap is the real story, and no Swedish incentive can close it.

**Reasoning.** First, the numbers are not close and not temporary: the customs stack multiplies a BYD's ex-factory price by 1.287 and an MG's by 1.488 before VAT, and the CVD regulation runs for five years from late 2024 — this is the decade's baseline, not a quarter's anomaly. Second, Sweden's own policy levers are pointed elsewhere: the purchase bonus was scrapped in 2022, so the state supports electric driving through annual tax relief, company-car benefit discounts and fuel disincentives — none of which reduce what an importer pays at the border. Third, the market itself argues against treating the tariff as prohibitive: 42–45% EV share with no purchase subsidy means the demand side is genuine, and for VAT-registered fleets the recoverable VAT removes the single heaviest line in the stack. The private-buyer math is the one that stays harsh.
