## TL;DR

Zeekr pays an **18.8%** countervailing duty — but the rate is inherited, not earned. Zeekr is a Geely Group entity, and Geely was one of the three producers individually sampled in the EU's anti-subsidy investigation, so every Geely-group BEV, including Zeekr's, carries Geely's 18.8% rather than the 20.7% "other cooperating" default.

That inheritance is a small but real advantage: Zeekr sits **1.9 points below** the 20.7% that Chery, Changan, NIO, XPeng, and Leapmotor all pay. A Zeekr 001 at $38,000 ex-factory China lands in Germany at **$65,694 (+72.9%)**. The 18.8% also means Zeekr pays *less* than the premium rivals its cars compete against — but the gap is modest, and base price, not the 1.9-point tariff edge, is what separates Zeekr from the field.

## Key statistics

- **Zeekr countervailing duty:** 18.8% (on top of 10% standard duty = **28.8% combined** before VAT)
- **Why 18.8%:** Zeekr is a Geely Group entity, and Geely was individually sampled in the investigation
- **BYD:** 17.0% · **SAIC/MG:** 35.3% · **Tesla (Shanghai):** 7.8% · **Other cooperating:** 20.7%
- **In force since:** 31 October 2024, for 5 years (EU Implementing Regulation 2024/2754)
- **Zeekr BEV count:** 7 models (X, 007 GT, 007, 7X, 001, MIX, 009)
- **Worked example:** Zeekr 001, $38,000 China → **$65,694 landed in Germany (+72.9%)**

## Why Zeekr pays 18.8% — the inherited rate

Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754 assigned individual rates to three individually sampled producers: BYD (17.0%), Geely (18.8%), and SAIC (35.3%). Zeekr was not sampled as a standalone company — it is a Geely Group entity, so its BEVs inherit Geely's 18.8% by group attribution, the same way Lynk & Co and Geely Galaxy do.

This is the single most important thing to understand about Zeekr's tariff: **the 18.8% is a group label, not a Zeekr finding.** A premium Zeekr 009 and a budget Geely Galaxy LC share the exact same 18.8% line because they share the same exporting group.

The practical upside is that Zeekr enjoys a slight tariff edge over the "other cooperating" 20.7% names it competes with — about 1.9 points, or roughly $800 on a $40,000 car. That is real but modest, and it should not be over-weighted against base-price and specification differences.

## The full tariff stack, worked example: Zeekr 001 → Germany

Here is the itemized math for a Zeekr 001 (base $38,000 ex-factory China) imported into Germany, using the same [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/) as the rest of the catalog:

| Cost item | Amount | How it's calculated |
|---|---|---|
| Base price (CIF) | $38,000 | Zeekr 001, China MSRP |
| Standard import duty | $3,800 | 10% of CIF |
| Countervailing duty | $7,858 | 18.8% of (CIF + duty) |
| VAT | $9,435 | 19% of (CIF + duty + CVD) |
| RoRo freight | $2,000 | — |
| Customs clearance | $350 | — |
| Certification | $3,250 | Homologation |
| Registration | $500 | — |
| Inland transport | $500 | — |
| **Total landed** | **$65,694** | **+72.9% over base** |

The countervailing duty ($7,858) is more than double the standard duty ($3,800). The $6,600 flat stack adds a further 17 points of premium. If Zeekr had instead fallen under the 20.7% default, the CVD would be about $8,651 — a difference of roughly **$790** on this car. The inherited 18.8% saves Zeekr buyers a little under $800, not thousands.

## Zeekr model by model: the landed-cost spread

| Zeekr 007 GT | $28,180 | $50,422 | +79% |
| Zeekr 007 | $29,560 | $52,569 | +78% |
| Zeekr 7X | $32,370 | $56,938 | +76% |
| Zeekr 001 | $38,000 | $65,694 | +73% |
| Zeekr MIX | $39,420 | $67,902 | +72% |
| Zeekr 009 | $61,830 | $102,751 | +66% |

The spread runs from +86% on the $21,940 Zeekr X to +66% on the $61,830 Zeekr 009. The absolute-dollar story is what matters at the top: the 009 carries a countervailing duty of roughly **$12,800** — more than the entire landed cost of a Zeekr X. A percentage tariff scales with price, so Zeekr's most expensive cars generate the largest dollar duty bills even though their percentage premium looks smallest.

## Same car, different market: where the 18.8% disappears

The countervailing duty is an EU measure. The same Zeekr 001 lands very differently outside the bloc:

| Market | Region | Zeekr 001 landed cost | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | EU | $65,694 | +73% |
| United Kingdom | Non-EU Europe | $56,410 | +48% |
| United Arab Emirates | Middle East | $46,345 | +22% |

A **$19,300 difference on a $38,000 car** between Germany and the UAE — almost entirely the 18.8% countervailing duty plus EU VAT. The UK's post-Brexit 10% duty with no CVD cuts the premium to +48%; the UAE's 5% duty and 5% VAT collapse it to +22%. For Zeekr's premium positioning, the EU is the most expensive destination by a wide margin.

## What buyers should ask before importing a Zeekr

1. **"Is this a BEV or a PHEV?"** — the 18.8% applies to BEVs only. Zeekr's PHEV models (8X, 9X) currently pay only the 10% standard duty.
2. **"What's the absolute duty bill, not the rate?"** — 18.8% on a $61,830 Zeekr 009 is ~$12,800. Always ask for the dollar figure.
3. **"How much is the 18.8% actually saving me?"** — versus the 20.7% default, roughly $790 on a $38,000 car. The edge is real but small.
4. **"Am I registering in the EU?"** — if not, the 18.8% may not apply. Get the landed cost for your actual destination.
5. **"How does this compare to BYD?"** — Zeekr's 18.8% is 1.8 points above BYD's 17%. The gap is small; base price and specification matter more.

## The Author's Take

**Position:** In my view, Zeekr's 18.8% is best understood as a small, inherited discount — it saves premium buyers a few hundred dollars versus the 20.7% crowd, but it should not be the reason anyone picks a Zeekr over a competitor.

**Reasoning:** First, the rate is group attribution, not a Zeekr finding, so treating it as evidence that Zeekr is "more competitive" than Changan or NIO is a category error — it is just Geely's sampled rate traveling with the group. Second, the dollar impact of the 1.9-point edge is modest: about $790 on a Zeekr 001, which is noise compared to the $6,600 fixed stack and the car's own base price. Third, the real Zeekr story is the inverse relationship between price and premium percentage — the $61,830 Zeekr 009 absorbs the 18.8% into a +66% markup and generates the group's largest dollar duty bill, so the premium end is where the tariff actually does the most absolute work.

**Disclosure:** This is my interpretation of published EU tariff law and the EV Hub landed-cost records, not legal or tax advice. Countervailing-duty rates and covered exporters are set by EU regulation and can change; verify the current schedule with a customs broker before pricing anything.
