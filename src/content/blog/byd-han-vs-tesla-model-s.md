## TL;DR

The [BYD Han](/vehicles/byd-han/) lands in Germany at roughly **$42,897** — a +81% premium over its $23,700 ex-factory price, driven by a 10% standard duty, a 17% BYD countervailing duty, 19% VAT, and about $6,600 of fixed import costs. The Tesla Model S, by contrast, lists at **€109,990** in Germany because it is built in Fremont, California and imported from the US — so it pays the 10% standard duty and 19% VAT but **no countervailing duty** (that measure targets China-origin BEVs only).

The headline gap is not really a tariff story. It is a **base-price story**: the Han starts at $23,700, the Model S at $86,630 in the US. Even after a heavier tariff stack (+53% on the Han versus +31% on the Model S, taxes only), the Han lands for roughly a third of what the Model S retails for in Germany.

## Key statistics

- **BYD Han:** $23,700 ex-factory China → **$42,897 landed Germany (+81.0%)**
- **Tesla Model S (AWD):** $86,630 US list → **€109,990 German list** (Plaid: €119,990)
- **Han tariff stack (Germany):** 10% duty + 17% BYD CVD + 19% VAT = **+53.2%** on taxes alone
- **Model S tariff stack (Germany):** 10% duty + **0% CVD** + 19% VAT = **+30.9%** on taxes alone
- **The one structural difference:** the 17% BYD countervailing duty — the Model S is US-built, so it is exempt
- **Model S status:** production ending in 2026; new orders closed in Germany — this is a run-out vehicle, not a fresh model

## What "landed cost" means here — and what it doesn't

Before the numbers, a definition block, because the single biggest mistake in this comparison is comparing two different kinds of price.

- **Landed cost** (EV Hub's term) is the importer-side cost to bring a China-market car to a destination port, registered and compliant: base price → import duty → countervailing duty → VAT → freight → clearance → certification → registration → inland transport. Full formula in our [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).
- **The Han's $42,897 figure is a landed cost.** It is what an importer pays *before* adding their own retail margin. It also includes $3,250 of certification, because a China-market Han is not built to EU type-approval and needs homologation.
- **The Model S's €109,990 is a retail price.** Tesla sells it directly in Germany, already type-approved for the EU, so the number already bundles the import duty, VAT, delivery, and Tesla's margin. It is not the same kind of number as the Han's $42,897.

The honest way to compare the two is therefore in **two steps**: first the tariff stack (which is genuinely like-for-like), then the "what you actually pay" picture, with the asymmetry stated out loud.

On a like-for-like WLTP basis the two are closer than the headline numbers suggest, though the Model S's 100 kWh pack still gives it a genuine range advantage.

## The tariff stack, worked: Han vs Model S into Germany

Both cars enter Germany through the same border, but they do not face the same tariff stack. The Model S is US-built, and the EU's countervailing duty — [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754](/blog/byd-countervailing-duty-landed-cost/) — applies only to battery-electric vehicles **originating in China**. A US-built Model S therefore skips the entire countervailing line.

Here is the itemized math for both, Germany, taxes only:

| Line | BYD Han | Tesla Model S (AWD) |
|---|---|---|
| Base value | $23,700 | $86,630 (US list, as proxy) |
| Standard duty (10%) | $2,370 | $8,663 |
| Countervailing duty | $4,432 (17%, BYD) | **$0 (US origin — exempt)** |
| VAT (19%) | $5,795 | $18,106 |
| **Tax-inclusive subtotal** | **$36,297** | **$113,399** |
| Effective tax uplift | **+53.2%** | **+30.9%** |

The two numbers differ by almost exactly the 17% countervailing duty: 1.532 ÷ 1.309 = 1.17. That is the entire structural tariff difference between a Chinese-built flagship and a US-built flagship entering the EU.

Two caveats belong next to this table:

1. **The Model S "base value" is a proxy.** Tesla's actual customs valuation is not public. I have used the $86,630 US list price as a stand-in for the pre-EU-tax value; the real duty base is Tesla's transaction value, which will differ. The point of the row is the *shape* of the stack, not a to-the-dollar claim about Tesla's invoice.
2. **The Model S base is a US retail price** (includes Tesla's US margin), while the Han base is an ex-factory price. Even on the "base" row the two are not identical concepts — but the duty, CVD, and VAT lines are applied consistently to whichever base each car actually carries.

## The US-import nuance, stated plainly

This is the part most "Chinese EV vs Tesla" comparisons get wrong:

- **Tesla Model S and Model X are not built in Berlin or Shanghai.** They are built only in Fremont, California, and were exported to Europe from the US. Berlin builds the Model Y; Shanghai builds Model 3/Y for some markets. The Model S/X EU supply was a US import.
- Because it is a US import, the Model S pays the EU's **10% standard (MFN) duty** on cars, plus **19% German VAT** — but **no countervailing duty**. The CVD is a China-only measure.
- The **Tesla 7.8% rate** you may have seen in the EU duty tables applies to **Tesla's Shanghai-built Model 3/Y**, not to the US-built Model S. Different car, different origin, different duty. Conflating the two is a common error.

That does not make the Model S cheap — it just means the Model S's high German price is driven by its US list price and Tesla's own margin, not by an anti-subsidy penalty.

## What you actually pay: the numbers are not comparable

Put the two figures next to each other and the gap is stark:

- **BYD Han:** $42,897 landed in Germany — about **€40,000** at current exchange rates (FX-dependent). This is *before* an importer's retail margin.
- **Tesla Model S:** **€109,990** German list price, already retail.

Even if you add a healthy importer and dealer margin on top of the Han's landed cost, the Han would still retail for well under half the Model S. The Model S is the faster, longer-range, more powerful car by a wide margin — but it is also more than two-and-a-half times the price, and the price gap comes almost entirely from the base price, not from tariffs.

A fleet buyer reading this should notice something else: the Han's fixed import costs — freight $2,000, certification $3,250, clearance $350, registration $500, inland $500 — are the same whether you import a $23,700 Han or a $8,940 Seagull. On the Han they are a modest +27.8% of base; on a microcar they would be catastrophic. This is the [fixed-cost paradox](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/) that runs through every Chinese EV import.

## What buyers should ask before choosing

1. **"Am I comparing landed cost to retail price?"** — the Han's number is importer-side; the Model S's is retail. Add margin to the Han or strip margin from the Model S, but do not compare them raw.
2. **"Which origin am I actually buying?"** — a Shanghai-built Tesla (Model 3/Y) pays 7.8% CVD; a Fremont-built Model S pays none. The duty follows the origin, not the badge.
3. **"Is the Model S even available?"** — production ends in 2026 and new orders are closed in Germany. You are comparing a car you can order against a run-out model.
4. **"Are the range numbers on the same cycle?"** — 605 km CLTC is not 744 km WLTP. Normalize before you decide.
5. **"What is my actual tariff bill in absolute dollars?"** — the Han's 17% CVD is ~$4,432 on a single car; the Model S's "no CVD" is real but irrelevant if you cannot afford the $86,630 base to begin with.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the Model S pay the EU countervailing duty?**
No. The countervailing duty under Regulation (EU) 2024/2754 applies to China-origin BEVs. The Model S is built in Fremont, California, and imported from the US, so it pays the 10% standard duty and VAT but no CVD.

**Why is the Model S so much more expensive than the Han?**
Base price, not tariffs. The Model S starts at $86,630 in the US versus $23,700 for the Han. The Han actually faces a heavier tariff stack (+53.2% vs +30.9% on taxes), but its low base price means it still lands far cheaper in absolute terms.

**Is the Han's 605 km range comparable to the Model S's 744 km?**
Not directly. The Han figure is CLTC (optimistic), the Model S figure is WLTP. On a comparable cycle the gap narrows, though the Model S's larger 100 kWh pack still gives it more real-world range.

**Is the Model S still being made?**
No. Tesla announced in January 2026 that Model S/X production would end, converting the Fremont lines to Optimus robots. Production wound down in 2026, and new orders are closed in Germany — the €109,990 figure is for a run-out vehicle.

**What is the single structural tariff difference between the two cars?**
The 17% BYD countervailing duty. Both cars pay 10% duty and 19% VAT; only the Han additionally pays the CVD, because it is Chinese-built.

## The Author's Take

**Position:** If you are importing for a fleet or a price-sensitive buyer, the BYD Han is the rational pick by a wide margin — not because it is a better car, but because a $23,700 base price survives a heavy tariff stack and still lands at a third of the Model S's German retail. If you want performance and range and money is not the constraint, the Model S is the better machine, but it is also a discontinued one.

**Reasoning:** The Model S's German price is almost entirely base price plus duty plus VAT; the car itself carries no China-origin penalty, so the "tariff" narrative that often gets attached to this matchup is mostly wrong. The Han, by contrast, is genuinely punished by its 17% countervailing duty — yet the punishment lands on a $23,700 car, so it cannot close a 3.6× base-price gap. For an importer, the Han is the lower-risk, higher-margin play; the Model S is a performance purchase, not a landed-cost win.
