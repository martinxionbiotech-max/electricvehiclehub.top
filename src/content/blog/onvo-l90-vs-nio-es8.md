## TL;DR

The [Onvo L90](/vehicles/onvo-l90/) and the [NIO ES8](/vehicles/nio-es8/) leave China through the same corporate door — both are NIO-group brands based in Hefei, Anhui — and both carry the EU's identical **20.7% countervailing duty**, the "other cooperating companies" rate, on top of the standard 10% import tariff. At the factory gate the value brand undercuts the premium badge by **$19,690** ($37,440 against $57,130). The tariff wall does not close that gap; it scales it. In Germany the L90 lands at **$65,753.82** and the ES8 at **$96,863.29** — a gap of **$31,109.47**, or 1.58× the China difference. The landed spread widens beyond the $19,690 China gap in **all 30 markets in the dataset** (from $20,674.50 in Qatar to $34,260.60 in Mexico); the seven markets modelled below run $21,659 to $31,371. The value brand keeps its dollar advantage everywhere. What it does not keep is proportionality: the identical fixed import block consumes **17.6% of the L90's base price against 11.6% of the ES8's**, so the cheaper car's landed markup is the heavier one (+75.6% vs +69.5% in Germany). One group, two price classes, one tariff line — and an asymmetry in who absorbs more of it, relatively.

## Key statistics

- **Factory-gate gap:** L90 $37,440 vs ES8 $57,130 — **$19,690**, making the Onvo 34.5% cheaper at the gate (the NIO 52.6% more expensive)
- **Identical tariff line:** both NIO-group brands pay the same **20.7%** countervailing duty plus 10% standard duty — **30.7% combined before VAT**
- **Germany landed:** L90 **$65,753.82 (+75.6%)** · ES8 **$96,863.29 (+69.5%)** · gap **$31,109.47**
- **The scaling identity:** $19,690 × (1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19) = $31,109.47 — identical rates and identical fixed costs mean the landed gap is just the base gap times the tax multiplier
- **Germany tax lines:** CVD $8,525.09 vs $13,008.50 (2.3× the standard-duty line in both cases); VAT $9,444.73 vs $14,411.79; total tax bill 58.0% of base for both
- **Fixed costs:** $6,600 per car in Germany — **17.6% of the L90's base**, 11.6% of the ES8's
- **Full-map spread:** the landed gap floors at **$20,674.50 (Qatar)** and peaks at **$34,260.60 (Mexico)** across all 30 markets
- **Relative compression:** the Onvo's discount against the ES8 narrows from **34.5% at the gate** to **31.3–32.2% landed** across the seven markets below
- **Spec gulf:** 85 vs 102 kWh · 605 vs 635 km CLTC · 340 vs 520 kW · 5.9 s vs 3.97 s to 100 km/h
- **Class beats tariffs:** the ES8 crossing zero-duty Norway lands at $78,662.50 — still $12,908.68 above the L90 crossing the full German wall

## One group, two price classes

Onvo is NIO's value brand — same parent (NIO, Hefei, Anhui), launched in 2024, three models in this catalogue (L60, L80, L90). NIO itself is the group's premium badge. That makes this pair the cleanest controlled comparison in the dataset: same corporate DNA, same compliance path, same tariff treatment, deliberately different price classes. The L90 is a full-size electric SUV at $37,440 ex-factory; the ES8 is the premium full-size SUV at $57,130. Both are aimed at the same buyer demographic — large families, executive fleets — which is exactly why the price ladder inside one group is worth measuring at a border, not just in a showroom.

At the gate, the $19,690 gap is the whole story: the ES8 costs 52.6% more than the L90 before either car pays a cent of tax. The question this article answers is what the customs wall does to that deliberate split when both cars are charged identically — and whether the cheaper product keeps the headroom it was designed to have.

Both columns are quoted manufacturer figures from the vehicle records, and both range figures use the same cycle — CLTC on each side — so unlike a CLTC-versus-WLTP cross-check, these two can be read against each other as quoted numbers. The L90's counter-position is efficiency and mass — 14.5 vs 16.6 kWh/100 km, 380 kg lighter — not output. One honest blank in the table: the ES8's record lists no fast-charge window, so it stays UNKNOWN rather than estimated; fleet planners should treat that as a due-diligence item, not a footnote.

## One wall, two bills

Both cars pay the same stack into the EU: 10% standard duty on the customs value, then 20.7% countervailing duty over the duty-paid value, then VAT over everything, then the flat logistics block. The 20.7% is the regulation's "other cooperating companies" line — not an Onvo rate, not an NIO rate, a category assigned to producers that cooperated with the EU investigation but were not individually sampled. Sub-brands inherit their group's treatment, which is why Onvo appears on the same 20.7% row as NIO: the duty attaches to exporting entities, not badges — the full 72-brand map is in the [CVD master table](/blog/china-ev-brand-cvd-master-table/), and the rate tiers are explained in the [NIO/XPeng/Leapmotor write-up](/blog/nio-xpeng-leapmotor-countervailing-duty/).

The compounding formula is the same one used across this catalogue:

`landed = base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs`

For Germany the factor is `1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 = 1.579963`; the order matters, because each charge compounds over the last. The full method is documented in the [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).

The consequence of identical rates is algebraic: subtract one car from the other in the same market and the rates stop distinguishing them — what remains is the base-price difference times the multiplier, plus the difference in fixed costs. The fixed costs are identical between these two cars in each market ($6,600 each in Germany: $2,000 RoRo freight, $350 customs clearance, $3,250 certification, $500 registration, $500 inland transport), so they cancel exactly. The landed gap is the base gap, scaled: **$19,690 × 1.579963 = $31,109.47**.

## Worked example: both cars through German customs

| Line | Onvo L90 | NIO ES8 |
|---|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $37,440.00 | $57,130.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $3,744.00 | $5,713.00 |
| Countervailing duty (20.7%) | $8,525.09 | $13,008.50 |
| VAT (19%) | $9,444.73 | $14,411.79 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$65,753.82** | **$96,863.29** |
| **Landed gap** | — | **$31,109.47** |

Formula, both columns: `37,440 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 65,753.82` and `57,130 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 96,863.29`.

Three lines deserve attention. First, the countervailing duty is 2.3× the standard-duty line for both cars — the same rate produces very different bills ($8,525.09 against $13,008.50) because it compounds over each base in turn. Second, the three tax lines equal 58.0% of the base price in both columns: the tax stack itself is scrupulously proportional; it is the fixed block that is not. Third, the fixed block is 17.6% of the L90's base price and 11.6% of the ES8's — the [fixed-versus-sticker mechanic](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/) that separates a cost table from a shopping list.

| Germany | $65,753.82 (+75.6%) | $96,863.29 (+69.5%) | **+$31,109.47** |
| United Kingdom | $55,670.80 (+48.7%) | $81,661.60 (+42.9%) | **+$25,990.80** |
| Norway | $54,050.00 (+44.4%) | $78,662.50 (+37.7%) | **+$24,612.50** |
| Switzerland | $48,141.55 (+28.6%) | $70,277.83 (+23.0%) | **+$22,136.28** |
| United Arab Emirates | $45,727.60 (+22.1%) | $67,435.83 (+18.0%) | **+$21,708.23** |
| Australia | $47,034.00 (+25.6%) | $68,693.00 (+20.2%) | **+$21,659.00** |

Every row is the same identity: the gap equals $19,690 times the market's tax factor — 1.58–1.59 in the two EU rows (standard duty + 20.7% CVD + VAT), 1.32 for the UK (no CVD), 1.25 for Norway (no duty, no CVD), 1.12 for Switzerland, 1.10 for the UAE and Australia. Among these seven, the gap is widest in France ($31,370.89) and narrowest in Australia ($21,659.00); across all 30 markets in the dataset it never compresses below $20,674.50 (Qatar) and reaches $34,260.60 (Mexico).

Two readings of the same table. In absolute dollars, the value brand's advantage grows with the wall — the two EU rows carry the widest gaps in the set. In percentage terms, the value brand's own markup is the higher one in every row: +75.6% vs +69.5% in Germany, +44.4% vs +37.7% in Norway, +22.1% vs +18.0% in the UAE. Both readings are true at once, because the fixed block is a bigger slice of the cheaper car.

## Does the value brand hold its advantage?

This is the strategic question, and the data answers it two ways — both at once.

**Yes, in dollars.** The $19,690 gate gap survives every tariff configuration in the dataset, and widens in all of them: $31,109.47 in Germany, $21,659.00 in Australia, $20,674.50 at the floor in Qatar. The wall amplifies the class gap proportionally instead of eroding it, because both cars are charged identical rates and the identical fixed block cancels in the subtraction. An importer cross-shopping within the group is never forced together by tariffs: the Onvo is the cheaper landed car in every market, usually by more than the Chinese price difference.

**No, in proportions.** The wall taxes the cheaper car harder. The L90's markup over its own base exceeds the ES8's in every market checked (+75.6% vs +69.5% in Germany; +76.7% vs +70.7% in France; +25.6% vs +20.2% in Australia), and the Onvo's relative discount against the ES8 narrows from 34.5% at the gate to 31.3–32.2% landed. The cause is structural, not policy: freight, clearance and certification cost what they cost, and the $6,600 block that is 11.6% of a $57,130 base is 17.6% of a $37,440 one. This is the same mechanic the [L60 comparison](/blog/onvo-l60-vs-tesla-model-y/) found at the lower rung — value-brand economics degrade faster at borders — and the L90 numbers make it visible inside a single family.

**And the class gap dwarfs the tariff stack.** Put the two cars in different markets and the point sharpens: the ES8 crossing zero-duty Norway lands at $78,662.50 — $12,908.68 above the L90 crossing the full 30.7% German wall ($65,753.82). No tariff schedule in the dataset closes a gap that wide; the badge, battery and output decide it. That is the same conclusion the [ES6 comparison](/blog/nio-es6-vs-tesla-model-y/) reached from the opposite direction and the [ET5 analysis](/blog/nio-et5-vs-tesla-model-3/) measured in the premium lane: within the NIO group, tariffs change both cars' prices without changing their order.

## Where this comparison can go wrong

1. **Cost versus sticker.** Both columns are landed import costs — no importer margin, no warranty provisioning, no dealer spread. They measure headroom against a competing shelf price, not retail prices.
2. **The China reference is a catalogue figure.** $37,440 and $57,130 are the dataset's ex-factory USD estimates from the vehicle records, not negotiated quotes; actual transfer prices move with trims and volume.
3. **Fixed costs are per-unit estimates.** Small orders land above these numbers; at volume, per-unit certification and freight fall, which slightly re-inflates the value brand's relative advantage.
4. **VAT is recoverable for registered importers.** It changes border cash, not the underlying duty cost. Duty and countervailing duty never come back.
5. **CLTC is quoted, not measured.** Both cars use the same cycle, so the figures are comparable to each other — neither is a real-world promise. The ES8's fast-charge figure is UNKNOWN in the record; do not infer one.
6. **Rates move.** The countervailing duty is set by EU regulation and can change; re-check rates and prices before structuring a deal.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, this is the cleanest controlled test in the dataset of whether a value brand's advantage survives a tariff wall — because both cars hit the same wall line-for-line, at the same rate, with the same fixed block, from the same parent. The answer splits cleanly. In dollars the advantage not only survives, it compounds: $19,690 becomes $21,659–$31,371 across the seven markets modelled here, and widens in all 30. In proportions it erodes: the cheaper car carries the heavier percentage markup (+75.6% vs +69.5% in Germany), and its relative discount against the ES8 compresses from 34.5% to roughly 31–32% at the port. "The wall kills the value brand" is wrong about the dollars and right about the ratios. "The Onvo keeps a $31k advantage" is right about the dollars and silent on the 17.6% of its factory price that goes to logistics before a single tax line.

### Reasoning

First, the algebra is checkable to the cent: identical rates plus identical fixed costs mean the landed gap equals the base gap times the market's tax factor — $19,690 × 1.579963 = $31,109.47 in Germany — and the same identity reproduces every other row. A tariff that preserves proportions cannot decide between two cars of one group; it can only scale the difference the badges already created. Second, the asymmetry that does not cancel is the fixed block: 17.6% versus 11.6% of base, which is why the value brand pays the higher percentage premium in every market and why its relative discount compresses — a structural headwind for value-brand imports that shows at every rung of the group's ladder. Third, the cross-market fact I would put in front of any planner: the premium car crossing the lightest duty lane (Norway — zero duty, zero CVD, $78,662.50) still lands $12,908.68 above the value car crossing the heaviest ($65,753.82 in Germany). The class decision dominates the tariff decision, and no rate schedule available here reverses it. Fourth, a caveat against my own headline: this is a spec gulf, not a twin test — the ES8 buys 17 kWh, 180 kW and 1.93 seconds the L90 does not have, and the quoted specs favor it. The honest frame is two products at two price points that happen to share a customs category, not the same car at two prices.
