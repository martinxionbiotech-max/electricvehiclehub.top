## TL;DR

Norway still charges **0% import duty and no countervailing duty** on a Chinese battery-electric vehicle, so on the customs side it is one of the cheapest markets on earth. But the country is no longer a VAT-free EV market: since **1 January 2026** the 25% VAT exemption applies only to the first **NOK 300,000** of an electric car's price (down from NOK 500,000), and the government has signalled full VAT from 2027. On our catalog's deliberately conservative model — which applies the headline 25% across the whole import value — a $24,690 BYD Seal lands at about **$38,112 (+54.4%)**. Treat that as an upper bound: a China-origin EV priced below the NOK 300,000 threshold currently pays no VAT at all.

## Key statistics

- **Import duty (BEV):** 0% — Norway is outside the EU customs union and levies no duty on electric passenger cars
- **Countervailing duty:** 0% — Norway applies no anti-subsidy tariff on Chinese EVs (the EU's brand-specific CVD does not exist here)
- **VAT:** 25% standard rate, but the EV exemption is now capped — first **NOK 300,000** exempt from 1 Jan 2026, **down from NOK 500,000**, with full VAT announced from 1 Jan 2027
- **One-off registration tax:** BEVs are exempt from the CO₂ and NOₓ components; a **weight component of NOK 12.71 per kg above 500 kg** still applies
- **Worked example (catalog model):** BYD Seal $24,690 → **$38,112 modelled in Norway (+54.4%)**, versus $33,807 Switzerland (+36.9%) and $44,413 Germany (+79.9%)
- **The real threshold:** not the tariff — there isn't one. It is the **NOK 300,000 VAT line**, and the announced 2027 phase-out behind it

## Defining the terms, because "Norway is tax-free for EVs" is now out of date

The shorthand "Norway has no tax on EVs" survived for years because two separate mechanisms both produced zero. The first is the **import duty**, which Norway — not an EU member — simply does not levy on electric passenger cars. The second is the **one-off registration tax**, a Norwegian domestic purchase tax that is structured around CO₂ and NOₓ emissions; a battery-electric car emits neither, so those components collapse to zero.

Both remain true. What changed is VAT. A BEV still avoids the emissions-based registration tax, and it still enters duty-free, but the blanket 25% VAT exemption has been narrowed to a price threshold. That is the single number an importer must model correctly in 2026.

## The 2026 threshold is the whole story

NOK 300,000 is roughly **USD 28,000** at recent exchange rates. That is the pivot point for the entire market. Below it, a Chinese EV competes in Norway essentially free of consumption tax, which is why the country's EV share has stayed near the top of the world. Above it, the car carries 25% VAT on the portion over the line, and every kroner of the excess is taxed twice in effect — once in the price, once in VAT.

Two details matter for planning:

1. **Order date does not save you.** The threshold applies to vehicles *delivered* in 2026, not ordered, so a car ordered in 2025 but delivered after 1 January 2026 falls under the new rule.
2. **The weight component is real money.** A 1,950 kg BYD Seal pays the weight element on 1,450 kg over the 500 kg floor: **1,450 × NOK 12.71 = NOK 18,430**. Our catalog's Norway model folds registration costs into a flat fee and does not itemise this, so budget for it separately.

The announced 2027 phase-out is the strategic risk. The threshold has already dropped from NOK 500,000 to NOK 300,000, with a further cut to **NOK 150,000 signalled before full removal**. If that path holds, by 2027 even a mid-priced compact EV will be taxed on most of its value, and Norway's VAT advantage over, say, Switzerland or Australia will largely disappear.

## Why the percentage premium on cheap cars is misleading

The +170% headline on the Wuling MINI EV looks alarming. It is arithmetic, not taxation. A $5,000 car still pays the same freight, customs clearance, certification and registration costs as a $25,000 car — the fixed block in our model runs to roughly $5,850–$7,250 depending on region, before any tax is applied. On a microcar, fixed costs dominate; on a Zeekr 009 they almost vanish into the price.

So when someone tells you Norway is a bad market because microcars "cost 2.7× their China price", that statement is true and irrelevant. What matters to an importer is the *absolute* landed figure and the *incremental* tax, not the percentage on a cheap base.

## What buyers should ask before importing to Norway

1. **"What is this model's Norwegian list price, and does it clear NOK 300,000?"** — the answer decides whether you pay any VAT at all.
2. **"Is delivery before or after a threshold change?"** — the 2026 rule and the announced 2027 phase-out apply on delivery, not order.
3. **"What is the kerb weight?"** — the NOK 12.71/kg component above 500 kg is a genuine line item.
4. **"Is the vehicle type-approved for Norway?"** — EU/EEA type approval generally carries over; a China-only homologation does not.
5. **"Am I comparing Norway against Switzerland or Australia?"** — on our catalog, Switzerland is consistently cheaper for the same car.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Norway charge import duty on Chinese EVs?**
No. Norway levies 0% import duty on electric passenger cars and applies no countervailing duty on China-origin vehicles. The only customs-stage charge is VAT, and that is now threshold-limited.

**Is Norway still a VAT-free EV market?**
Partly. Since 1 January 2026 the 25% VAT exemption covers only the first NOK 300,000 of the price. A car below that line pays no VAT; a car above it pays 25% on the excess. Full VAT is announced from 1 January 2027.

**Why does EV Hub's Norway figure look higher than the headline rules suggest?**
Because our model applies the headline 25% VAT to the full import value as a uniform, conservative assumption across 30 markets. It deliberately does not encode country-specific threshold relief. For sub-threshold Chinese EVs in Norway, the true landed cost is below the modelled number.

**Is Norway cheaper than Switzerland for a Chinese EV?**
Not on our catalog. Switzerland's 4% automobile tax plus 8.1% VAT produces a lower landed figure than Norway's threshold-limited 25% VAT for every model we track, including the BYD Seal ($33,807 versus $38,112).

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view, Norway is still the best *structural* market for a Chinese EV in Europe — zero duty, zero countervailing duty, zero emissions tax — but the 2026 VAT threshold has turned the country into a two-tier market, and the 2027 phase-out makes it a closing window rather than a permanent advantage.

**Reasoning.** First, the tax stack is genuinely the cleanest in Europe on the customs side; there is no anti-subsidy tariff to litigate and no 10% EU duty to absorb, which is worth more than the VAT threshold costs on a mid-priced car. Second, the threshold relief is real while it lasts: a China-origin EV priced under NOK 300,000 is effectively consumption-tax-free today, and that is a materially better position than anything the EU offers. Third, the announced trajectory — NOK 500,000, then 300,000, then 150,000, then full VAT — tells you the direction of travel is one-way, so the question for an importer is not "is Norway attractive" but "for how many more model years".
