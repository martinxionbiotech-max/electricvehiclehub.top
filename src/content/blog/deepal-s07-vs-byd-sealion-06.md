## TL;DR

Two mid-size SUVs leave China almost at the same money: $21,110 for the **Deepal S07**, $20,250 for the **BYD Sealion 06** — **$860 apart**. What separates them at the border is which group built them: the S07 carries Changan Group's **20.7%** EU countervailing duty (the "other cooperating companies" rate), the Sealion 06 carries BYD's sampled **17.0%**. Three-point-seven percentage points is a rounding error in a tariff table and a four-figure spread on a customs entry, because the gap compounds — duty first, then the CVD on the duty-paid value, then VAT on everything.

In Germany the S07 lands at **$39,953.02** and the Sealion 06 at **$37,613.48** — a **$2,339.54** gap on cars $860 apart. In the UAE the same pair lands $948.15 apart; in Denmark, where 25% VAT compounds hardest, **$2,457.49**. The Sealion 06 lands lower in every one of the 30 markets in the catalog — the operational question is by how much, and the number roughly doubles the moment the two cars are looked at on the EU side of the customs border.

## Key statistics

- **China ex-factory price:** S07 $21,110 · Sealion 06 $20,250 — an $860 gap, 4.1% of the S07's base
- **EU countervailing duty:** S07 20.7% (Changan Group, "other cooperating companies") · Sealion 06 17.0% (BYD, sampled producer) — a **3.7-point spread**
- **Combined tariff before VAT:** 30.7% versus 27.0%
- **Germany, computed from the records:** S07 $39,953.02 (+89.3%) · Sealion 06 $37,613.48 (+85.7%) · gap **$2,339.54** = $860 sticker + $86 duty + $1,020 CVD + $373.54 VAT
- **Seven-market gap (S07 above Sealion 06):** Denmark +$2,457 · France +$2,359 · Germany +$2,340 · UK +$1,135 · Norway +$1,075 · UAE +$948 · Thailand +$920
- **Full catalog (30 markets):** gap runs from $903.00 (Qatar) to $2,457.49 (Denmark and Sweden); the Sealion 06 is cheaper landed in all 30
- **Compounding multiplier on the $860 China gap:** ×1.07 Thailand · ×1.10 UAE · ×1.25 Norway · ×1.32 UK · ×2.72 Germany · ×2.86 Denmark
- **The premium flip:** outside the EU the Sealion 06 carries the bigger percentage premium over its base (UAE +32.2% versus +31.3%); inside the EU the 3.7-point spread flips it (Germany +85.7% versus +89.3%)

## The basis: ex-factory versus landed cost

Same discipline as every EV Hub landed-cost record. **Ex-factory** is the China gate price — $21,110 for the [Deepal S07](/vehicles/deepal-s07/), $20,250 for the [BYD Sealion 06](/vehicles/byd-sealion-06/). **Landed cost** is the cash an importer needs at the border: standard import duty, countervailing duty where the EU applies it, and VAT/GST, charged in that order on an increasing base, plus a fixed block for RoRo freight, customs clearance, certification, registration and inland transport.

The arithmetic compounds:

```
landed = base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs
```

Only two inputs differ between these two cars: the base price ($860 apart) and the CVD rate (3.7 points apart). The duty rate, the VAT rate and the fixed costs belong to the destination — $6,600 in Germany ($2,000 freight, $350 clearance, $3,250 certification, $500 registration, $500 inland). Which means this comparison reduces to one question: what does a 3.7-point difference in a single rate do once everything else stacks on top of it? The [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/) documents the compounding order; [why the sticker price is never the landed price](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/) is the longer argument — and the same discipline governs the [Sealion 7 vs Tesla Model Y comparison](/blog/byd-sealion-7-vs-tesla-model-y/) and the [Atto 3 vs Model Y pairing](/blog/byd-atto-3-vs-tesla-model-y/).

## Same class, two engineering answers

The S07 comes from Deepal, Changan Group's EV brand; the Sealion 06 is BYD's mid-size SUV, sold as the Sealion 6 / Sealion 06 depending on the market. Park them side by side and the segment question mostly disappears: 60 mm of length separates them, and both quote the same 7.5-second 0–100 km/h. The records diverge in how each car gets there:

| Specification | Deepal S07 | BYD Sealion 06 |
|---|---|---|
| China ex-factory price | $21,110 | $20,250 |
| Length × width × height | 4,750 × 1,930 × 1,625 mm | 4,810 × 1,920 × 1,675 mm |
| Wheelbase | 2,900 mm | 2,820 mm |
| Curb weight | 1,970 kg | 1,920 kg |
| Battery (BEV) | 79.97 kWh | 65.28 kWh |
| Range, as quoted | 620 km (CLTC) | 520 km (CLTC) |
| Motor | 190 kW / 290 Nm | 170 kW / 330 Nm |
| 0–100 km/h | 7.5 s | 7.5 s |
| Top speed | 180 km/h | 185 km/h |
| Efficiency, as quoted | 17.4 kWh/100 km | 13.7 kWh/100 km |
| Fast charge, as quoted | 15 min (30–80%) | 20 min (30–80%) |
| EU countervailing duty | 20.7% | 17.0% |
| Germany landed cost | $39,953.02 | $37,613.48 |

Two cautions before quoting the table. First, 620 km and 520 km are CLTC figures — the Chinese cycle flatters, and neither number should be read as range to expect; [the cycle explainer](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/) covers the mechanics. Second, both nameplates have plug-in alternatives outside this comparison: the S07 line includes EREV variants and the Sealion 06 is also sold as a DM-i PHEV — powertrains the EU countervailing measure does not touch at all, because it covers BEVs only. This article keeps to the BEVs so the tariff stack is the only variable.

The engineering split is legible: the S07 carries a battery 14.7 kWh larger and quotes faster charging; the Sealion 06 weighs 50 kg less, quotes 3.7 kWh/100 km better efficiency and higher torque. Same claimed acceleration, different routes to it. On the spec sheet, neither car is $2,340 worse than the other — which is exactly why the rest of this article is about tariffs.

## The tariff stacks: 20.7% versus 17.0%

Under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, the EU's definitive countervailing measure on China-built battery-electric vehicles, the rate follows the producer, not the car. BYD was one of three producers sampled and received an individual rate: 17.0%. Changan was not sampled; as a cooperating exporter it falls under the weighted-average 20.7% assigned to "other cooperating companies" — the same line shared by Chery, NIO, XPeng, Xiaomi and everyone else outside the sampled three ([the Changan rate, unpacked](/blog/changan-countervailing-duty/); [every brand's rate in one table](/blog/china-ev-brand-cvd-master-table/)).

Two features of the measure shape everything below:

- **It is EU-only and BEV-only.** No market outside the EU — the UK, Norway, Switzerland, the Gulf, Australia, Thailand — levies a countervailing duty on these cars. There, both pay only destination-level charges and sit $860-plus-tax apart. PHEV and EREV variants skip the CVD even inside the EU.
- **Inside the EU, the rate spread compounds rather than adds.** Standard duty is 10% of the customs value; the CVD is charged on the duty-paid value; VAT is charged on the sum. Changan's extra 3.7 points therefore applies to a taller number than the $21,110 sticker, and VAT taxes the result.

So the full tariff question is not "which rate is higher" — it is "how much does the higher rate cost after everything downstream of it".

## Worked example: both cars through German customs

Germany is the reference market: 10% standard duty, the CVD, 19% VAT, and the same $6,600 fixed block for both cars. Computed line by line from the two vehicle records:

| Cost item (Germany) | Deepal S07 | BYD Sealion 06 |
|---|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $21,110.00 | $20,250.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $2,111.00 | $2,025.00 |
| Countervailing duty (20.7% / 17.0%) | $4,806.75 | $3,786.75 |
| VAT (19%) | $5,325.27 | $4,951.73 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$39,953.02** | **$37,613.48** |
| Premium over each car's base | +89.3% | +85.7% |

Formulas, so the arithmetic can be checked rather than trusted: `21,110 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 39,953.02` and `20,250 × 1.10 × 1.17 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 37,613.48`. The difference: **$2,339.54** on cars that leave China $860 apart.

## The compounding, line by line

Subtract the two stacks and the gap decomposes cleanly — and the headline is not the sticker gap:

| Gap component (S07 minus Sealion 06) | Germany | UAE |
|---|---|---|
| China ex-factory price | $860.00 | $860.00 |
| Standard duty | $86.00 (10%) | $43.00 (5%) |
| Countervailing duty | $1,020.00 | $0.00 |
| VAT / GST | $373.54 (19%) | $45.15 (5%) |
| **Landed-cost gap** | **$2,339.54** | **$948.15** |

In Germany, $1,479.54 of the $2,339.54 gap — 63% — is created by duty, the CVD difference, and the VAT that compounds over both. In the UAE, the tax machinery adds $88.15, or 9.3%. The same $860 sticker difference lands roughly 2.5× wider in Europe than in the Gulf, because both of Germany's percentage charges are levied on a base that already contains the previous ones.

Two footnotes on that $1,020 CVD line, because it repays a closer look. It is the difference between the two CVD charges ($4,806.75 versus $3,786.75), which mixes the 3.7-point rate gap with the different values each charge applies to; isolated to the S07's own duty-paid value, the rate gap alone is **$859.18** ($23,221 × 3.7 points). And a counterfactual, clearly labelled as arithmetic rather than a shopping option: apply BYD's 17% to the Deepal and it lands at $38,930.60, shrinking the German gap from $2,339.54 to $1,317.12. The rate differential — including the VAT compounded over it — is worth **$1,022.42 per car** in Germany.

Reading notes. EU markets cluster at $2,340–$2,457 of gap; non-EU markets at $920–$1,135. Denmark and Sweden tie at $2,457.49 — the catalog's widest gap — and the tie is itself evidence about what does and does not drive the number: Denmark's registration fee is $3,500 against Sweden's $70, yet because both cars carry the identical fee in each market, the $3,430 difference cancels out of the gap. What does not cancel is the CVD spread, and that is why the EU rows sit roughly twice as far apart as the rest. The catalog's smallest gap is $903.00 in Qatar; among the seven markets above, it is Thailand at $920.20.

## Where the outcome flips — and where it doesn't

Does any market flip the ranking? In dollars, no — and that is worth stating plainly. The Sealion 06 lands below the S07 in every market modelled, all 30 in the two records, because it starts $860 cheaper and, inside the EU, pays the lower producer rate. Nothing in the tax stack reverses either fact.

What flips is **which car carries the heavier percentage premium** — and the flip sits exactly on the EU's customs border. Outside it, the Sealion 06's smaller base makes its fixed-cost block proportionally heavier: UAE +32.2% versus +31.3%, Thailand +30.0% versus +29.0%, UK +62.9% versus +61.6%. Inside it, the 3.7-point CVD spread outweighs that small-base effect and hands the higher markup to the S07: Germany +89.3% versus +85.7%, France +90.1% versus +86.5%, Denmark +111.4% versus +108.3%.

The magnitude flips with the ranking. Cross from the UK into Germany — 10% standard duty on both sides, one customs border between them — and the gap goes from $1,135 to $2,340, roughly doubling on nothing but the countervailing duty and the VAT that compounds over it. The cleanest single-market comparison in this article is that pair: the UK is what the stack looks like without the CVD, Germany with it. And Denmark is where the compounding peaks: 25% VAT charged on the widest duty-paid base produces a $2,457.49 gap — 2.86 times the $860 that separates the two cars in China.

## Where this comparison can go wrong

1. **VAT is not always a cost.** A VAT-registered B2B importer usually recovers VAT, which cuts the border cash requirement by the VAT layer — $373.54 of the German gap, $45.15 of the UAE gap — leaving $1,966.00 and $903.00 respectively. Duty and countervailing duty never come back.
2. **Fixed costs are central estimates.** Freight, clearance and certification are volume-sensitive: small orders land above these numbers, per-unit costs fall at scale. They are identical for both cars within a market, so they cancel inside the gap — but not inside the premium percentages.
3. **Range figures are cycles, not expectations.** 620 km and 520 km CLTC cannot be compared with WLTP/EPA numbers or treated as real-world range. Quoted specifications carry sources; nothing here is a measured result.
4. **The measure is scoped, not universal.** BEV only, EU only. The moment the discussion moves to a Sealion 06 DM-i or an S07 EREV, or to a non-EU destination, the 20.7-versus-17.0 comparison stops applying — model the actual car and the actual destination.
5. **Rates are assigned, not chosen — and can change.** The rates come from EU regulation; model at current rates and stress the numbers a point or two in either direction before committing to a price.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, these two cars are one purchasing decision with one regulatory tiebreaker. At effectively the same China money, the product differences are stylistic — bigger battery and faster charging on the S07; lower weight and materially better quoted efficiency on the Sealion 06 — while the commercial difference inside the EU is structural: Changan's 20.7% rate loads roughly $1,022 per car of extra duty-and-VAT cost onto the S07, relative to BYD's 17%. An EU distributor reading that number knows exactly what the S07 has to do to earn its keep on the same forecourt; one ignoring it is guessing.

### Reasoning

First, the sticker gap is the least useful number in the file: $860 in China becomes $903–$2,457 landed depending on the destination, and the multiplier — ×1.07 to ×1.32 outside the EU, ×2.72 to ×2.86 inside it — is the decision-relevant figure. Second, the CVD spread is worth $1,022 per S07 in Germany: about 2.6% of its landed cost, but roughly 44% of the entire gap between the two cars — a bigger share than the difference in their factory prices supplies, and the single largest identifiable cause of the gap. Third, the products genuinely do not separate themselves: same segment, same claimed acceleration, complementary trade-offs on battery versus efficiency — so anyone presenting one of these cars as a value story "because it is cheaper in China" is solving the wrong equation. Fourth, market selection beats negotiation: the identical pair is $920 apart in Thailand and $2,457 in Denmark; the first decision on any deal is which border it crosses, not what discount can be extracted. Fifth, the parts not in either column — certification work at $3,250, service network, warranty and residuals — are where an importer's money actually gets made or lost, and a tariff-aware importer should budget for them with the same rigour as the duty lines.
