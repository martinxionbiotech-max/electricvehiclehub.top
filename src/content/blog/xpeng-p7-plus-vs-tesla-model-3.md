## TL;DR

The [XPeng P7+](/vehicles/xpeng-p7-plus/) is the first Model 3 rival we've priced to land below the Tesla's local price in any market: **$43,210 in the UK against a £37,990 Model 3 — a $7,489 gap in the importer's favour** — with further undercuts in the UAE (−$4,160) and Australia (−$2,386), and near-parity in Thailand ($38 apart). In the EU the tariff wall still decides it: XPeng's 20.7% countervailing duty lands the P7+ in Germany at **$50,838.96 (+81.6%)**, $8,478 above the Model 3's converted €36,990 — and even rebuilt at Tesla's 7.8% rate it stays roughly $3,750 above.

## Key statistics

- **P7+ base price:** $28,000 China ex-factory MSRP — 76.3 kWh, 725 km CLTC, 230 kW / 450 Nm, 5,056 mm long, 3,000 mm wheelbase, 2,073 kg
- **Germany, itemized:** 10% duty ($2,800.00) + 20.7% countervailing duty ($6,375.60) + 19% VAT ($7,063.36) + $6,600 fixed block = **$50,838.96 (+81.6%)**
- **Model 3 reference prices** (carried from our September comparisons): €36,990 Germany · €36,990 France · £37,990 UK · NOK 330,056 Norway · A$54,900* Australia · AED 144,990 UAE · THB 1,149,000 Thailand
- **Converted at 22 Sep 2026 rates:** ≈$42,361 · ≈$42,361 · ≈$50,699 · ≈$34,969 · ≈$39,036 · ≈$39,480 · ≈$34,648
- **P7+ landed, seven markets:** Germany $50,839 (+81.6%) · France $51,111 (+82.5%) · UK $43,210 (+54.3%) · Norway $42,250 (+50.9%) · Australia $36,650 (+30.9%) · UAE $35,320 (+26.1%) · Thailand $34,610 (+23.6%)
- **Gaps vs. the Model 3:** Germany **+$8,478** · France **+$8,750** · Norway **+$7,281** · UK **−$7,489** · UAE **−$4,160** · Australia **−$2,386** · Thailand **−$38**
- **Counterfactual, clearly labelled as arithmetic and not a shopping option:** rebuilt at Tesla's 7.8% rate, the German stack lands at **≈$46,110.86 (+64.7%)** — a $4,728 swing ($3,973.20 of duty plus $754.91 of VAT charged over it) that still leaves the car ≈$3,750 above the Model 3's converted entry
- **The rate spread:** XPeng pays the "other cooperating" **20.7%** — 12.9 points above Tesla's individually assessed **7.8%** (BYD 17.0%; Geely 18.8%; SAIC 35.3%)

*Sticker before on-road costs; Australia's drive-away basis runs higher, which would widen the P7+'s margin further.

## The basis: ex-factory versus landed cost

**Ex-factory** is the China gate price — $28,000 for the P7+. **Landed cost** is the cash an importer needs to clear that car into a destination market and register it: standard import duty first, then countervailing duty where the EU applies one, then VAT/GST compounding over both, plus a fixed block for RoRo freight, customs clearance, certification, registration and inland transport.

```
landed = base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs
```

The compounding order is not cosmetic: each layer is assessed on the running total, which is why 10% + 20.7% + 19% produces a +81.6% premium rather than a 49.7% one. The single formula behind every number below is documented at [Landed Cost Methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).

One more definition, because it decides how to read the tables: the P7+ column is an **import cost** — no dealer margin, no warranty provisioning, no finance — while the Model 3 column is a **retail price** that already contains Tesla's duties, logistics and margin. Cost versus price is the right frame for measuring importer headroom; it is not a like-for-like verdict on the two cars ([why the sticker price is never the landed price](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/)).

## The tariff wall, in one table

| Layer | XPeng P7+ into Germany | Tesla Model 3 for Europe |
|---|---|---|
| Standard import duty | 10% | 10% (embedded in retail) |
| Countervailing duty | **20.7%** (XPeng, "other cooperating") | **7.8%** (Tesla Shanghai) |
| VAT | 19% compounded | 19% (inside retail) |

Both cars are China-built as far as EU customs is concerned, and both sit inside the countervailing-duty regime — at very different rates. XPeng was not individually sampled in the EU's anti-subsidy investigation and carries the weighted-average **20.7%** "other cooperating" default, the same line as Chery, Changan, Xiaomi, Hongqi, NIO and Leapmotor ([the full rate map](/blog/nio-xpeng-leapmotor-countervailing-duty/)). The Model 3 volume sold in Europe is supplied from Giga Shanghai — Berlin builds the Model Y — so it pays Tesla's individually assessed **7.8%** under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754. No market outside the EU levies a countervailing duty at all: the UK, UAE, Australia and Thailand stacks stop at ordinary duty and VAT/GST.

Read the columns as what they are. The P7+ side is an import cost with no margin inside it; the Model 3 side is a finished retail product with Tesla's own costs and margin already paid for. A challenger needs its landed number *meaningfully below* the retail number to give an importer room to work: in three markets above it has it, in one the two sides are level to within $40, and in the EU and Norway the challenger sits above.

That UK result is the first undercut in this site's Model 3 comparison series — the Seal, SU7 and ET5 analyses all finished above the Tesla's local price in every market modelled — and the reason is arithmetic, not sentiment: a $28,000 China base multiplied through markets that charge no countervailing duty, against a Tesla retail price that holds firm locally.

## Worked example: the P7+ through German customs

| Line (Germany) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $28,000.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $2,800.00 |
| Countervailing duty (XPeng, 20.7%) | $6,375.60 |
| VAT (19%) | $7,063.36 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$50,838.96** |

Formula and check: `28,000 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 50,838.96`. Walking the compounding on the record's numbers: $28,000 × 1.10 = $30,800.00; × 1.207 = $37,175.60; × 1.19 = $44,238.96; + $6,600 = $50,838.96 — matching the vehicle record to the cent.

Of the $22,838.96 premium over base, $16,238.96 is duty and VAT lines and $6,600 is the fixed block. The countervailing duty alone is $6,375.60 — more than twice the standard duty and 22.8% of the factory price by itself.

The counterfactual isolates what the rate actually costs:

| Line | XPeng (20.7% CVD) | Rebuilt at Tesla's rate (7.8%) |
|---|---|---|
| Base + 10% duty | $30,800.00 | $30,800.00 |
| Countervailing duty | $6,375.60 | $2,402.40 |
| VAT (19%) | $7,063.36 | $6,308.46 |
| Fixed block | $6,600.00 | $6,600.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$50,838.96** | **≈$46,110.86** |

The 12.9-point rate difference is worth **$4,728** on this car — $3,973.20 of duty plus $754.91 of VAT charged over it — and even after the swap the German stack lands ≈$3,750 above the Model 3's converted entry price. Rates are assigned by regulation, not chosen by importers. The exercise exists to locate where the German problem lives: in the duty and tax stack, not in the car.

## Why the same arithmetic produces a flip

- **The EU is where the duty bites.** Germany (+$8,478) and France (+$8,750) are the two markets where the P7+ pays 20.7% and VAT compounds over it; both gaps are four figures, and the counterfactual that swaps in Tesla's rate only brings Germany within ≈$3,750 of the Tesla — never past it.
- **Non-EU markets drop the countervailing duty entirely, and the sign flips with it.** The UK lands $7,489 below the Model 3's local price, the UAE $4,160 below, Australia $2,386 below.
- **Norway is the exception that tests the rule.** Zero duty, zero countervailing duty — and the P7+ still lands $7,281 *above* the Model 3, because Tesla's Norwegian entry (NOK 330,056 ≈ $34,969) is among the cheapest Model 3 prices measured anywhere, while 25% VAT still compounds over the full base.
- **Thailand is parity, not a win — treat it as noise.** $38 on a ~$35,000 stack is about 0.1%; freight-lane and certification variance can flip that sign for your consignment, so do not build a case on it.
- **The series context.** The P7+'s $28,000 gate price is $2,320 below the SU7's and $13,850 below the ET5's, which is most of why earlier rounds never crossed while this one does. One rate caveat: BYD's 17.0% still gives its cars the smaller EU handicap than XPeng's 20.7%, so on EU arithmetic alone BYD remains the rate-advantaged challenger.

## What an importer should verify before pricing a P7+ deal

1. **Your VAT position.** On the German bill, $7,063.36 of the $50,838.96 is recoverable VAT for a registered B2B importer; the $9,175.60 of duty and countervailing duty never comes back. Price the deal both with and without recovery.
2. **Which side of the line your market sits on.** In the EU and Norway, the pitch has to be specification — the P7+ is above the Tesla before margin. In the UK, the UAE, Australia and Thailand, there is headroom to defend, but verify it against your own freight quotes and a current Model 3 price list before quoting anything.
3. **The range claim you plan to market.** 725 km is CLTC; the Model 3's 534 km is WLTP. Quoting them side by side without normalising hands your competitor a credibility win — [the explainer](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/) is the short version.
4. **Rate and rule stability.** The countervailing regime carries built-in reviews, and rates assigned today can move; confirm the current schedule, and confirm your homologation route, because the $3,250 certification line is a single-vehicle central estimate that scales differently at volume.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, the P7+ is the most complete Model 3 challenger this site has priced — not because it wins everywhere, but because it is the first where the answer changes by market. An importer can build a real margin case on it in the UK, the UAE, Australia and (razor-thin) Thailand, while in the EU the pitch has to be specification, against a four-figure gap rather than the five-figure ones earlier rounds produced.

### Reasoning

First, the arithmetic is transparent and checkable to the cent: $28,000 compounds to $50,838.96 in Germany, and the countervailing-duty line alone is the single largest non-VAT cost in the stack, more than double the standard duty. Second, the sign flips track jurisdictions, not products — remove the 20.7% duty and light VAT regimes do the rest — which tells an importer exactly which markets deserve allocation. Third, the counterfactual matters for negotiation: at Tesla's rate the German stack closes to ≈$3,750 above the Model 3, inside the range a disciplined importer and a motivated supplier can actually discuss; the gaps the SU7 and ET5 rounds measured in five figures were not. Fourth, the honest caveat: the undercuts come from tax architecture meeting a lower China base, not from any durable cost advantage held over Tesla — if duties or Tesla's price lists move, the flips move with them, so build the deal to survive a rate change.
