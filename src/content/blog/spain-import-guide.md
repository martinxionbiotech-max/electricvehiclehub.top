## TL;DR

Importing a Chinese EV to Spain means the full EU customs stack — **10% standard duty, a brand-specific countervailing duty of 17% to 35.3%, then 21% VAT on the running total** — landing a BYD Seal at about **$44,659 (+80.9%)**, $246 above Germany and $1,231 below Sweden. Spain is where that tariff wall meets Europe's most price-driven big market: BEV registrations are up more than 30% year on year, BYD just entered the brand top 10 for the first time, MG/SAIC is building a factory in Galicia, and the new Plan Auto+ subsidy tilts toward cheaper, EU-made cars.

## Key numbers

- **Import duty:** 10% — the EU's common external tariff on electric passenger cars, identical at every EU border (TARIC heading 8703.80)
- **Countervailing duty (CVD):** brand-specific and BEV-only — 17.0% BYD group · 18.8% Geely, Zeekr and Lynk & Co · 20.7% most other cooperating producers (Neta, Xiaomi, Changan and others) · 35.3% SAIC brands (MG, Maxus, Roewe, Wuling, IM) · 7.8% Tesla Shanghai
- **VAT:** 21% on the duty-paid value — mid-pack among the EU's big four (Germany 19%, France 20%, Italy 22%) and recoverable for VAT-registered businesses
- **Registration:** ~$110 in our records — and separately, Spanish law exempts BEVs from the CO₂-based registration tax (Impuesto de Matriculación) outright [Source: EAFO]
- **Worked example:** BYD Seal $24,690 ex-factory → **$44,659 landed (+80.9%)**
- **Market context:** 68,544 new passenger cars registered in August 2026, up 11.8% — the strongest August since 2019; battery-electric and plug-in hybrid cars took a record 27.7% share of the month [Source: ANFAC, September 2026]
- **BEV pulse:** 9,446 pure electric cars registered in August, up 31.6% year on year; 85,306 year-to-date, up 34.2% [Source: DGT data, September 2026]
- **Chinese momentum:** BYD 3,191 units in August (+74.7%), seventh brand overall and the best-selling Chinese brand for a second straight month; MG 2,531; Chery's Omoda and Jaecoo 2,312 combined; Leapmotor 740 (+270%); XPeng +520% [Source: ChinaEVHome/ANFAC]
- **The policy turn:** Plan Auto+, Spain's new national incentive scheme, runs 2026–2030 with €400 million a year and favors vehicles under €35,000 and EU-made content — a relevant nuance for anyone shipping cars from China [Source: SharingMobility.news/NewMobility.news]

## The terms that decide this market

- **Landed cost** — the importer-side cost to bring a China-market car to a Spanish buyer, registered and compliant: base price → import duty → countervailing duty → VAT → freight → clearance → certification → registration → inland transport. The full formula is documented in our [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).
- **MFN duty** — the EU's "most-favoured-nation" tariff of 10% that applies to cars without a free-trade agreement covering them. China has no EU car FTA, so 10% is the floor.
- **Countervailing duty (CVD)** — the anti-subsidy tariff layered on top of the MFN duty. For Chinese BEVs the EU set definitive, brand-specific rates in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, effective 31 October 2024 for five years; no amendment to the rate schedule could be verified as of September 2026. Plug-in hybrids and range-extenders sit outside the scope. Confirm the rate for the exact exporting producer before contracting.
- **CIF** — cost, insurance, freight; the customs value. In our model duty applies to the ex-factory value, with freight, clearance and certification carried as a fixed block per market.
- **Compounding, not adding** — duty first, CVD on the duty-paid value, then VAT on the running total. Never sum 10 + 35.3 + 21 = 66.3%; the layers multiply.
- **Impuesto de Matriculación** — Spain's CO₂-banded registration tax, charged once when a vehicle is registered. BEVs are exempt at 0% [Source: EAFO]. Our model's $110 registration line covers administrative fees, not this tax.
- **IVTM** — the annual municipal road tax. Municipalities may reduce it by up to 75% for BEVs; Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia apply significant discounts [Source: EAFO].
- **Plan Auto+ / MOVES III** — Spain's purchase-incentive schemes. MOVES III ran through 31 December 2025 (up to €7,000 with scrappage, €4,500 without); Auto+ replaced it from January 2026, paying the incentive as a point-of-sale discount.
- **WVTA** — Whole Vehicle Type Approval, the EU-wide homologation route; a China-only homologation needs single-vehicle approval, which is slower and costlier — see our [WVTA explainer](/blog/wvta-vs-single-vehicle-approval/).

## What a Chinese EV pays at the Spanish border

| Layer | Rate for a China-origin BEV | Levied on | In our landed model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Import duty (MFN) | **10%** | CIF value | Yes |
| Countervailing duty | **17% BYD / 18.8% Geely group / 20.7% default / 35.3% SAIC / 7.8% Tesla Shanghai** | duty-paid value | Yes |
| VAT | **21%** | running total (value + duty + CVD) | Yes |
| Registration & plates | ~**$110** flat in our records | — | Yes |
| Freight, clearance, certification, inland | $2,000 + $350 + $3,250 + $500 fixed | — | Yes |

Read the table top to bottom and the structure is clear: Spain adds no car-specific purchase tax beyond VAT at the border — because the country sits inside the EU customs union, every euro of the customs stack is set in Brussels, not Madrid. That is why "which EU country is cheapest" collapses to the VAT rate plus the registration line, a point our [Germany vs Netherlands vs France comparison](/blog/germany-netherlands-france-import-comparison/) works through in detail.

| Neta V | 20.7% | $8,320 | $19,576 | +135.3% |
| MG 4 | 35.3% | $9,690 | $23,660 | +144.2% |
| MG 4X | 35.3% | $13,860 | $31,170 | +124.9% |
| BYD Dolphin | 17% | $14,020 | $28,043 | +100.0% |
| BYD Atto 3 | 17% | $16,260 | $31,531 | +93.9% |
| BYD Seal | 17% | $24,690 | $44,659 | +80.9% |
| Xiaomi SU7 | 20.7% | $30,320 | $54,920 | +81.1% |
| Zeekr 009 | 18.8% | $61,830 | $103,977 | +68.2% |

Two patterns jump out, and both are structural.

First, **the brand you pick matters almost as much as the price you negotiate.** The MG 4X and the BYD Dolphin sit only $160 apart ex-factory, yet the SAIC car pays 35.3% against BYD's 17% and lands **$3,127 higher**. At an identical $9,690 ex-factory price, the gap between the 17% and 35.3% tiers is about **$2,360** once duty and VAT compound; at $24,690 it is about **$6,014**. That spread is set in Brussels, not Madrid — and it is the single biggest lever a Spain-bound importer does not control.

Second, **cheap cars wear the premium worst.** A $5,000 Wuling pays the same fixed block as a $61,830 Zeekr — about $6,210 in our model — so its percentage premium runs to +204.3% while the Zeekr 009 "only" pays +68.2%. Percentage comparisons between a microcar and a luxury MPV tell you almost nothing about either import decision. Absolute landed cost and incremental tax do.

### Worked example: a BYD Seal in Spain

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (ex-factory China) | $24,690 |
| Import duty (10% of base) | $2,469 |
| Countervailing duty (17% of base + duty) | $4,617 |
| VAT (21% of base + duty + CVD) | $6,673 |
| Freight, clearance, certification, registration, inland | $6,210 |
| **Total landed** | **$44,659** |

Formula: `base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs`. Every input comes from EV Hub's tariff and landed-cost records dated 2026-09-15.

## Spain vs its neighbours: the VAT ladder inside the EU

Because the customs stack is identical across the customs union, the same car's price in the EU's big markets moves with exactly two things: the VAT rate and the registration line. On the Seal:

| Market | VAT (our records) | Registration line | Seal landed | vs Spain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | 19% | $500 | $44,413 | −$246 |
| France | 20% | $400 | $44,631 | −$28 |
| **Spain** | **21%** | **$110** | **$44,659** | **—** |
| Italy | 22% | $250 | $45,117 | +$458 |
| Sweden | 25% | $70 | $45,890 | +$1,231 |

(negative = below Spain)

Spain's position is the middle of the ladder, and instructive: its 21% VAT is two points above Germany's — worth about $636 on this car — but its registration line is $110 against Germany's $500, so the net gap shrinks to $246. Against Italy, Spain saves $458; against Sweden, $1,231. For a VAT-registered business the VAT differences largely wash out because the tax is recoverable, leaving the registration line and financing costs as the real comparators.

## Plan Auto+: Spain's new subsidy and what it means for imports

Spain's incentive landscape turned over at the end of 2025. MOVES III, the long-running purchase scheme, closed on 31 December 2025 having offered up to €7,000 for a BEV with scrappage and €4,500 without. Its successor — Plan Auto+, approved in mid-2026 as part of the Plan Auto 2030 strategy — runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2030 with **€400 million a year**, and pays the incentive at the point of sale as a dealer discount rather than a months-later reimbursement.

For private buyers, Auto+ is worth up to **€4,500 plus a mandatory €1,000 dealer discount**; the self-employed can get up to €6,000, and businesses eligible under the Climate Social Fund up to €7,000 for cars and €12,000 for vans. Rentals and leases of at least three years qualify, and the scheme covers new and nearly-new vehicles — up to 12 months old and already registered in Spain. Three details matter for anyone importing from China:

- **Eligibility is content-sensitive.** The scheme favours vehicles under €35,000 and EU-manufactured models with EU-produced batteries — and how cars imported from China will be treated under those criteria could not be verified from primary sources at the time of writing. Treat this as UNKNOWN and confirm with the dealer or IDAE before pricing a deal.
- **The scheme requires Spain's ZERO emissions label**, which battery-electric vehicles qualify for; plug-in hybrids sit lower in the subsidy scale.
- **Charging infrastructure is not funded** under Auto+, unlike MOVES III.

Below the subsidy line, the structural benefits remain: BEVs pay no Impuesto de Matriculación, municipalities can cut the annual IVTM by up to 75%, and companies can deduct the 21% VAT on BEVs used for professional purposes [Source: EAFO]. The 15% personal income-tax deduction that accompanied MOVES III was reported cancelled for 2026 amid the transition, leaving a short-term gap in the incentive landscape [Source: EAFO].

## Spain is where the tariff wall is being answered with factories

Spain is not just a destination for Chinese EVs — it has become the chosen bridgehead for the brands paying the highest duties. In June 2026, MG confirmed a factory in Spain — announced by the regional government of Galicia — with an annual production target of **120,000 vehicles** [Source: electrive.com]. The logic is direct: a car built in Spain avoids the 35.3% CVD that MG's China-built cars pay at the border.

The list is growing:

- **BYD** is weighing a third European plant, with Spain favoured for its relatively low costs, per Reuters-sourced reporting [Source: electrive.com, October 2025].
- **Leapmotor**, distributed by Stellantis, is preparing B10 production at the Stellantis plant in Zaragoza — delayed to Q3 2026 — to serve Europe from inside the customs union [Source: electrive.com].
- **Chery** has assembled vehicles in Barcelona with local partner Ebro-EV Motors since 2024 [Source: electrive.com].
- **CATL and Stellantis** are building a €4.1 billion LFP battery plant in Spain, with operations targeted for late 2026 [Source: electrive.com].

For an importer the reading is straightforward: Spain is simultaneously the most active market for Chinese brands and the market where they are most aggressively relocating production. Every factory that opens in Galicia, Zaragoza or Barcelona changes the landed-cost math for the China-built version of the same car.

## What importers should check before committing

1. **Which CVD tier does your brand sit in?** The difference between 17% and 35.3% on the same ex-factory price is worth about $2,360 on a sub-$10k car and $6,014 on a $24,690 one. Confirm the rate for the exact exporting producer, not just the badge.
2. **Are you buying as a business or a private individual?** VAT-registered businesses can deduct the 21% import VAT on commercial use; private buyers cannot. The same car has two different effective costs depending on who registers it.
3. **Check Auto+ eligibility early.** The scheme's content criteria favour EU-made cars; whether a China-built import qualifies — and for how much — needs confirmation before you promise a customer a net price.
4. **Budget the registration steps properly.** A privately imported car needs an ITV inspection and DGT registration; the exact fee schedule and documentation for private vehicle import could not be verified from primary sources at the time of writing — treat specifics as UNKNOWN and confirm with a customs broker before wiring money.
5. **Check the homologation route.** A car with EU whole-vehicle type approval is registered directly; a China-only homologation needs single-vehicle approval — see our [WVTA vs single-vehicle approval](/blog/wvta-vs-single-vehicle-approval/) explainer.
6. **Left-hand drive and CCS2.** Spain is a left-hand-drive market with CCS2 charging; a China-market car needs the right port and charging configuration — our [CCS2 vs GB/T note](/blog/ccs2-vs-gbt-charging/) covers the compatibility cost.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view, Spain is the proof that a tariff stack alone does not decide a market's outcome. Chinese brands pay the full EU customs bill here — the same 10% duty and 17–35.3% CVD that applies in Germany or France — yet Spain is where they are growing fastest: BYD cracked the country's brand top 10 for the first time in August 2026, and the broader cohort (MG, Chery, Leapmotor, XPeng) all posted gains. Spain is the EU's volume-price market, and value positioning survives even an 80–200% landed premium versus China pricing, because the comparison local buyers make is against European sticker prices, not Chinese ones.

**Reasoning.** First, the demand numbers are unambiguous: a record 27.7% electrified share in August and BEV registrations up 31.6% year on year, in what ANFAC calls the strongest August since 2019 — this is a market converting to electric quickly, and Chinese brands are capturing that growth rather than waiting for it. Second, the counter-move is already visible: MG — the brand paying the harshest 35.3% rate — is the one building the 120,000-unit plant in Galicia, and BYD is scouting a third European site with Spain favoured. When the most tariff-penalised players choose your country to localise, the tariff is shaping investment rather than stopping it. Third, the policy tilt cuts both ways for importers: Auto+ subsidises cars under €35,000 with EU content in mind, so a China-built import competes against subsidised domestic production — the customs bill above the border and the content rules below it squeeze the same deal. My read is that the sustainable import play in Spain is the mid-price BEV segment where Chinese cost structures are strongest, priced with the full stack and VAT recovery modelled in from day one — not the sub-€20k end where subsidy-ineligible imports meet the fixed-cost block that hurts cheap cars most.
