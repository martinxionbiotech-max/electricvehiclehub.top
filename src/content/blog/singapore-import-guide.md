## TL;DR

On the border, importing a Chinese EV to Singapore costs a **20% excise duty on the car's Open Market Value plus 9% GST**, compounded on top of it — a BYD Seal priced at $24,690 ex-factory China lands at **$37,154.52 (+50.5%)** on EV Hub's model, and premiums across our catalog run from +43.6% (Zeekr 001) to +128.0% (Wuling Hongguang MINI EV). Keep the boundary of that number in view, though: **our landed-cost model excludes Singapore's COE and ARF**, and the September 2026 COE alone closed at **S$133,009 (Category A)** and **S$135,001 (Category B)** — before a registration tax that runs up to 320% of OMV. The border half is mid-pack for Southeast Asia; the full bill is where Singapore becomes one of the most expensive car markets on earth.

## Key statistics

- **Excise duty (our model's duty line):** 20% of the Open Market Value, paid to Singapore Customs at import
- **GST:** 9%, charged on the total cost of importing the vehicle — the car, freight, insurance and any duties
- **Countervailing duty:** none — Singapore runs no EU-style anti-subsidy tariff on China-origin EVs
- **COE, September 2026 first exercise:** Category A **S$133,009** (a record — the first time it crossed S$130,000), Category B **S$135,001**, Category E **S$137,890**. Outside our landed-cost model
- **ARF:** 100% to 320% of OMV in five tiers — also outside our landed-cost model
- **Rebates currently offsetting the ARF on EVs:** VES Band A up to **S$22,500** (2026 registrations); EEAI 45% of ARF capped at **S$7,500**, expiring 31 December 2026
- **Worked example:** BYD Seal $24,690 → **$37,154.52 landed at the border (+50.5%)**
- **The real threshold:** the COE — roughly **US$105,000** for Category A at prevailing exchange rates

## Three stacked systems decide a Singapore car bill

Most import guides start with a customs line. Singapore needs three lines, because three separate systems combine to set what a new car actually costs:

1. **Border charges** — excise duty and GST, paid to Singapore Customs when the car is imported. This is the layer our landed-cost model captures.
2. **Registration taxes** — the Registration Fee, the Additional Registration Fee (ARF), and the emissions-based rebates or surcharges (VES and EEAI) that offset it. Assessed when the car is registered with LTA.
3. **The right to own the car at all** — a Certificate of Entitlement (COE), won at auction, valid ten years. No COE, no registration, no road use. This layer sits outside any customs formula.

Every Singapore figure on EV Hub is a system-1 number. This guide prices the border first, then walks through what systems 2 and 3 add — and why the second half is where this market differs from every other one we cover.

## Defining the terms that shape the bill

- **OMV (Open Market Value)** — the value Singapore Customs assigns to the vehicle as imported: purchase price plus freight, insurance and all other sale-and-delivery charges. The excise duty, GST and ARF all key off it.
- **Excise duty** — 20% of OMV for cars, payable at import.
- **GST** — Singapore's 9% goods and services tax, charged at import on the cost of the vehicle plus freight, insurance and duties.
- **COE (Certificate of Entitlement)** — a ten-year entitlement to own and use a vehicle, obtained by bidding in LTA's open exercises, held twice a month. Cars bid in Category A, B or E.
- **ARF (Additional Registration Fee)** — a tiered registration tax on OMV: 100% of the first S$20,000, rising to 320% above S$80,000.
- **VES (Vehicular Emissions Scheme)** — the emissions banding that grants ARF rebates (Band A) or applies surcharges (C1–C3). Electric cars are rated using an emissions factor of 0.4g CO2 per Wh applied to their energy consumption.
- **EEAI (Electric Vehicle Early Adoption Incentive)** — an additional ARF rebate for EVs: 45% of ARF, capped at S$7,500 for 2026 registrations. It expires on 31 December 2026.
- **PARF (Preferential Additional Registration Fee)** — the rebate returned when the car is deregistered. For cars registered from 13 February 2026, it is calculated on a lower schedule and capped at S$30,000.

## The border stack, as our model counts it

| Layer | Rate | Applied to | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Excise duty | 20% | Ex-factory price | LTA: 20% of OMV for cars; this is the standard duty line in our model |
| GST | 9% | Ex-factory price + duty | Singapore Customs charges GST on the car plus freight, insurance and duties; our model applies it to the car and duty only, so the real GST line runs higher |
| Countervailing duty | 0% | — | No anti-subsidy tariff on China-origin EVs |
| Fixed block | $4,860 | Flat, per vehicle | Freight, clearance, certification, registration ($260) and inland transport |

Two percentage lines compound in a specific order — duty first, GST last, on the duty-paid value — so the border tax works out at **+30.8% of the car price** before any fixed cost: 1.20 × 1.09 − 1. That is the honest headline for Singapore's customs stage, and it is exactly the arithmetic behind every number in the table below.

This is the same pattern we have documented for other markets, and it is why the percentage premium needs the absolute number next to it.

## Worked example: a BYD Seal in Singapore

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (ex-factory China) | $24,690.00 |
| Excise duty (20% of base) | $4,938.00 |
| GST (9% of base + duty) | $2,666.52 |
| Fixed block — freight, clearance, certification, registration, inland | $4,860.00 |
| **Total landed, border only** | **$37,154.52** |

Formula: `base × (1 + duty) × (1 + GST) + fixed costs`. Inputs from EV Hub's tariff and vehicle records, verified 15 September 2026.

**Where this model stops — and why that matters more here than anywhere else.** Three exclusions to be explicit about:

1. **COE is not in the number.** The largest single item on a Singapore car bill sits outside the model by design. September 2026 prices started at S$133,009.
2. **ARF and the VES/EEAI rebate schedule are not in the number either.** They are registration-stage items, assessed on the Customs OMV, not on our ex-factory base.
3. **The base is an estimate, and the GST base is conservative.** Singapore Customs assesses its own OMV, and the statutory GST applies to freight and insurance as well as the car and duty. Our model applies GST to the car and duty only. Net effect: treat the border figure as a planning floor, not a quote.

## The other half, part one: the COE

The Certificate of Entitlement is an auction for the right to own a car, run by LTA twice a month, with quotas set quarterly and certificates valid for ten years. Renewal happens at the prevailing quota premium (PQP) — the three-month moving average of COE prices — without bidding. Categories for cars:

| Category | What it covers | September 2026 price | September PQP |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Cars up to 1,600cc and 97kW; fully electric cars up to 110kW (147bhp) | S$133,009 | S$126,208 |
| B | Cars above 1,600cc or 97kW; fully electric cars above 110kW | S$135,001 | S$128,697 |
| E | Open — all categories except motorcycles | S$137,890 | Not applicable |

September's first exercise set records: Category A rose 3.5% to S$133,009 — the first time the category crossed S$130,000 — while Category B climbed 3.1% to S$135,001. LTA attributed the jump partly to the longer interval since the previous exercise, and reminded buyers to bid prudently. Those numbers are not in our landed cost, and they dwarf it: at prevailing rates the Category A certificate alone is roughly US$105,000, which is more than six times the entire border cost of a BYD Seagull and nearly three times a BYD Seal's. **Category placement matters for Chinese EVs.** Power output decides which auction you are in: ≤110kW is Category A, above is Category B. By rated power in our catalog, the Wuling Hongguang MINI EV (20kW), BYD Seagull (55kW) and BYD Dolphin (70kW) sit in Category A; the MG4 (120kW), BYD Atto 3 (150kW), [BYD Seal](/vehicles/byd-seal/) (170kW) and [Zeekr 001](/vehicles/zeekr-001/) (680kW) need Category B. Confirm the approved rating for the exact variant — the category decides which auction you are in, and the two auctions move independently.

## The other half, part two: ARF and the emissions clock

The ARF is the second registration-stage gate, and it scales with OMV in five tiers:

| OMV band | ARF rate |
|---|---|
| First S$20,000 | 100% |
| Next S$20,000 (S$20,001–40,000) | 140% |
| Next S$20,000 (S$40,001–60,000) | 190% |
| Next S$20,000 (S$60,001–80,000) | 250% |
| Above S$80,000 | 320% |

Run the published tiers and the first S$80,000 of OMV generates **S$136,000** of ARF on its own; every dollar above that adds S$3.20. Against that, two EV rebates currently offset the bill:

| Scheme | 2026 registrations | 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| VES Band A (cars) | Up to S$22,500 off ARF | S$20,000 off ARF |
| EEAI | 45% of ARF, capped at S$7,500 | Expires 31 December 2026 |
| PARF exit rebate | 30% of ARF if deregistered within five years, capped at S$30,000 | Same |

For a Chinese EV — zero tailpipe emissions — the VES band is decided by the CO2 rating LTA computes from electricity consumption at 0.4g CO2 per Wh. A 13.0 kWh/100km car like the Seal rates at about 52 g CO2/km, comfortably inside the Band A threshold of 90 g/km. The minimum ARF, normally S$5,000, is lowered to S$0 for electric cars registered through 2027, so the rebates can offset the ARF to zero on lower-OMV vehicles. Add the S$350 Registration Fee and the registration stage is complete. Note the two clocks this section runs on: EEAI dies at the end of 2026, and the VES rebate steps down in 2027. A Singapore registration is partly a calendar decision.

## So what does the full bill look like?

We deliberately do not publish a single out-the-door figure for Singapore. OMV is assessed car by car, and the COE is an auction outcome — any precise total would be false precision. But the shape is clear: start with the border figure from the table above, add a COE (S$133,009 to S$137,890 across September's categories), add ARF on the assessed OMV, subtract the VES and EEAI rebates. For every model in our catalog, the COE line alone exceeds the entire border cost of the car. That is the sentence worth taking away — and the reason the [margin math for importers](/blog/fleet-buyer-margin-math/) in Singapore starts with certificate strategy, not freight rates.

The BYD Seal, priced through four Southeast Asian markets on our model:

| Market | Duty layer | Consumption tax | Seal landed | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Singapore | 20% | 9% GST | $37,154.52 | +50.5% |
| Malaysia | 5% | 10% SST | $33,166.95 | +34.3% |
| Thailand | 0% | 7% VAT | $31,068.30 | +25.8% |
| Indonesia | 50% | 11% VAT | $45,808.85 | +85.5% |

Read across the row: Singapore's border cost sits between Thailand and Malaysia on one side and Indonesia on the other — mild at customs relative to the region's extremes. The difference is what happens after customs. Singapore is the only market here that auctions the right to own the car, and in September 2026 that auction cleared above S$133,000 for the mainstream category. That is why Singapore tops global rankings of car ownership cost while its import tariff is unremarkable — the cost is deliberately pushed from the border into ownership. Indonesia does the opposite: it hits the border hard (see the [Indonesia import guide](/blog/indonesia-import-guide/) for how the 50% duty landed), then lets the car be owned. Same region, opposite designs.

## What buyers should check before committing

- **What OMV will Singapore Customs assess?** Excise, GST and ARF all key off it — get the assessment route right before any other math.
- **Which COE category does the exact variant fall into?** 110kW or below means Category A; above means Category B. A detuned or high-power variant can change your auction.
- **What is the registration calendar?** EEAI expires 31 December 2026; the VES Band A rebate steps down in 2027. A month can matter more than a discount.
- **Is there a used-car route, and does it fit?** Imported used cars must be under three years old and attract a S$10,000 used car surcharge.
- **Who funds the COE bid, and what does it do to your working capital?** The certificate is the single largest cash item in the transaction.
- **Can you recover the GST?** The import GST is paid at the border regardless; whether a business can recover GST on motor vehicles is a narrow area we did not verify for this guide — treat it as UNKNOWN and model the GST as a cost until a Singapore GST adviser says otherwise.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Singapore charge import duty on Chinese EVs?**
Singapore charges a 20% excise duty on the Open Market Value at import, plus 9% GST on the import cost, and applies no countervailing duty on China-origin EVs. On our model that compounds to +30.8% of the car price before logistics, and +50.5% all-in on a BYD Seal.

**Is the COE included in EV Hub's Singapore landed-cost figures?**
No — and the caveat is explicit for this market. Our model covers the border: excise duty, GST, freight, clearance, certification, registration and inland transport. The COE and ARF are registration-stage costs and are excluded. In September 2026 the COE alone ran S$133,009 to S$137,890 depending on category.

**Why is Singapore's premium lower than Indonesia's if Singapore is the more expensive market?**
Because they are different lenses. The border premium (+50.5% versus +85.5%) reflects Singapore's milder customs stack. Add the ownership layer and the ranking flips: no other market we cover sits a six-figure auction between you and the road.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view, Singapore is the clearest proof on earth that landed cost is not market viability: the border bill on a Chinese EV is mid-pack for Southeast Asia, while the COE auction — S$133,009 and up in September 2026 — makes Singapore one of the most expensive places anywhere to put a new car on the road. Any Singapore number that stops at customs is half a number, and the wrong half at that.

**Reasoning.** First, the COE is not a tax on the car; it is the price of the right to own one, and at September's prices a Category A certificate alone cost more than six times the entire border cost of the cheapest Chinese EVs we track. When the right to own dwarfs the cost to import, tariffs stop being the story. Second, ARF tops out at 320% of OMV while the 2026 PARF cut (the exit rebate now 30% of ARF, capped at S$30,000) means the state captures heavily at both ends of the ownership cycle — that changes financing and resale math, not just sticker prices. Third, the sweeteners are a calendar, not a structure: EEAI expires on 31 December 2026 and the VES Band A rebate steps down in 2027, so anyone planning a Singapore registration should plan the timing as carefully as the car.
