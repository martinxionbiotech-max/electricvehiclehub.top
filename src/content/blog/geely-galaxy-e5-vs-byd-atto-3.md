## TL;DR

The [Geely Galaxy E5](/vehicles/geely-galaxy-e5/) undercuts the [BYD Atto 3](/vehicles/byd-atto-3/) by $800 at the factory gate and lands below it in every one of the 30 markets modelled — **$856 (Thailand) to $1,056 (UK)** across the seven markets here, including **$30,641.72 vs $31,502.68 in Germany**. That net result sits on two different EU countervailing-duty lines — Geely group 18.8%, BYD 17.0%, neither the 20.7% default — and inside the EU the E5's higher rate claws back **$364.27 per car** in Germany, roughly 45% of its ex-factory head start.

## Key statistics

- **Galaxy E5 base price:** $15,460 China ex-factory MSRP — 60.22 kWh battery, 530 km CLTC, 160 kW / 320 Nm, 4,615 mm long
- **BYD Atto 3 base price:** $16,260 China ex-factory MSRP — 49.92 kWh Blade battery, 430 km CLTC, 150 kW / 310 Nm, 4,455 mm long
- **Factory-gate gap:** $800; landed gap **$856 (Thailand) to $1,056 (UK)** across the seven markets here — **$840–$1,392** across all 30 markets modelled in the two vehicle records
- **Germany:** E5 $30,641.72 (+98.2%) vs Atto 3 $31,502.68 (+93.7%)
- **United Kingdom:** $26,657.20 (+72.4%) vs $27,713.20 (+70.4%) — no countervailing duty in either column
- **Thailand:** $21,192.20 (+37.1%) vs $22,048.20 (+35.6%) — zero duty both ways, the narrowest gap in the set
- **The two duty lines:** Geely group 18.8% vs BYD 17.0% — neither is the 20.7% rate for other cooperating producers; the countervailing duty applies in EU markets only
- **Germany, absolute dollars:** E5 countervailing duty $3,197.13 vs Atto 3 $3,040.62 — the cheaper car pays the larger CVD bill
- **The rate gap's price:** $364.27 per car in Germany; at rate parity the German gap would be $1,225.22, not $860.96
- **The fixed block:** $6,600 per car in Germany = 42.7% of the E5's base, 40.6% of the Atto 3's — before any tax line

## The basis: ex-factory, landed cost, and two duty lines

**Ex-factory** is the price at the factory gate in China — what the car costs before it moves anywhere. Both base prices in this comparison are China ex-factory MSRP from the EV Hub catalogue: $15,460 for the E5, $16,260 for the Atto 3 (sold as the Yuan Plus in China).

**Landed cost** is what an importer pays to clear the car through customs in the destination market: the base compounded through standard import duty, countervailing duty where the EU applies it, and VAT or GST — plus a flat block covering RoRo freight, customs clearance, certification, registration and inland transport. It is the cash needed at the border, not a retail price; no importer margin, dealer spread or warranty provisioning is inside.

```
landed = base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs
```

Duty is assessed on the car's value, countervailing duty compounds on the duty-paid amount, and VAT is charged on the duty- and CVD-inclusive total. The flat block is added after the tax stack. Adding the rates instead of compounding them is the most expensive arithmetic error in importing — the [methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/) documents the order, the fixed-cost benchmarks and the producer-specific duty table.

The two lines in this comparison are the EU's producer-specific countervailing duties: **18.8% for Geely group vehicles** — which travel under the Galaxy, Zeekr and Lynk & Co badges — and **17.0% for BYD**, against a 20.7% default for other cooperating producers. Neither car here pays the default, and the 1.8-point gap between the two is the quietest variable in the whole comparison. Outside the EU the countervailing duty disappears entirely: the UK, Norway, Australia, the Gulf and ASEAN markets carry standard duty and VAT/GST only. For registered B2B importers VAT/GST is usually recoverable — treat it as border cash flow, because duty and countervailing duty never come back.

## The gap: $856–$1,056, and the $364 that shrinks it

| Market | Galaxy E5 landed | Atto 3 landed | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | $30,641.72 | $31,502.68 | $860.96 |
| France | $30,743.75 | $31,611.94 | $868.19 |
| United Kingdom | $26,657.20 | $27,713.20 | $1,056.00 |
| Norway | $26,575.00 | $27,575.00 | $1,000.00 |
| Australia | $22,856.00 | $23,736.00 | $880.00 |
| United Arab Emirates | $21,494.65 | $22,376.65 | $882.00 |
| Thailand | $21,192.20 | $22,048.20 | $856.00 |

Read the no-CVD lanes first, because there the gap is pure arithmetic — the $800 factory gap compounded by duty and VAT, nothing else:

- **United Kingdom:** 800 × 1.10 × 1.20 = $1,056.00
- **Norway:** 800 × 1.25 = $1,000.00
- **UAE:** 800 × 1.05 × 1.05 = $882.00
- **Australia:** 800 × 1.10 = $880.00
- **Thailand:** 800 × 1.07 = $856.00

The EU rows are the only two where both cars pay producer-specific countervailing duties — and they sit at the tight end of the set ($860.96 in Germany, $868.19 in France; second and third only to Thailand's $856), because the rate differential works against the cheaper car. Baseline arithmetic: stamp BYD's 17.0% on the E5 and the German gap would be $1,225.22, the French gap $1,235.52. The 1.8-point premium costs the E5 **$364.27 and $367.33 per car** respectively — call it $365, or roughly 45% of its $800 head start. That is the arithmetic price of being a Geely under this regulation, and it is a counterfactual, not a shopping option.

One presentation warning before these numbers travel: the E5 wears the heavier percentage premium in all seven markets (+98.2% against +93.7% in Germany, +37.1% against +35.6% in Thailand). That is not a flaw in the car — it is the same flat block spreading over a smaller base. Read dollars for the decision and percentages for the burden; the percentages point at the wrong car.

## Worked example: both cars through one German customs office

| Line (Germany) | Galaxy E5 | BYD Atto 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $15,460.00 | $16,260.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $1,546.00 | $1,626.00 |
| Countervailing duty (18.8% / 17.0%) | $3,197.13 | $3,040.62 |
| VAT (19%) | $3,838.59 | $3,976.06 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$30,641.72** | **$31,502.68** |

Both formulas, in compounding order: `15,460 × 1.10 × 1.188 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 30,641.72` and `16,260 × 1.10 × 1.17 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 31,502.68`. Re-adding the lines above reproduces both totals to the cent; the full method is in [the methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).

The German gap of $860.96 decomposes line by line: **$800.00** of ex-factory base, **plus $80.00** more standard duty and **$137.47** more VAT paid by the Atto 3, **minus $156.51** more countervailing duty paid by the E5. Note the direction of that last line — the cheaper car writes the larger CVD cheque, because 18.8% on a duty-paid $17,006 beats 17.0% on a duty-paid $17,886.

Then the flat block. $6,600 of freight, clearance, certification, registration and inland transport is **42.7% of the E5's ex-factory price** and 40.6% of the Atto 3's — before a single tax line lands. The frame applied to the [Onvo L60](/blog/onvo-l60-vs-tesla-model-y/) fits at this end of the ladder with full force: fixed costs do not scale down with the sticker, so cheap cars carry the heaviest percentage premia. Both of these cars land at close to double their base price in Germany (+98.2% and +93.7%) — and it is the cheaper car that carries the heavier premium. Cash planning, not badging, decides these deals.

## Where this comparison can go wrong

1. **Cost versus sticker.** Both landed columns are import costs, not retail prices — no margin, warranty reserve or dealer spread inside. Subtracting one from a showroom price measures the wrong thing; [why the sticker price is never the landed price](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/) covers that error properly.
2. **Mixing range cycles.** 530 km and 430 km are both CLTC — fair against each other, not against WLTP or real-world distance ([the explainer](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/)).
3. **Treating VAT as a final cost.** Registered B2B importers usually recover it; duty and CVD never come back. The pair's ranking is unaffected — both cars pay the same VAT rate within a market — but cash-at-border is not net cost.
4. **Fixed blocks are central estimates.** They are volume- and lane-sensitive; small orders land above these numbers, consolidated volumes below them.
5. **Duty regimes move.** The EU countervailing regime is under periodic review, and the 1.8-point differential is the most exposed line in this analysis. Model the deal at current rates and stress it a few points either way before committing volume.
6. **The margin you must add.** $30,642 buys the E5 in Germany; it does not sell it. Every dollar of importer or dealer margin narrows the gap against the Atto 3 — which is the point of a gap: headroom, not profit.

## What an importer should do with these two

- **If the deal needs the lowest border cash:** the E5 wins in every market modelled — $856–$1,056 across the seven here, $840–$1,392 across all 30. The largest advantage sits in the UK ($1,056), a right-hand-drive, no-CVD lane where configuration work starts earlier than tariff work.
- **If you are pricing EU volume:** underwrite the rate differential as your own exposure. The E5's $364–$367 per-car penalty in Germany and France is a policy variable, not a product variable — it could narrow or widen without either car changing.
- **If you are comparing against local retail:** use the gap as headroom, then subtract margin, warranty provisioning and resale support before it means anything; the [fleet margin math](/blog/fleet-buyer-margin-math/) is the right companion.
- **If you are sourcing volume:** the negotiable half is the flat block — freight consolidation, batch certification, registration handling — not the 1.8-point rate difference. Vendors can move the first; nobody can negotiate the second.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, this is the rare head-to-head where the cheaper car also wins the specification sheet — and the numbers still refuse to produce a blowout. The E5 lands below the Atto 3 in every market modelled ($856–$1,056 across the seven here), but roughly half of its $800 ex-factory edge is policy-dependent: inside the EU, Geely's higher countervailing-duty rate trims about $365 per car off the advantage before margin. An importer choosing the Atto 3 is not buying a tariff story — BYD's 17.0% is the lower line — they are buying whatever the landed table cannot see. On the arithmetic shown here, the E5 is the default choice; the Atto 3 needs a non-numeric reason to win the order.

### Reasoning

First, the product case is unusually clean: the cheaper car is longer, roomier on wheelbase, carries 10.3 kWh more battery, quotes 100 km more CLTC range and charges faster — against a 30 kg weight penalty. Price gaps usually buy something; this one buys the better sheet and still saves money, which is why the landed numbers matter more here than in most comparisons. Second, the tariff case is genuinely two-sided: both cars sit below the 20.7% default, so neither is a tariff victim relative to non-cooperating producers — but BYD's 1.8-point advantage is real money ($364.27 in Germany, $367.33 in France), and it moves with policy, which is why I treat it as an adjustable plug rather than a fixed rate. Third, the no-CVD lanes give the cleanest read on the true price gap: $856–$1,056 built entirely from the $800 base gap compounded by duty and VAT, with the flat block cancelling. Fourth, that flat block is the elephant: $6,600 is 42.7% of the E5's base before taxes, and it is why the cheaper car carries the heavier percentage premium in all seven markets. An importer who negotiates only the sticker and ignores certification, freight consolidation and clearance will leave more money on the table than the entire E5–Atto 3 gap.
