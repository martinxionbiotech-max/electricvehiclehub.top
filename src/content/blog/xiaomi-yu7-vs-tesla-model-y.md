## TL;DR

The [Xiaomi YU7](/vehicles/xiaomi-yu7/) is the strongest Chinese claim on Tesla Model Y territory yet — 96.3 kWh against a ~63 kWh entry pack, 835 km quoted range against 534 km, 4,999 mm against 4,797 mm — and its landed-cost map splits almost exactly opposite to the cheaper challengers. In Germany the YU7 lands at **$63,004.68 (+76.5%)**, roughly **$17,132 above** a Model Y from €39,990 (~$45,873) built in Berlin, outside the countervailing-duty regime. In Thailand the same car lands at **$42,849.00** — **$8,838 below** the Model Y's 1.719-million-baht entry. The YU7 wins the low-tax lanes and loses the EU, and the counterfactual that flips the cheaper Chinese SUVs — Tesla's 7.8% rate — does not flip this one.

## Key statistics

- **YU7 base price:** $35,700 China ex-factory — 96.3 kWh battery, 835 km CLTC, 235 kW / 528 Nm, 4,999 mm long, 2,315 kg
- **Model Y reference prices (verified 22 Sep 2026):** from €39,990 (Germany; carwow cash from €40,970) · €40,990 (France) · £41,990 (UK) · NOK 422,560 (Norway) · A$58,900 (Australia) · AED 174,990 (UAE) · THB 1,719,000 (Thailand)
- **Converted at 22 Sep 2026 rates:** ≈$45,873 · ≈$47,020 · ≈$56,155 · ≈$44,786 · ≈$41,942 · ≈$47,649 · ≈$51,687
- **YU7 landed:** Germany $63,004.68 (+76.5%) · UK $53,374.00 (+49.5%) · Norway $51,875.00 (+45.3%) · Australia $45,120.00 (+26.4%) · UAE $43,809.25 (+22.7%) · Thailand $42,849.00 (+20.0%)
- **Gaps:** from −$17,132 in Germany (YU7 above) to +$8,838 in Thailand (YU7 below)
- **The duty line:** Xiaomi pays the EU's 20.7% "other cooperating producer" countervailing rate — $8,128.89 per car in Germany before VAT compounds over it
- **Origins decide it:** the Model Y sold in Europe is Berlin-built and never crosses a customs border; Tesla's China-built exports pay an individually assessed 7.8%
- **Counterfactual:** at Tesla's 7.8% rate the YU7 would land at ≈$56,976 in Germany — still ≈$11,103 above the Model Y's cheapest German offer (from €39,990). At zero countervailing duty, ≈$53,331 — still above. No rate flips Germany for this car.

## The basis: ex-factory versus landed cost

**Ex-factory** is the China gate price — $35,700 for the [YU7](/vehicles/xiaomi-yu7/). **Landed cost** is what an importer pays to get that car through customs, registered and road-legal in the destination market: duty, countervailing duty where the EU applies it, VAT or GST compounding in that order, plus a fixed block for RoRo freight, clearance, certification, registration and inland transport. It is cash at the border — not a retail price, and not inclusive of importer margin or warranty provisioning. The compounding chain is documented in the [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/); why we compare cost against sticker has [its own explainer](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/).

One asymmetry runs through the tables below: the Model Y column is a **local retail price** — Tesla's taxes, logistics and margin already inside it — while the YU7 column is an import-cost estimate with no margin. That frame answers the question this article is for: how much headroom do I have against the shelf price my customers will compare me to?

Two cautions. ## The tariff wall, in one table

Both cars are China-related for tariff purposes — that sentence does very different work in different jurisdictions:

| Layer | YU7 into Germany | Model Y for Germany |
|---|---|---|
| Standard import duty | 10% | None — Berlin-built; no customs entry |
| Countervailing duty | **20.7%** (other-cooperating rate) | None — outside the measure |
| VAT | 19% compounded | 19% (inside retail price) |

Xiaomi was not individually sampled in the EU's investigation, so it sits at the 20.7% "other cooperating producer" rate under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754 — the same line Hongqi carries ([the rate explainer](/blog/xiaomi-hongqi-countervailing-duty/)). Tesla's European Model Y avoids the regime entirely: Giga Berlin supplies it, and those cars never cross a customs border. Tesla's China-built exports — the Model 3 volume for Europe, Model Y units for several non-EU markets — sit inside the regime at its individually assessed 7.8%.

*Sticker before on-road costs — the least favorable basis for the YU7; drive-away pricing runs higher, which would widen its advantage.

Three reading notes. The YU7 column is an import cost with no margin inside; the Model Y column is a finished retail product with Tesla's margin inside. Premium percentages are over each car's own base. And the pattern inverts the one our [G6 vs Model Y](/blog/xpeng-g6-vs-tesla-model-y/) and [Sealion 7 vs Model Y](/blog/byd-sealion-7-vs-tesla-model-y/) comparisons found: the cheaper Chinese SUVs win the EU-adjacent lanes; the YU7 loses the EU and wins the low-tax lanes.

## Worked example: the YU7 through German customs

| Line (Germany) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $35,700.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $3,570.00 |
| Countervailing duty (20.7%) | $8,128.89 |
| VAT (19%) | $9,005.79 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$63,004.68** |

Formula: `35,700 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 63,004.68`. The order matters — standard duty first, countervailing duty on the duty-paid value, VAT last, then the flat block.

Now the counterfactual, clearly labelled as arithmetic and not a shopping option. Rebuild the German stack with Tesla's 7.8% rate: `35,700 × 1.10 × 1.078 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 56,976.34`, about **$56,976**. The rate swap is worth **$6,028.34 per car** — but the YU7 would *still* land ≈$11,103 above the Model Y's cheapest German offer (from €39,990). Delete the countervailing duty entirely and it lands at **$53,331.30** — still $7,458 above the €39,990 list. That is the finding our other counterfactuals never produced: for the [G6](/blog/xpeng-g6-vs-tesla-model-y/) and the [Sealion 7](/blog/byd-sealion-7-vs-tesla-model-y/), the rate was the whole German problem. For the YU7, no countervailing-duty rate puts it under the Model Y in Germany. The gap is structural — base price, duty, VAT and the fixed block — and the 20.7% line only widens it.

## Why the same car flips from $17,132 behind to $8,838 ahead

- **Inside the EU, the import trails by five figures.** Germany −$17,132 and France −$16,359 are the worst lanes, where the full 10% + 20.7% + VAT stack compounds against a competitor that pays no import line at all. Norway (−$7,089) and Australia (−$3,178) sit in the middle — zero duty, but VAT/GST still lands on a $35,700 base.
- **The cheap lanes are real wins.** Thailand (+$8,838, zero duty + 7% VAT), the UAE (+$3,840, 5% + 5%) and the UK (+$2,781, 10% duty, no CVD) are the only three of the seven where the YU7 lands below Tesla's local entry.
- **The counterfactual flips nothing.** At Tesla's 7.8% rate the YU7 still lands ≈$11,103 above the Model Y in Germany. An importer cannot price around the regulation; the German business case has to come from spec and segment, not arithmetic.
- **Read dollars for decisions, percentages for burden.** The YU7's premium runs +76.5% in Germany and +20.0% in Thailand; the Model Y prices carry Tesla's margin while the landed estimates carry none, so the dollar gaps are the decision tool.

## Where this comparison can go wrong

1. **Cost versus sticker.** The YU7 column is cash-at-the-border; the Model Y column is a retail product with a service network included. The subtraction measures headroom, not value — [sticker vs landed](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/).2. **VAT is not always a cost.** Registered B2B importers usually recover VAT, which cuts the border cash requirement. Duty and countervailing duty never come back; the German ranking survives either way.
3. **Fixed costs are central estimates.** Freight, certification and clearance are volume-sensitive; small orders land above these numbers.
4. **Range cycles are not interchangeable.** 835 km CLTC cannot be subtracted from 534 km WLTP; the [cycle explainer](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/) covers the mechanics.
5. **Trim and market variance.** Model Y trims differ by market and Tesla's prices move — the French entry rose €1,000 in April 2026, and the German carwow cash price now sits €1,000 above where our earlier comparisons quoted it.
6. **Duty regimes are not furniture.** The EU countervailing schedule is under review and legal challenge; stress the deal a few points in either direction before committing.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, the YU7 is the strongest product challenge the Model Y has faced from China — it out-specs the entry Model Y on every line that prints in a brochure — but it is the weakest EU *import* of the three Chinese SUVs we have priced against Tesla. The G6 and the Sealion 7 lose in Germany because of a tariff line; the YU7 loses because of what the car itself costs. Outside the EU the situation reverses cleanly, and in Thailand the YU7 carries the widest advantage of any Chinese SUV in this series.

### Reasoning

First, the counterfactual is the evidence: rebuild the German stack at Tesla's own 7.8% rate and the YU7 still lands ≈$11,103 above the Model Y's entry — and at zero countervailing duty it is still $7,458 above. About $6,028 of the German gap is the rate differential; the remaining ~$11,000 are base price, duty, VAT and fixed costs, which no rate negotiation touches. Second, the geographic flip is too clean to ignore: −$17,132 in Germany to +$8,838 in Thailand on the same car means the market choice, not the product, is the business decision an importer makes first. Third, the spec case is genuinely strong where the price case is not: 33 kWh more battery, 301 km more quoted range, 202 mm more length — against an entry pack that is the ~63 kWh LFP unit, the YU7 sells space and battery in a way the [G6](/blog/xpeng-g6-vs-tesla-model-y/) cannot.Fourth: the Model Y price includes Tesla's service network and the landed cost includes none — whoever imports a YU7 is also building the after-sales answer, and that decides year three, not month one.
