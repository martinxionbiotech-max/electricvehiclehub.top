## TL;DR

The single most overlooked cost in a Chinese EV import is **homologation** — the process of proving a vehicle meets a market's safety and emissions rules. A car built for the Chinese domestic market is *not* automatically road-legal in Europe, and the route you take to make it legal depends almost entirely on **how many vehicles you're bringing in**.

For a one-off or low-volume import, you'll almost certainly use **national single-vehicle approval** (IVA in the UK, NSSTA-style approval in EU states) — typically **$1,500–$5,000 and 2–6 weeks per vehicle**. Whole-vehicle type approval (ECWVTA) is the "proper" route for manufacturers, but it's a type-level process with upfront engineering and testing costs that only make sense at volume. Pick the wrong route and you'll either overpay on a single car or fail compliance on a batch.

## Key statistics

- **Single-vehicle approval (IVA/NSSTA):** $1,500–$5,000 · 2–6 weeks · per vehicle
- **ECWVTA (whole-vehicle type approval):** type-level · engineering + testing · amortizes over volume
- **Small Series:** a middle route for limited production runs
- **The rule of thumb:** below ~10 vehicles, single-vehicle approval; at volume, type approval
- **A Chinese-market EV generally does not satisfy ADRs/EU type-approval out of the box**

## The three routes, in plain terms

**1. ECWVTA — Whole-Vehicle Type Approval.** The full EU route. A manufacturer proves a vehicle *type* meets all applicable EU directives, then every vehicle of that type is approved by default. It's what volume OEMs do. For an importer bringing in a handful of China-market cars, this is usually the wrong tool: the testing and engineering work is front-loaded and expensive.

**2. National single-vehicle approval (IVA / NSSTA).** Each *individual* vehicle is inspected and approved against a national technical standard. In the UK this is Individual Vehicle Approval (IVA); EU member states have equivalents (e.g., national single-vehicle approval in Germany, réception à titre isolé in France). This is the practical route for low-volume imports — it accepts a China-market car that wasn't type-approved for the EU, subject to inspection and any required adaptations.

**3. Small Series.** A limited-quantity type approval between the two — useful for small production runs, but with per-year caps and its own requirements.

## Which route should you use?

The decision is driven by volume and by whether the vehicle already has a compliant homologation:

| Your situation | Route | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 vehicles, China-market spec | Single-vehicle approval | Per-vehicle inspection, no type-level testing |
| Small production run, same model | Small Series | Type-level but volume-capped |
| High volume / manufacturer | ECWVTA | Amortize engineering over thousands of units |

The practical reality for most readers of this guide: **you're importing a handful of China-market cars, and single-vehicle approval is your route.** Budget $1,500–$5,000 and 2–6 weeks *per vehicle* — and expect the inspection to flag adaptations.

## What the inspection usually flags

A China-market EV frequently needs modifications before it passes single-vehicle approval:

- **Lighting** — headlamp beam pattern, rear fog lamp position, and lamp markings often differ from EU requirements.
- **Speedometer** — for the UK, a mph scale; EU markets need km/h (usually fine, but confirm calibration).
- **Rear fog lamp and reflectors** — mandatory in Europe, sometimes absent or non-compliant on China-market cars.
- **Child restraint / seatbelt anchors** — anchor points and markings may not meet EU rules.
- **Charging inlet** — the GB/T port itself (see our [CCS2 vs GB/T guide](/blog/ccs2-vs-gbt-charging/)) may need adaptation for European networks.

Each adaptation adds cost and delay — which is exactly why the $1,500–$5,000 range exists, and why a "cheap" import can quietly get more expensive at the homologation stage.

## The hidden cost nobody quotes

Homologation isn't just a line item — it's a *gate*. You cannot register the car without it, and until it's done, the vehicle is essentially a paperweight. Budget for the cost **and** the 2–6 weeks of lead time, and don't assume the first inspection passes. A realistic plan includes one round of adaptations after the initial inspection.

## What buyers should ask before committing

1. **"Is the vehicle already homologated for my market?"** — if it's not, single-vehicle approval is your default.
2. **"Which national approval applies, and what does it inspect?"** — routes and standards differ by country (IVA in the UK, national single-vehicle approval in Germany, réception à titre isolé in France).
3. **"What adaptations will the inspection flag?"** — lighting, rear fog, speedometer, anchors. Ask before you ship.
4. **"What's the all-in homologation cost and timeline?"** — get the $1,500–$5,000 and 2–6 weeks in writing, plus any adaptation work.
5. **"Does this scale?"** — if you plan to repeat the import, small series or type approval may be cheaper per unit than repeated single-vehicle approvals.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I register a Chinese-market EV in the EU without homologation?**
No. It must pass either whole-vehicle type approval or a national single-vehicle approval first. A China-market car generally does not satisfy EU requirements out of the box.

**How much does single-vehicle approval cost?**
Typically $1,500–$5,000 and 2–6 weeks per vehicle, before any adaptation work the inspection flags.

**When is WVTA worth it?**
At volume. It's a type-level process with upfront engineering and testing that only makes sense when amortized over many vehicles — the route a manufacturer or high-volume importer uses, not a one-off buyer.

**Do I need a different route for the UK?**
Yes — the UK has its own IVA (Individual Vehicle Approval) regime post-Brexit, separate from EU national approvals.
