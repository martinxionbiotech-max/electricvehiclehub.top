## TL;DR

NIO, XPeng, and Leapmotor all pay the EU's **20.7%** countervailing duty — the "other cooperating companies" rate on top of the 10% standard import duty, a **30.7% combined tariff before VAT**. None of the three was individually sampled in the EU's anti-subsidy investigation, so all three inherit the same blanket rate as Chery, Changan, Xiaomi, and Hongqi.

What separates them is not the rate but the *band* each occupies. Leapmotor's $8,440 T03 lands in Germany at **$19,935 (+136%)** — the fixed import stack, not the tariff, does the damage. XPeng's $16,800 Mona M03 lands at **$33,143 (+97%)**, squarely in the mid-market. NIO's $108,170 ET9 lands at **$177,505 (+64%)**, where the 20.7% is the largest absolute-duty bill of the three but the smallest percentage markup.

## Key statistics

- **Countervailing duty for all three:** 20.7% (on top of 10% standard duty = **30.7% combined** before VAT)
- **Rate type:** the "other cooperating companies" line — none of the three was individually sampled
- **BYD:** 17.0% · **Geely/Zeekr:** 18.8% · **SAIC/MG:** 35.3%
- **In force since:** 31 October 2024, for 5 years (EU Implementing Regulation 2024/2754)
- **BEV count:** NIO 10, XPeng 9, Leapmotor 10 — 29 models combined
- **Worked examples:** Leapmotor T03 $8,440 → **$19,935 (+136%)** · NIO ET5 $41,850 → **$72,721 (+74%)**

## Why all three pay the same 20.7% — and why that's the point

The EU's definitive countervailing duty, Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, gave individual rates only to the three individually sampled producers — BYD (17.0%), Geely (18.8%), and SAIC (35.3%) — plus Tesla's Shanghai exports at 7.8%. Every other cooperating Chinese BEV exporter, including NIO, XPeng, and Leapmotor, was assigned the weighted-average **20.7%**.

That means the three "new force" brands, despite very different products, carry an identical tariff line. The regulatory logic is collective, not individual: they cooperated with the investigation, they were not sampled, so they share the default. There is no NIO-specific or XPeng-specific subsidy finding in the regulation — the 20.7% is a category, not a verdict.

The practical consequence is that the tariff tells you almost nothing about which of the three to import. The decision comes down to base price, fixed costs, and positioning:

- **Leapmotor** — the value play, with micro- and compact EVs (T03, A05, A10) and mid-size crossovers (C10, C11, C16)
- **XPeng** — the technology-forward mid-to-premium band (Mona M03, G6, G7, P7, G9, X9)
- **NIO** — the premium band, $41,850 to $108,170 (ET5 through ET9)

## The full tariff stack, worked example: Leapmotor T03 → Germany

Here is the itemized math for a Leapmotor T03 (base $8,440 ex-factory China) imported into Germany, using the same [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/) as the rest of the catalog:

| Cost item | Amount | How it's calculated |
|---|---|---|
| Base price (CIF) | $8,440 | Leapmotor T03, China MSRP |
| Standard import duty | $844 | 10% of CIF |
| Countervailing duty | $1,922 | 20.7% of (CIF + duty) |
| VAT | $2,129 | 19% of (CIF + duty + CVD) |
| RoRo freight | $2,000 | — |
| Customs clearance | $350 | — |
| Certification | $3,250 | Homologation |
| Registration | $500 | — |
| Inland transport | $500 | — |
| **Total landed** | **$19,935** | **+136% over base** |

The countervailing duty ($1,922) is more than double the standard duty ($844), but the $6,600 fixed stack is the dominant line — it is 78% of the base price on its own. On a $8,440 micro-EV, the tariff is almost incidental; the freight, certification, and clearance costs are what make a value car unviable in the EU.

Contrast that with the same tariff on a premium car. The NIO ET5 at $41,850 carries a CVD of $9,529 and a VAT of $10,557, landing at **$72,721 (+74%)**. The 20.7% rate is identical; only the base price changed.

A percentage rate scales with price, so the premium brands pay the largest *dollar* bills even though their *percentage* premium looks smallest.

## Same car, different market: where the 20.7% disappears

The countervailing duty is an EU measure. The same cars land very differently outside the bloc:

| Model | Germany (EU) | UK (Non-EU) | UAE (Middle East) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leapmotor T03 | $19,935 (+136%) | $17,391 (+106%) | $13,755 (+63%) |
| XPeng Mona M03 | $33,143 (+97%) | $28,426 (+69%) | $22,972 (+37%) |
| NIO ET5 | $72,721 (+74%) | $61,492 (+47%) | $50,590 (+21%) |

The EU premium is substantial at every band — roughly $9,800 on the Mona M03 between Germany and the UAE, and $22,000 on the ET5. If your buyer is not in the EU, the 20.7% countervailing duty simply does not apply.

## What buyers should ask before importing a NIO, XPeng, or Leapmotor EV

1. **"Which band am I actually buying in?"** — the same 20.7% hits a $8,440 Leapmotor and a $108,170 NIO very differently. Know the base price before reasoning about the tariff.
2. **"Is this a BEV or a PHEV/EREV?"** — the 20.7% applies to BEVs only. Leapmotor and XPeng both sell EREV variants that skip the CVD.
3. **"What's the absolute duty bill, not the rate?"** — 20.7% on a NIO ET9 is ~$24,600. Always ask for the dollar figure.
4. **"Am I registering in the EU?"** — if not, the 20.7% may not apply. Get the landed cost for your actual destination.
5. **"Does the brand's rate differ from BYD's?"** — NIO, XPeng, and Leapmotor all pay 20.7%, 3.7 points above BYD's 17%. The gap is real but smaller than base-price differences between specific models.

## The Author's Take

**Position:** In my view, lumping NIO, XPeng, and Leapmotor under one 20.7% line is tariff convenience, not tariff fairness — and the import decision should be made on band economics, not on a rate that all three share anyway.

**Reasoning:** First, the 20.7% is the "other cooperating companies" default, so it carries zero signal about any one brand's subsidy margin; comparing NIO to XPeng by tariff is meaningless because they are identical on paper. Second, the binding constraint differs by band — Leapmotor's value EVs die on the $6,600 fixed stack long before the tariff matters, while NIO's premium cars absorb the same fixed cost and turn the 20.7% into the largest single dollar line, so the "right" import is a positioning decision, not a tariff-arbitrage decision. Third, the EREV loophole is the quiet wildcard: Leapmotor and XPeng both sell range-extended variants that currently avoid the CVD entirely, and any buyer ignoring powertrain when pricing the tariff is leaving money on the table.

**Disclosure:** This is my interpretation of published EU tariff law and the EV Hub landed-cost records, not legal or tax advice. Countervailing-duty rates and covered exporters are set by EU regulation and can change; verify the current schedule with a customs broker before pricing anything.
