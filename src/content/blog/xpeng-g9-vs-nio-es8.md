## TL;DR

The [XPeng G9](/vehicles/xpeng-g9/) and the [NIO ES8](/vehicles/nio-es8/) are two flagship SUVs from the same export wave, and they enter Europe through the same tariff door: neither was individually sampled in the EU's anti-subsidy investigation, so both carry the **20.7% "other cooperating companies" countervailing duty** on top of the standard 10% import duty — identical layers, identical rate. The difference sits in the price list. **The G9 leaves China at $43,162; the ES8 at $57,130 — a $13,968 head start for the XPeng that the tariff stack compounds rather than erases.** In Germany they land at **$74,794.37 vs $96,863.29 — a $22,068.92 gap.** Across the seven markets below, and in all 23 others in the dataset, the ES8 never closes it: the gap runs from $14,666.40 in Qatar to $24,304.32 in Mexico. The G9 wins the customs ledger in every market modelled. The ES8 answers with 520 kW, 102 kWh, a 5,280 mm body and 3.97 seconds to 100 km/h — because this is a product fight, not a tariff fight.

## Key statistics

- **China ex-factory:** G9 $43,162 · ES8 $57,130 — the ES8 starts 32.4% higher; the gate gap is $13,968
- **The rate:** both carry the 20.7% "other cooperating" CVD + 10% standard duty = 30.7% combined before VAT; neither NIO nor XPeng has an individually sampled rate ([rate tiers explained](/blog/nio-xpeng-leapmotor-countervailing-duty/), [full master table](/blog/china-ev-brand-cvd-master-table/))
- **Germany landed:** G9 $74,794.37 (+73.3%) · ES8 $96,863.29 (+69.5%) — gap $22,068.92
- **30-market gap range:** $14,666.40 (Qatar) to $24,304.32 (Mexico) — the ES8 lands above the G9 in every market, by between 28% and 31% of the G9's landed cost
- **Spec anchors:** G9 — 79 kWh, 625 km CLTC, 285 kW / 465 Nm, 6.6 s, 2,196 kg · ES8 — 102 kWh, 635 km CLTC, 520 kW / 700 Nm, 3.97 s, 2,630 kg
- **Efficiency, as quoted:** G9 13.9 kWh/100 km · ES8 16.6 kWh/100 km
- **Fast charge, as quoted:** G9 10 min (10–80%) · ES8 not published in the record — UNKNOWN
- **European badge:** the ES8's European branding is not recorded in this dataset; an EL8 designation could not be verified — treat it as UNKNOWN (see naming note below)

## The spec duel: a class apart

Read the spec sheets before the price lists, because these two flagship SUVs are not the same size of car:

| Specification | XPeng G9 | NIO ES8 |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning, per records | mid-size SUV | full-size SUV |
| Length × width × height | 4,891 × 1,937 × 1,670 mm | 5,280 × 2,010 × 1,800 mm |
| Wheelbase | 2,998 mm | 3,130 mm |
| Curb weight | 2,196 kg | 2,630 kg |
| Battery | 79 kWh | 102 kWh |
| Range, as quoted | 625 km CLTC | 635 km CLTC |
| Motor | 285 kW / 465 Nm | 520 kW / 700 Nm |
| 0–100 km/h | 6.6 s | 3.97 s |
| Top speed | 200 km/h | 220 km/h |
| Efficiency, as quoted | 13.9 kWh/100 km | 16.6 kWh/100 km |
| Fast charge, as quoted | 10 min (10–80%) | — (not published; UNKNOWN) |

The pattern in one line: the ES8 is a class above in hardware — 389 mm longer, 434 kg heavier, 23 kWh more battery, 235 kW more motor — and yet quotes only 10 km more CLTC range. That is the efficiency trade priced in: 13.9 vs 16.6 kWh/100 km quoted. Two caveats before those numbers travel. CLTC is not a real-world unit and cannot be lined up against WLTP ([why](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/)). And the ES8's record lists no DC fast-charge time at all, so fleet planning against a charging figure should wait for verified data — we do not fill spec gaps with guesses.

Naming note for importers: NIO's second-generation ES6 ships in Europe as the EL6, as recorded in its own [comparison article](/blog/nio-es6-vs-tesla-model-y/). For the ES8, no European badge is recorded in this dataset — an EL8 designation could not be verified either, so treat the European name as UNKNOWN and confirm it against EU homologation documents before it goes on a dealer quote.

## The tariff wall, in one table

| Layer | G9 into Germany | ES8 into Germany |
|---|---|---|
| Standard import duty | 10% | 10% |
| Countervailing duty | **20.7%** (other cooperating) | **20.7%** (same tier) |
| VAT | 19% compounded | 19% compounded |

This is the article's central symmetry: the rate stack does not distinguish these two cars. Neither NIO nor XPeng was sampled in the EU investigation, so both inherit the weighted-average "other cooperating companies" line — the same rate Chery, Changan, Xiaomi and Hongqi carry ([rate tiers](/blog/china-ev-brand-cvd-master-table/)). The tariff is a category, not a verdict on either vehicle.

Because the stack is identical, it cannot be the *cause* of any difference between them — it is an amplifier. Where the two diverge is the base price, and the stack multiplies that divergence by the same factor in every market: **gap = $13,968 × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT)**. Outside the EU the CVD is zero for both (it applies to BEVs in the EU only), and within each market the fixed-cost block is identical for the two cars — $6,600 in Germany — so it cancels in the gap. In Germany the tax multiplier is 1.579963, which is why the $13,968 gate gap lands as $22,068.92. The identity holds across all 30 markets in the dataset.

The countervailing layer exists only inside the EU; the UK, Norway, the Gulf, Australia and Thailand stacks stop at standard duty and VAT/GST. Both columns are landed estimates from the same model — no importer margin, no warranty provisioning inside either number — so the gap column is like-for-like: same fixed block, same tax logic, same no-sticker caveat. The percentages in parentheses are each car's premium over its own base price, and they read lower for the ES8 purely because a fixed cost block sits on a larger base. [Sticker vs landed](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/) is the two-minute explainer on why those columns are not shelf prices. All figures are USD as recorded in the dataset; no currency conversion is applied in this article.

## Where the G9's price advantage survives tariffs

- **Everywhere — it cannot flip.** Both cars traverse each market through the same rate structure, so the landed gap is bound to the gate gap times the market multiplier. Across the 30 markets recorded, the ES8 lands above the G9 in every one; no tax schedule in the dataset reverses the ranking.
- **The EU is where it is widest in dollars.** The 10/20.7/19 compound stack stretches the $13,968 gate gap to between $22,068.92 (Germany) and $23,181.65 (Sweden, Denmark) across the 12 EU markets modelled — the heaviest taxes produce the largest absolute advantage.
- **The Gulf and ASEAN are where it is narrowest.** Qatar $14,666.40, Thailand $14,945.76, Australia $15,364.80 — light tax stacks leave the gap close to the raw price difference. A smaller gap in a cheaper market is still a real edge; it is just worth fewer dollars per unit.
- **Mexico is the dataset ceiling.** $24,304.32 — the widest ES8-above-G9 gap anywhere in the data, because Mexico's 50% duty multiplies the base gap by half. The full span across 30 markets is $9,637.92.
- **The percentage trap.** The ES8's premium over its own base reads *milder* than the G9's — 69.5% vs 73.3% in Germany, 12.3% vs 14.7% in Qatar — yet the ES8's German tax lines total $33,133.29 against the G9's $25,032.37, or $8,100.92 more. Rank these two by percentage premium and you rank them backwards from the dollars.

## Worked example: one formula, two answers

The dataset's landed-cost model compounds the taxes and adds a fixed block — `landed = base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs` — documented in the [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/). Through German customs, one expression takes two inputs and returns two answers:

| Line (Germany) | XPeng G9 | NIO ES8 |
|---|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $43,162.00 | $57,130.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $4,316.20 | $5,713.00 |
| Countervailing duty (20.7%) | $9,827.99 | $13,008.50 |
| VAT (19%) | $10,888.18 | $14,411.79 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$74,794.37** | **$96,863.29** |

`43,162 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 ≈ 74,794.37`

`57,130 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 ≈ 96,863.29`

The countervailing-duty line is the largest single tax on both cars — $9,827.99 for the G9, $13,008.50 for the ES8 — and it is also the one line the two share as a rate. Restated: the entire German outcome of this duel is the $13,968 gate gap stretched by 1.579963, and nothing else.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, this is the cleanest duel in the dataset: two flagship SUVs in the same tariff class, separated by a price list, not a policy. I would quote the G9 to any importer who needs the cheaper landed dollar — it wins every market recorded, by $14,666 to $24,304 — and I would not let the ES8's higher bill be reported as a tariff problem, because at 20.7% its rate is identical to the G9's. The ES8 sells a class above and prices a class above; on customs arithmetic the verdict is unambiguous, and what remains is a product decision, not a border one.

### Reasoning

First, the identical rate stack means the tariff cannot explain the ES8's position — both pay the same 30.7% before VAT, so the entire difference traces to the $13,968 gate gap multiplied by each market's tax schedule. Second, the percentage view is a trap worth naming: NIO's +69.5% German premium reads milder than XPeng's +73.3%, yet the ES8's tax lines add $33,133.29 against the G9's $25,032.37 — $8,100.92 more — because percentages shrink against a bigger base. Third, the product counterweight is real and I would not dismiss it: 102 kWh, 520 kW / 700 Nm, 3.97 s, 5,280 mm. A buyer cross-shopping these two is choosing between a cheaper landed mid-size and a dearer full-size, and NIO's China price — 32.4% above the G9's — is itself the statement that comparisons here are about class, not about tariff luck. Fourth, a caveat against my own tables: landed cost is not retail, and neither record carries a European retail price, so nothing here measures dealer margin or a consumer's out-the-door number — margin math needs local benchmarks this dataset does not hold, and [the sticker explainer](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/) is where that conversation starts.
