## TL;DR

The [BYD Dolphin](/vehicles/byd-dolphin/) lands cheaper than the [XPeng Mona M03](/vehicles/xpeng-mona-m03/) in all seven markets we priced — **$28,072.05 against $33,143.38 in Germany, a $5,071.33 gap** — yet it carries the higher percentage premium in every one of them, because a **$6,600 fixed import block is 47.1% of its base price against 39.3%** for the Mona M03. In Germany the 3.7-point countervailing-duty spread (20.7% vs 17.0%) is worth **$813.67 — 16% of the gap**; the other 84% was decided at the factory gate, which is the number to watch in this segment.

## Key statistics

- **Base prices (China ex-factory):** Mona M03 $16,800 · Dolphin $14,020 — $2,780 apart
- **Countervailing duty lines:** XPeng **20.7%** (the "other cooperating" default — XPeng was not individually sampled) · BYD **17.0%** (individually assessed); both on top of the 10% standard EU duty, BEV-only and EU-only
- **Germany landed:** Mona M03 $33,143.38 (+97.3%) · Dolphin $28,072.05 (+100.2%) → Dolphin cheaper by **$5,071.33**
- **German gap, decomposed:** $2,780.00 base + $1,477.65 compounding + $813.67 rate differential = $5,071.33; the rate accounts for **16.0%**
- **Seven markets:** the Dolphin wins every row, by **$2,974.60 (Thailand) to $5,327.02 (Denmark)** — and pays the higher premium in all seven, by 2.9 to 8.3 points
- **The fixed block:** $6,600 (freight, clearance, certification, registration, inland) = **39.3% of the Mona M03's base, 47.1% of the Dolphin's**; Denmark's $3,500 registration fee lifts it to $9,600 (57.1% / 68.5%)
- **Specs:** Mona M03 51.8 kWh / 515 km CLTC / 140 kW · Dolphin 45.12 kWh / 418 km CLTC / 70 kW
- **Venue beats model:** the same Mona M03 spans **$22,626 (Thailand) to $37,481.70 (Denmark)** — a $14,855.70 swing, nearly three times the widest car-to-car gap in this article
- **Cross-check:** both cars land higher in Denmark than behind Mexico's 50% tariff wall ($37,481.70 vs $36,282.00; $32,154.68 vs $31,444.80)

## Ex-factory vs landed cost: the two prices this article compares

**Ex-factory price** is what the car costs at the Chinese factory gate — EV Hub's catalog lists the Mona M03 at $16,800 and the Dolphin at $14,020 as China ex-factory MSRPs. It is the number on a quotation, not the number on the final invoice when the car arrives at a port.

**Landed cost** is what one unit actually costs delivered through a destination's border and registered: the ex-factory price compounded through standard duty, countervailing duty and VAT — in that order, because that is how customs valuations cascade — plus a fixed block of freight, clearance, certification, registration and inland transport. In formula:

`landed = base × (1 + standard duty) × (1 + countervailing duty) × (1 + VAT) + fixed block`

Every figure in this article comes from EV Hub's catalog under the same documented method; the [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/) page carries the formula, the fixed-cost benchmarks and the assumptions.

| Battery | 51.8 kWh | 45.12 kWh |
| Range, as quoted | 515 km CLTC | 418 km CLTC |
| Motor | 140 kW / 225 Nm | 70 kW / 180 Nm |
| Length × width × height | 4,780 × 1,896 × 1,445 mm | 4,280 × 1,770 × 1,570 mm |
| Wheelbase | 2,815 mm | 2,700 mm |
| Curb weight | 1,661 kg | 1,410 kg |
| Claimed efficiency | 11.5 kWh/100km | 11.2 kWh/100km |
| Fast-charge claim | 25 min (30–80%) | 25 min (30–80%) |
| EU countervailing duty | 20.7% | 17.0% |

Three notes against a lazy reading of that table. First, a label flag: our catalog files the Mona M03 in its SUV grouping, but its recorded dimensions — 4,780 mm long, just 1,445 mm tall — describe a long, low fastback silhouette, not a tall crossover; the Dolphin's 4,280 mm by 1,570 mm are a two-box hatch. The landed-cost model never reads the label, and neither should you. Second, the ranges are both CLTC — a Chinese cycle that flatters, never to be subtracted from a WLTP figure — and the [cycle explainer](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/) is the short honest read on why. Third, the motor row is the real divide: 140 kW against 70 kW is a different driving proposition, not a trim detail, and the Mona M03 weighs 251 kg more for it.

## Two duty lines: 20.7% vs 17.0%

The two rates at the center of this comparison are not brand verdicts — they are a sampling artifact of EU law.

| Layer | Mona M03 (XPeng) | Dolphin (BYD) |
|---|---|---|
| Standard EU import duty | 10% | 10% |
| Countervailing duty | **20.7%** — "other cooperating" default | **17.0%** — individually assessed |
| Combined, pre-VAT | 30.7% | 27.0% |
| Where it applies | EU only, BEV only | EU only, BEV only |

The EU's definitive countervailing duty, Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, gave individual rates to the producers it sampled — BYD at 17.0% among them, the lowest in the sampled Chinese producer table — and assigned the weighted-average **20.7%** to every other cooperating exporter it did not individually sample. That is the line XPeng shares with NIO, Leapmotor, Chery, Changan, Xiaomi and Hongqi, as our [NIO/XPeng/Leapmotor write-up](/blog/nio-xpeng-leapmotor-countervailing-duty/) documents; there is no XPeng-specific subsidy finding in the regulation. BYD's [17.0%](/blog/byd-countervailing-duty-landed-cost/) is its own assessed rate.

Two consequences for anyone pricing either car. The gap between the two lines is 3.7 percentage points — real, but at the bottom of the price list a rate is only as expensive as the base it lands on. And neither line exists outside the EU: the UK, the Gulf and Australia levy no countervailing duty on either car, so in those markets the comparison reduces to two bases and a light stack. (If the CVD mechanics are new to you, the short version is in our [EU import guide](/blog/import-chinese-ev-eu-guide/).)

## The seven-market matrix

All landed figures are EV Hub model estimates from the two vehicle records; each car's premium over its ex-factory base is shown in parentheses.

| Market | Mona M03 landed | Dolphin landed | Dolphin cheaper by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Denmark | $37,481.70 (+123.1%) | $32,154.68 (+129.3%) | $5,327.02 |
| Germany | $33,143.38 (+97.3%) | $28,072.05 (+100.2%) | $5,071.33 |
| Mexico | $36,282.00 (+116.0%) | $31,444.80 (+124.3%) | $4,837.20 |
| United Kingdom | $28,426.00 (+69.2%) | $24,756.40 (+76.6%) | $3,669.60 |
| United Arab Emirates | $22,972.00 (+36.7%) | $19,907.05 (+42.0%) | $3,064.95 |
| Australia | $24,330.00 (+44.8%) | $21,272.00 (+51.7%) | $3,058.00 |
| Thailand | $22,626.00 (+34.7%) | $19,651.40 (+40.2%) | $2,974.60 |

Read the last column first: the Dolphin wins every row, by $2,974.60 to $5,327.02. Then read the parentheses: the Dolphin also pays the steeper premium in every row — the cheaper car is the more expensive import in proportional terms, in all seven markets.

The structure underneath is worth seeing, because it isolates where the rate story lives. The same fixed block applies to both cars within each market, so it cancels out of the gap — and in the five non-EU rows the entire gap is exactly the $2,780 base difference compounded by the local tax stack, with no rate term at all: Thailand, ×1.07 → $2,974.60; Australia, ×1.10 → $3,058.00; the UAE, ×1.1025 → $3,064.95; the UK, ×1.32 → $3,669.60; Mexico, ×1.74 → $4,837.20. Only the two EU rows add a rate differential, because only inside the EU do these two cars pay different rates: **$813.67 in Germany and $854.70 in Denmark** — in both cases 16% of the gap.

Remember what these figures are: landed import estimates — no importer margin, no warranty provisioning, no dealer spread. The [sticker problem](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/) applies with full force in both directions.

## Worked example: both cars into Germany

| Line | Mona M03 | Dolphin |
|---|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $16,800.00 | $14,020.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $1,680.00 | $1,402.00 |
| Countervailing duty (20.7% / 17.0%) | $3,825.36 | $2,621.74 |
| VAT (19%) | $4,238.02 | $3,428.31 |
| Fixed block (freight $2,000 + clearance $350 + certification $3,250 + registration $500 + inland $500) | $6,600.00 | $6,600.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$33,143.38** | **$28,072.05** |
| Premium over base | +97.3% | +100.2% |

The formulas, so you can reproduce both columns: `16,800 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 33,143.38` and `14,020 × 1.10 × 1.17 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 28,072.05`. Duty first, countervailing duty on the duty-paid value, VAT over everything above it, then the flat block — order matters, and adding rates instead of compounding them is the most expensive misunderstanding in EV importing.

Now decompose the $5,071.33 gap, because where it lives decides what an importer can do about it:

| German gap component | Amount | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Base-price difference | $2,780.00 | 54.8% |
| That difference compounding through duty, countervailing duty and VAT | $1,477.65 | 29.1% |
| Countervailing-rate differential (20.7% vs 17.0%) | $813.67 | 16.0% |
| **Total** | **$5,071.33** | **100.0%*** |

*Components rounded to the nearest cent; the total is taken from the vehicle records.

The countervailing-duty line on the Mona M03 ($3,825.36) is $1,203.62 larger than the Dolphin's ($2,621.74) — yet even that full pre-VAT difference is smaller than the base-price difference it accompanies. For an EU buyer the moral is narrow and clear: at these bases, the tariff rate is the secondary variable; the factory gate is the primary one. At the USD/EUR 0.8732 reference rate cited in Sources, the German totals read approximately €28,941 (Mona M03) and €24,513 (Dolphin) — conversions illustrative, since the catalog prices these cars in USD.

## The fixed-cost paradox: why the cheaper car pays the steeper premium

Here is the counterintuitive core of this pair. In all seven markets the Dolphin is the cheaper car to buy and the cheaper car to land — and in all seven it is the more expensive import in percentage terms, because the fixed block refuses to shrink with the sticker:

| Fixed-cost share | Mona M03 ($16,800 base) | Dolphin ($14,020 base) |
|---|---|---|
| $6,600 block (Germany) | 39.3% of base | 47.1% of base |
| $3,250 certification line alone | 19.3% of base | 23.2% of base |
| $9,600 block (Denmark) | 57.1% of base | 68.5% of base |

That table is the paradox in three rows. Freight, clearance, certification, registration and inland transport cost the same to move a $14,020 car as a $16,800 one — so the cheaper the car, the larger the share of its price that is logistics and paperwork, and the steeper the landed premium. It is the same force that pushes a $5,000 Wuling microcar to a +209% German premium: at the bottom of the price list, [the fixed stack, not the tariff, is the binding constraint](/blog/wuling-leapmotor-microcar-import/). These two cars sit in the middle band of that curve — cheap enough that the premium stays above most premium cars, dear enough that it stays under microcar territory. The same logic runs through the [MG4 duel](/blog/mg-4-vs-byd-dolphin/) and the [MG budget-EV paradox](/blog/mg-budget-ev-tariff-paradox/); the [Onvo L60 comparison](/blog/onvo-l60-vs-tesla-model-y/) is where the fixed-cost framework was first applied end to end.

Denmark shows the lever at maximum: add the country's $3,500 registration fee and the fixed block becomes $9,600 — 68.5% of the Dolphin's base — and both premiums jump about 26 to 29 points against Germany. Both cars then land higher in Denmark than behind Mexico's 50% tariff wall: $37,481.70 vs $36,282.00 for the Mona M03, $32,154.68 vs $31,444.80 for the Dolphin. A registration desk, not a tariff, took the top spot on the cost table.

And here is the part that should settle the rate question for good: the 3.7-point spread between these two cars is worth $813.67 in Germany and $854.70 in Denmark — 16% of each gap — because dollar terms scale with the base. The same 20.7% line is a roughly $24,600 item on a $108,170 NIO ET9, as the [NIO/XPeng/Leapmotor breakdown](/blog/nio-xpeng-leapmotor-countervailing-duty/) shows: rate differentials are a premium-car problem. At $14,000–$17,000, the countervailing-duty differential is a sub-$1,000 item and the $6,600 fixed block — identical for both cars — is the single largest cost on either invoice after the car itself. Rank by landed cost, budget the block, and treat the rate as the tiebreaker it has become.

One more pass before the verdict, because it cuts against the obvious reading. Price the battery rather than the car and the two converge: in Germany the Dolphin lands at $622.16 per kWh against the Mona M03's $639.83 — but in Australia the Mona M03 is actually the cheaper battery at $469.69 against $471.45, and in the UK the two tie within ten cents ($548.76 vs $548.68). On quoted range per dollar the Mona M03 wins in all seven markets (15.54 km per $1,000 landed in Germany against 14.89). The Dolphin is cheaper because it is a smaller car — 45.12 kWh and 70 kW against 51.8 kWh and 140 kW — not because the Mona M03 is overpriced. That distinction matters the moment a buyer actually needs the range.

## What importers should check before committing

1. **Rate assignment and review risk.** XPeng's 20.7% is a category default, not a finding; the EU regime carries reviews and legal challenges, and rates can move. Stress-test the deal at plus or minus a few points — at these bases a 3.7-point swing is roughly $800–$900 per car, not a deal-breaker, but enough to erase a thin margin.
2. **Variant discipline.** Both cars ship in multiple battery and trim configurations, and China-market specs do not always map to export variants. Confirm the exact variant — and that it is a BEV, since the countervailing duty is BEV-only — before applying any figure here.
3. **Certification route and batch size.** The $3,250 certification line is the largest fixed item and does not scale down with the car; the [approval-route explainer](/blog/wvta-vs-single-vehicle-approval/) covers single-vehicle versus type approval. Volume is the only lever that reduces the fixed share of either car's landed cost — freight and certification are volume-sensitive; registration is not.
4. **Destination before model.** Both cars are cheapest to land in Thailand ($22,626.00 / $19,651.40) and the UAE, and dearest in Denmark; the EU lane carries the only rate difference. Pick the lane before the car — the venue swing on the Mona M03 alone is $14,855.70.
5. **Margin and service reality.** Every figure here excludes importer margin, warranty provisioning and dealer spread; benchmark against local street pricing, and price the service network in your market, because for value EVs it is often the deciding ownership cost. The [margin math](/blog/fleet-buyer-margin-math/) is the companion piece.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, the Mona M03 vs Dolphin duel is the cleanest proof yet that at the bottom of the price list the countervailing-duty line is a footnote and the fixed block is the headline: the Dolphin wins the landing table in all seven markets and pays the steeper premium in all seven, while the 3.7-point rate spread between the two cars turns out to be worth under $900 anywhere we priced. If I were advising a budget importer, I would say: budget the $6,600 block and the factory gate first, treat the rate as a tiebreaker, and only then decide whether the Mona M03's $2,974.60–$5,327.02 landed premium buys the range and power your buyer actually needs.

### Reasoning

First, the arithmetic is unambiguous and it is not the tariff's story: $813.67 of a $5,071.33 German gap is the rate differential while $4,257.65 is base price and its compounding — 84% of the decision made in China. Second, the percentage table inverts the ranking a margin owner cares about: the Dolphin is the better buy and the worse import in proportional terms (+129.3% vs +123.1% in Denmark), because a flat $9,600 block against a $14,020 base is 68.5% of the car — and the fixed block, not the rate, is the binding constraint at these bases. Third, the venue matters more than either car: $14,855.70 between the Mona M03's cheapest and dearest market, nearly three times the widest car-to-car gap, which makes destination selection the first-order lever and model selection the second. Fourth, a caution against my own headline: on quoted range per dollar the Mona M03 beats the Dolphin in all seven markets, and per kWh the two converge to near-parity in light-tax lanes — the $2,974.60–$5,327.02 premium is mostly a bigger car, not a tax penalty. If the buyer's use case is city-only, the Dolphin is the rational unit; if it stretches to intercity work, price the Mona M03 spec before assuming the cheaper landing number is the cheaper fleet.
