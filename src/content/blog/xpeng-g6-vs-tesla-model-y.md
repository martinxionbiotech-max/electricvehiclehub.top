## TL;DR

The [XPeng G6](/vehicles/xpeng-g6/) was built to hunt the Tesla Model Y — same segment, more range on paper, faster charging — and on landed cost it wins in two of the three markets we priced, losing in the one that matters most. **In Germany the G6 lands at $45,830.48 (+84.6%), roughly $1,120 above a Model Y that starts at €38,970 (~$44,710)** — because the G6 pays a 20.7% countervailing duty while the Model Y sold in Europe comes out of Berlin, outside the CVD regime entirely. In the UK and Australia the same car lands **$17,111** and **$8,715** below the Model Y's local price. The tariff map decides this matchup, and it decides it by geography.

## Key statistics

- **G6 base price:** $24,830 China ex-factory MSRP — 68.5 kWh, 625 km CLTC, 218 kW, 4,758 mm long
- **Model Y reference prices:** from €38,970 (Germany; list €39,990) · £41,990 (UK) · A$58,900 before on-road costs (Australia) → ≈$44,710 · ≈$56,136 · ≈$41,878 converted at 17 Sep 2026 rates
- **G6 landed:** Germany $45,830.48 (+84.6%) · UK $39,025.60 (+57.2%) · Australia $33,163.00 (+33.6%)
- **Gaps:** Germany **+$1,120** (G6 above) · UK **−$17,111** · Australia **−$8,715**
- **The duty line:** 10% standard duty + 20.7% countervailing duty + 19% VAT in Germany; the CVD alone is **$5,653.79** per car
- **Origins matter:** the Model Y for Europe is Berlin-built — no import duty, no CVD; Tesla's individual China rate (7.8%) applies to its China-built exports, not to European Model Y supply
- **Muted spec note:** Model Y battery capacity and motor output are not published for the Standard in the sources we verified — marked "—" below, not guessed

## The tariff wall, in one table

| Layer | XPeng G6 into Germany | Tesla Model Y for Germany |
|---|---|---|
| Standard import duty | 10% | None — Berlin-built; no customs entry |
| Countervailing duty | **20.7%** (other-cooperating rate) | None — outside the CVD regime |
| VAT | 19% | 19% (inside retail price) |

When Tesla does export China-built cars — the Model 3 volume for Europe, and Model Y for some other markets — they sit inside the same regime, just at Tesla's individually assessed **7.8%** rate. That number never applies to the Berlin-built Y, and it is worth saying plainly: Tesla did not dodge the tariff; its European supply simply doesn't cross a tariff border. The G6 pays the rate the regulation assigns to XPeng as an "other cooperating" producer.

## The landed-cost matrix

| Market | G6 landed | Model Y local price | Converted (17 Sep 2026) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | $45,830.48 (+84.6%) | from €38,970 | ≈ $44,710 | **+$1,120** (G6 above) |
| United Kingdom | $39,025.60 (+57.2%) | £41,990 | ≈ $56,136 | **−$17,111** |
| Australia | $33,163.00 (+33.6%) | A$58,900* | ≈ $41,878 | **−$8,715** |

*Sticker before on-road costs — the least favorable basis for the G6; CarExpert's Sydney drive-away display runs higher still, which would widen its advantage.

Remember what each column is. The G6 side is a landed import cost — no importer margin, no warranty provisioning. The Model Y side is a retail price that contains Tesla's entire cost structure and margin. In Germany the gap between "I can land this" and "what the competitor charges for a finished product" is approximately zero. Read that sentence again, because it is the whole article.

## Worked example: G6 → Germany, line by line

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory MSRP) | $24,830.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $2,483.00 |
| Countervailing duty (20.7%) | $5,653.79 |
| VAT (19%) | $6,263.69 |
| Fixed costs (freight, clearance, certification, registration, inland) | $6,600.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$45,830.48** |

Formula: `24,830 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 45,830.48`. The countervailing-duty line alone — **$5,653.79** — is larger than the car's entire fixed-cost block, and more than twice the standard duty.

One illustrative counterfactual, clearly labelled as arithmetic and not a shopping option: if the same car carried Tesla's 7.8% rate instead of 20.7%, it would land at approximately **$41,638** — some $3,073 *below* the Model Y's converted entry price. Rates are assigned, not chosen. The exercise exists to locate where the German problem lives: in the regulation, not the vehicle.

## Why the same car flips from $17,111 ahead to $1,120 behind

- **The EU customs zone** is where the G6 pays its 10% + 20.7% stack, and where its competitor is exempt by virtue of building inside the zone. That is the whole German story.
- **The UK** applies no countervailing duty — the G6 pays the standard 10% duty and 20% VAT, ordinary treatment — and the £41,990 Model Y entry price leaves room the German market does not. The G6 lands $17,111 below it.
- **Australia** is lighter still: zero duty in the China lane and 10% GST. The G6 lands at $33,163 against a Model Y sticker from A$58,900, and the gap — $8,715 — is the widest margin of the three for an importer to work with.

Same vehicle. Same documents. The ranking changes because three jurisdictions tax the same transaction differently — and because Tesla's supply geography is not uniform across them.

## Where the G6 still has to prove itself

Beyond the arithmetic, three things buyers should carry into a G6 decision, because the case is stronger than the car has yet demonstrated in every market:

1. **Network breadth.** Tesla's service network is one of its most under-discussed advantages; XPeng's European coverage is younger and thinner. Verify dealer and parts coverage in your specific market before promising fleet uptime.
2. **Margin geography.** In Germany, a landed cost *above* the competitor's retail entry means importer margin must come from the spec story, not from price. In the UK and Australia there is genuine headroom to defend.
3. **Policy risk both ways.** The EU's countervailing regime carries reviews and challenges; rates assigned today can move. Build the deal so it survives a rate change, and re-check quarterly. The full stack, if you are new to it, is in [our EU import guide](/blog/import-chinese-ev-eu-guide/).

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view, the G6 is the stronger product and the weaker European import: at $45,830 landed in Germany it sits about $1,120 above the Model Y's $44,710 entry price before a dollar of importer margin, while in the UK and Australia the same tariff arithmetic flips to a $17,111 and $8,715 advantage. Whoever sells a G6 in the EU is selling against a policy, not only against a car — and on price, the policy tends to win.

**Reasoning.** First, the number that decides Germany is regulatory, not engineering: a $5,653.79 countervailing-duty line attaches to every G6 while the competing product is manufactured inside the customs zone it sells into. No amount of product quality out-runs a six-figure-per-container cost difference. Second, the flip is too clean to be chance — same car, same freight, and the advantage swings from −$1,120 to +$17,111 purely by leaving the EU's customs zone, which tells an importer exactly which markets to prioritize. Third, the spec gap is real: more battery (68.5 kWh against an unpublished-but-smaller Standard pack), a longer quoted range, a faster charging claim, in a package whose wheelbase matches the Model Y's exactly — the reason I would not ignore the EU entirely is that product stories sell above price in the premium trims this car competes in.
