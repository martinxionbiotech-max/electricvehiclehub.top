## TL;DR

This is the rare BYD–Tesla comparison that needs no segment apology: the [BYD Sealion 7](/vehicles/byd-sealion-07/) and the Tesla Model Y are both mid-size SUVs within 33 mm of each other in length, aimed at the same forecourts. The landed-cost split runs from **$2,811 behind in Germany** — the Sealion 7 lands at **$47,538** against a Model Y from €38,970 (~$44,727) — to **$18,284 ahead in Thailand**. Between those poles sits the policy line: inside the EU, BYD pays a 10% duty plus a 17% countervailing duty plus VAT, while the Model Y Europe buys is Berlin-built and never crosses a customs border; outside the EU, no countervailing duty exists for anyone.

## Key statistics

- **Sealion 7 base price:** $26,730 China ex-factory — 71.8 kWh battery, 550 km CLTC, 170 kW, 4,830 mm long, 2,155 kg
- **Model Y reference prices:** €38,970 (Germany; list €39,990) · €40,990 (France) · £41,990 (UK) · NOK 422,560 (Norway) · A$58,900 before on-road costs (Australia) · AED 174,990 (UAE) · THB 1,719,000 (Thailand)
- **Converted at 20 Sep 2026 rates:** ≈$44,727 · ≈$47,045 · ≈$56,141 · ≈$44,855 · ≈$41,924 · ≈$47,649 · ≈$51,535
- **Sealion 7 landed:** Germany $47,538 (+77.8%) · UK $41,534 (+55.4%) · UAE $33,920 (+26.9%) · Thailand $33,251 (+24.4%)
- **Gap across the seven markets modelled:** −$2,811 (Germany, Sealion above) to +$18,284 (Thailand, Sealion below)
- **The duty line:** BYD's 17% countervailing duty is $4,998.51 per car in Germany before VAT compounds over it; the policy's total cost is $5,948 per car once it does
- **Origins decide it:** the Model Y sold in Europe is Berlin-built and sits outside the countervailing-duty measure; Tesla's China-built exports pay an individually assessed 7.8%; non-EU markets levy no countervailing duty on any brand
- **Counterfactual:** strip the countervailing duty from the Sealion 7's German stack and it lands at ≈$41,590 — below both of the Model Y's German price bases

## The basis: ex-factory versus landed cost

Same discipline as every EV Hub comparison. **Ex-factory** is the China gate price — $26,730 for the [Sealion 7](/vehicles/byd-sealion-07/) (sold as the Sealion 07 in China). **Landed cost** is what an importer pays to put that car through customs in the destination market: duty, countervailing duty where the EU applies it, and VAT or GST compounding in that order, plus a fixed block for RoRo freight, clearance, certification, registration and inland transport. It is the cash needed at the border — not a retail price, and not inclusive of importer margin or warranty provisioning.

The arithmetic compounds:

```
landed = base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs
```

One deliberate asymmetry runs through the tables below, the same one our other Tesla comparisons carry: the Model Y column is a **local retail price**, sourced per market — it already contains Tesla's taxes, logistics and margin — while the Sealion column is an import-cost estimate. Cost versus price is the right frame for an importer asking "what headroom do I have against this shelf price?"; it is the wrong frame for a like-for-like verdict on the two cars, which is [why the sticker price is never the landed price](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/). Full compounding detail sits in the [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).

Measure these two and the segment confusion that muddies most BYD–Tesla comparisons disappears. There is no size gap to explain away — 33 mm of length separates them:

Two cautions before the table gets quoted out of context. First, 550 km CLTC and 534 km WLTP are different measuring sticks — the Chinese cycle flatters, and the two should never be subtracted from each other; [CLTC vs WLTP](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/) is the short version of why. Second, the Model Y figures describe the entry RWD trim as sold in Europe; the lineup and specs vary by market, so every number above carries a source in the list at the bottom. Where a figure is not published for the entry trim, it stays out rather than getting estimated.

## The tariff wall, in one table

Both cars are China-related for tariff purposes — but that sentence does very different work in different jurisdictions:

| Layer | Sealion 7 into Germany | Model Y for Germany |
|---|---|---|
| Standard import duty | 10% | None — Berlin-built; no customs entry |
| Countervailing duty | **17%** (BYD) | None — outside the measure |
| VAT | 19% compounded | 19% (inside retail) |

The EU's countervailing duty under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754 applies to China-built battery-electric vehicles only, at producer-specific rates — BYD's sampled rate is 17%. Tesla's European Model Y misses the regime entirely because it is assembled in Grünheide: Giga Berlin supplies the Model Y for Europe, and those cars never cross a customs border as imports ([Germany import guide](/blog/germany-import-guide/)). Where Tesla does export China-built cars — the Model 3 volume for Europe, and Model Y units for several non-EU markets — they sit inside the same regime at Tesla's individually assessed 7.8%. No market outside the EU levies a countervailing duty at all.

Three reading notes. The Sealion column is an import cost — no margin inside; the Model Y column is a finished retail product — Tesla's margin inside. Premium percentages are over each car's own base, and the Model Y's German figure flips the ranking depending on basis: at the €39,990 list price the German gap narrows from $2,811 to $1,640, still with the Sealion above. And the Thai figure reflects the RWD entry price when the Juniper line-up launched, with 2026 lists retaining that entry point; verify current lists before acting, as always.

## Worked example: the Sealion 7 through German customs

| Line (Germany) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $26,730.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $2,673.00 |
| Countervailing duty (BYD, 17%) | $4,998.51 |
| VAT (19%) | $6,536.29 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$47,537.80** |

Formula: `26,730 × 1.10 × 1.17 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 47,537.80`. The order matters — standard duty first, countervailing duty on the duty-paid value, VAT last, then the flat block.

Now the counterfactual, clearly labelled as arithmetic and not a shopping option: strip the 17% countervailing duty and the same car lands at `26,730 × 1.10 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 41,589.57` — about **$41,590**, which is *below* both Model Y bases in Germany ($44,727 at the offer price, $45,897 at list). The duty line plus the VAT that compounds over it costs **$5,948 per car**; that single regulatory fact is the entire difference between the Sealion 7 landing above the Model Y in Germany and landing below it. Rates are assigned, not chosen — the exercise exists to locate where the problem lives.

## Why the same car flips from $2,811 behind to $18,284 ahead

- **Inside the EU, the import trails.** Germany −$2,811 and France −$737 are the only two markets where the Sealion's landed cost sits above Tesla's entry price — and both are markets where BYD's full stack (10% + 17% + 19%) compounds against a competitor that pays no import line at all.
- **Delete the countervailing duty and the picture flips.** In the UK, where no CVD applies, the Sealion lands $14,607 below the Model Y's £41,990. In Norway, zero duty and no CVD leave a $4,193 advantage despite 25% VAT.
- **The cheapest lanes are the biggest wins.** Australia (+$6,671, zero duty), the UAE (+$13,729, 5% + 5%) and Thailand (+$18,284, zero duty + 7% VAT) are where an importer has genuine headroom against Tesla's local pricing.
- **Read dollars for decisions, percentages for burden.** The Sealion's premium runs +77.8% in Germany and +24.4% in Thailand; the Model Y prices carry Tesla's margin while the landed estimates carry none, so the dollar gaps above understate nothing and the percentage columns measure two different things.

## Where this comparison can go wrong

1. **Cost versus sticker.** The Sealion column is cash-at-the-border; the Model Y column is a retail product with margin and support network included. Subtracting one from the other measures headroom, not value — see [sticker vs landed](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/).
2. **VAT is not always a cost.** Registered B2B importers usually recover VAT, which cuts the border cash requirement. Duty and countervailing duty never come back; the flip in Germany survives either way.
3. **Fixed costs are central estimates.** Freight, certification and clearance are volume-sensitive; small orders land above these numbers, and per-unit costs fall at scale.
4. **Range cycles are not interchangeable.** 550 km CLTC is not 550 real kilometres and cannot be compared to 534 km WLTP directly; the [cycle explainer](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/) covers the mechanics.
5. **Trim and market variance.** Model Y trims differ by market (and Tesla's prices move — the French entry rose €1,000 in April 2026). Re-check every price before pricing a deal.
6. **Duty regimes are not furniture.** The EU countervailing regime is under review and legal challenge; model at current rates, then stress the deal a few points in either direction.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, the Sealion 7 is the first BYD I would take seriously as a head-to-head Model Y import — right size, right battery, right price — but its economics split cleanly by jurisdiction, and an EU importer should read that split honestly. Inside the EU it lands *above* Tesla's entry pricing before a dollar of margin; outside it, the same car carries four- and five-figure headroom. That is not a product verdict; it is a map.

### Reasoning

First, the numbers leave no ambiguity about where the German loss lives: $5,948 per car of countervailing duty and the VAT compounded over it — delete that line and the Sealion lands at ≈$41,590, below both of the Model Y's German bases. Second, the same car beats Tesla's retail price by $14,607 in the UK and $18,284 in Thailand with nothing changed but the tariff stack, which tells an importer exactly which markets justify the exercise and which ones require winning on something other than sticker. Third, the product case is real but not free: the Sealion carries 71.8 kWh against roughly 60 kWh usable in the entry Model Y, yet weighs 174 kg more and quotes a slower 0–100 km/h — an importer selling against Tesla is selling space and battery, not pace or efficiency. Fourth, and least visible in any table: the Model Y price includes a service network, and the landed cost includes none — whoever imports a Sealion 7 is also building the after-sales answer, and that cost decides year three, not month one.
