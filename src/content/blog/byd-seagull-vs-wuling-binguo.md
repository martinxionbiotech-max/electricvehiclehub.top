## TL;DR

Two of the cheapest new EVs China exports, two tariff fates: the $8,940 **[BYD Seagull](/vehicles/byd-seagull/)** pays a **17.0%** EU countervailing duty while the $7,980 **[Wuling Bingo](/vehicles/wuling-binguo/)** pays **35.3%** — and on that 18.3-point gap the segment flips. In Germany the Bingo lands at **$20,733.19 (+159.8%)** against the Seagull's **$20,291.88 (+127.0%)**, so the $960-cheaper car costs **$441.31 more** to land; outside the EU, where no countervailing duty applies, the Bingo wins every market we price by $1,008.00–$1,670.40.

## Key statistics

- **Base prices (China ex-factory MSRP):** BYD Seagull $8,940 · Wuling Bingo $7,980 — $960 apart
- **Countervailing duty rates:** BYD **17.0%** · SAIC group incl. Wuling **35.3%** — an 18.3-point spread, on top of the 10% standard EU duty; BEV-only and EU-only
- **Germany landed:** Seagull $20,291.88 (+127.0%) · Bingo $20,733.19 (+159.8%) → **Bingo costs $441.31 more**
- **The EU/non-EU split:** Seagull cheaper in **all 12** EU markets ($441.31–$463.56); Bingo cheaper in **all 18** non-EU markets ($1,008.00–$1,670.40)
- **Germany tax lines:** Seagull $4,751.88 vs Bingo $6,153.19; the CVD alone is $1,671.78 vs $3,098.63
- **Fixed costs:** $6,600 — 73.8% of the Seagull's and 82.7% of the Bingo's ex-factory price
- **Flip threshold:** the Bingo needs a $7,730.82 (−13.5%) factory price to match the Seagull in Germany; it is $249.18 short
- **Worked example:** Seagull $8,940 → $20,291.88 Germany (≈€17,719) · Bingo $7,980 → $20,733.19 (≈€18,104)

## Ex-factory vs landed cost: the two numbers that define this matchup

**Ex-factory** is the price at the China factory gate — $8,940 and $7,980 here, both China ex-factory MSRPs from EV Hub's vehicle records. **Landed cost** is what the same car costs delivered and cleared in an import market. The tax terms compound rather than add: standard duty (10% in the EU) on the car, countervailing duty on the duty-paid value, then VAT on everything — duty → CVD → VAT. A fixed block of freight, clearance, homologation, registration and inland transport sits on top (**$6,600 for Germany**: $2,000 RoRo freight + $350 clearance + $3,250 certification + $500 registration + $500 inland). The [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/) documents every input; the compounding order is the part most import spreadsheets get wrong.

That is the argument buyers have been having about this pair — and it is the argument the EU tariff table overrules.

## Two tariff fates: 17.0% vs 35.3%

Both brands build in China and export BEVs, so both sit inside the EU's countervailing-duty regime — Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, in force since 31 October 2024. But the measure is producer-specific:

| Layer | Seagull (BYD) | Bingo (Wuling/SAIC) |
|---|---|---|
| Standard EU import duty | 10% | 10% |
| Countervailing duty | **17.0%** | **35.3%** |
| VAT (Germany) | 19% | 19% |
| Combined, pre-VAT | 27.0% | 45.3% |

BYD's 17.0% reflects its assessed subsidy margin as a cooperating producer ([the complete math](/blog/byd-countervailing-duty-landed-cost/)). Wuling belongs to SAIC-GM-Wuling, which the Commission assigned the table's ceiling rate of 35.3% — the same ceiling applied to non-cooperating exporters ([why, and what SAIC disputes](/blog/saic-35-percent-countervailing-duty/)). The duty is BEV-only (PHEVs sit outside it — see the [EU PHEV tariff window](/blog/eu-phev-tariff-window/)) and EU-only: the UK, the Gulf and Australia apply no countervailing duty at all.

## Germany, worked to the dollar

| Cost item | Seagull (17.0%) | Bingo (35.3%) |
|---|---|---|
| Base price (CIF) | $8,940.00 | $7,980.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $894.00 | $798.00 |
| Countervailing duty | $1,671.78 | $3,098.63 |
| VAT (19%) | $2,186.10 | $2,256.56 |
| Fixed block (freight + clearance + certification + registration + inland) | $6,600.00 | $6,600.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$20,291.88** | **$20,733.19** |
| Premium over base | **+127.0%** | **+159.8%** |

The formulas behind the two columns — both reproduce the records to the cent:

`8,940 × 1.10 × 1.17 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 20,291.88`
`7,980 × 1.10 × 1.353 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 20,733.19`

At the ExchangeRate-API reference rate of 0.873207 USD→EUR (23 September 2026), the Seagull lands at about €17,719 and the Bingo at €18,104 — a €385 flip, in the wrong direction for the cheaper car.

## Why the cheaper car lands dearer — and where that flips

Decompose the $441.31 gap line by line (Bingo minus Seagull):

| Component | Effect on Bingo vs Seagull |
|---|---|
| Base price | −$960.00 |
| Standard duty | −$96.00 |
| Countervailing duty | **+$1,426.85** |
| VAT (19% on the larger duty-paid total) | **+$70.46** |
| Fixed block | $0.00 |
| **Net** | **+$441.31** |

The Bingo's badge advantage — a cheaper car, hence a lower duty — is worth $1,056.00. The rate disadvantage is worth $1,497.31 in CVD plus stacked VAT. The 18.3-point gap wins, and the ranking of the pair inverts.

Two consequences to carry into any negotiation:

- **A ~$249 price cut flips Germany.** At these rates the Bingo would need to sell for $7,730.82 ex-factory — a 13.5% undercut — to land level in Germany. Its actual discount is 10.7%, leaving it $441.31 short. Every $100 off the Bingo's factory price cuts about $177 of German landed cost (multiplier ×1.77108) versus about $153 for the Seagull (×1.53153).
- **At equal bases, the rate penalty is pure.** Price both cars at $8,940 and the SAIC-rate car lands $2,141.55 higher. The Seagull doesn't win because it is better; it wins because BYD's subsidy margin is 18.3 points lower.

Across the complete records the split is clean and total: **the Seagull is cheaper in all 12 EU markets, by $441.31–$463.56** (the range tracks each member state's VAT rate), **and the Bingo is cheaper in all 18 markets outside the EU, by $1,008.00–$1,670.40.** Inside the customs union the badge decides; outside it, the price decides — which is another way of saying the EU's producer-specific duty is stronger than a 10.7% price advantage.

## The fixed-cost trap: microcar economics at the extremes

Prices this low strain every fixed input in the formula:

- The **$6,600 fixed block** is 73.8% of the Seagull's ex-factory price and 82.7% of the Bingo's. Freight plus certification alone is $5,250 — 58.7% and 65.8% of the two bases.
- Fixed costs are roughly **one-third of each car's landed total** (32.5% and 31.8%) — the single largest line item after the cars themselves, ahead of every tax.
- Both cars land at **2.27× and 2.60×** their factory prices in Germany. The gate price is the small part of the story ([sticker vs landed, explained](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/)).

For B2B importers who can recover VAT, the picture sharpens rather than improves. Strip the recoverable 19% and the German comparison reads $18,105.78 vs $18,476.63 — still $370.85 against the Bingo, but the **permanent** duty load (duty + CVD) is $2,565.78 versus $3,896.63. The Bingo's irreversible tax is **1.52×** the Seagull's — a $1,330.85 gap that the recoverable VAT partly hides. For the margin math on that distinction, see the [fleet buyer's guide](/blog/fleet-buyer-margin-math/).

And the alternative is stark: the same Bingo that lands at $20,733.19 in Germany lands at $13,247.95 in the UAE — a $7,485.24 spread, or **93.8% of its ex-factory price**. The Seagull's Germany-to-UAE spread is $5,985.53. If your end customer is outside the EU, this inversion never happens.

## Does a microcar make sense as an EU import?

For a single unit or a small batch, the answer from these numbers is no — and it is no twice. First the fixed block: $6,600 against an $8,000–$9,000 car is a structural penalty no sourcing trick removes ([the segment's full case, here](/blog/wuling-leapmotor-microcar-import/)). Then the premium: +127.0% to +159.8% in Germany, against +60.0% to +66.0% for the same two cars in the UAE. Three situations survive the math:

1. **Non-EU destinations.** The Bingo's $1,008.00–$1,670.40 advantage in the 18 non-EU markets is real money, and no countervailing duty applies.
2. **Volume, not singles.** Freight and clearance amortize across volume; certification and registration mostly do not. Multi-unit importers should re-run the fixed block at their actual scale — it is the only line that moves ([the margin math](/blog/fleet-buyer-margin-math/)).
3. **Buyers who value the Seagull's drivetrain.** In Germany the two land within 2.2% of each other ($441.31 on ~$20,500), and the Seagull — already the cheaper of the two — adds 102 km of range, 25 kW and a 30 km/h higher top speed for that money. This is the rare case where the tariff table and the spec sheet agree.

Between the two cars specifically: **inside the EU the Seagull is the better import — cheaper to land and more capable.** The Bingo can only reverse that by cutting its factory price by more than 13.5%, or by selling where no countervailing duty exists. Outside the EU, the Bingo's price win is genuine but modest against its 102 km range deficit — which is a product decision, not a tariff one.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view the Seagull–Bingo pair is the cleanest case in this series that the EU tariff table, not the showroom, decides the microcar segment: inside the EU the $8,940 car beats the $7,980 car outright, and it is also the more capable car — a rare double win. A buyer who shops on sticker price alone gets the worse import and the dearer landing.

### Reasoning

First, the inversion is arithmetic: an 18.3-point rate gap is worth $1,497.31 in CVD and stacked VAT on these bases, against the Bingo's $1,056.00 base-and-duty advantage — a net $441.31 in Germany, repeated in all 12 EU markets ($441.31–$463.56), while the same two cars rank the other way in all 18 non-EU markets. Second, the threshold is fragile: the Bingo needs a 13.5% factory discount to land level in Germany and offers 10.7%, and every $100 of its price moves roughly $177 of landed cost — so a modest Wuling price move, or any change to the CVD schedule, redraws this map. Third, for B2B buyers the recoverable VAT hides how much of the Bingo's cost is permanent: strip it and the Bingo still trails by $370.85, with an irreversible duty load 1.52× the Seagull's. My practical reading for this segment: check the badge's rate before the sticker, and the destination before both.
