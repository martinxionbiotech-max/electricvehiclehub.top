## TL;DR

The [NIO ET5](/vehicles/nio-et5/) and the Tesla Model 3 are both mid-size electric sedans built in China for export — and that last fact is exactly why this comparison has no price-arbitrage ending. The ET5's ex-factory price of $41,850 sits within 1.5% of the Model 3's finished, VAT-paid German sticker (~$42,454); once 10% duty, NIO's 20.7% countervailing rate and 19% VAT land on top, the ET5 lands at **$72,721 in Germany** — **$30,267 above** the Model 3's local price, and above it in **all seven markets modelled**, from +$10,699 in the UK to +$30,723 in France.

## Key statistics

- **ET5 base price:** $41,850 China ex-factory — 100 kWh battery, 740 km CLTC, 360 kW / 700 Nm, 4.0 s to 100 km/h, 2,690 kg
- **Model 3 reference prices:** €36,990 (Germany, list) · €36,990 (France) · £37,990 (UK) · NOK 330,056 (Norway) · A$54,900 before on-road (Australia) · AED 144,990 (UAE) · THB 1,149,000 (Thailand)
- **Converted at 20 Sep 2026 rates:** ≈$42,454 · ≈$42,454 · ≈$50,793 · ≈$35,036 · ≈$39,077 · ≈$39,480 · ≈$34,446
- **ET5 landed:** Germany $72,721 (+73.8%) · UK $61,492 (+46.9%) · UAE $50,590 (+20.9%) · Thailand $49,430 (+18.1%)
- **Gaps:** +$10,699 (UK, narrowest) to +$30,723 (France, widest) — the ET5's landed cost exceeds the Model 3's local price in every market
- **The duty line:** NIO's 20.7% countervailing rate is $9,529.25 per car in Germany — more than twice the standard-duty line
- **The rate spread:** 12.9 points against Tesla's 7.8% rate — worth $5,939 on this car before VAT, $7,067 once German VAT compounds over it
- **The structural check:** even rebuilt at Tesla's 7.8% countervailing rate, the ET5 would land at ≈$65,655 in Germany — still $23,200 above the Model 3's retail

## The basis: ex-factory versus landed cost

**Ex-factory** is the China gate price — $41,850 for the [ET5](/vehicles/nio-et5/). **Landed cost** is what an importer pays to clear that car through customs in the destination market: standard duty, countervailing duty where the EU applies it, and VAT or GST compounding in that order, plus a fixed block for RoRo freight, clearance, certification, registration and inland transport. It is the cash needed at the border, not a retail price — no importer margin or warranty provisioning sits inside it.

```
landed = base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs
```

As in every Tesla comparison on this site, the second column in the tables below is a **local retail price** — it already contains Tesla's own tax bill, logistics and margin — while the ET5 column is an import-cost estimate. Cost versus price is the right frame for an importer asking what headroom exists against the competing shelf price; it is not a like-for-like verdict on the two cars ([why the sticker price is never the landed price](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/)). Compounding detail: [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).

## Two tiers of premium sedan

Read the spec sheet before the prices, because these two are not the same animal under the skin — the ET5 is the bigger, far more powerful car:

| Specification | NIO ET5 | Tesla Model 3 (entry RWD) |
|---|---|---|
| Length × width × height | 4,790 × 1,960 × 1,499 mm | 4,720 × 1,850 × 1,440 mm |
| Wheelbase | 2,888 mm | 2,875 mm |
| Curb weight | 2,690 kg | 1,847 kg |
| Battery | 100 kWh | 60.0 kWh usable (LFP) |
| Range, as quoted | 740 km CLTC | 534 km WLTP |
| Motor | 360 kW / 700 Nm | 208 kW / 420 Nm |
| 0–100 km/h | 4.0 s | 6.2 s |
| Top speed | 200 km/h | 201 km/h |
| Efficiency, as quoted | 14.1 kWh/100 km | ~13.0 kWh/100 km (WLTP rated) |
| Fast charge, as quoted | 35 min (to 80%) | ~24 min (45→360 km window) |

Two notes before those numbers travel. First, the trim mismatch is real and it matters: 360 kW against 208 kW, 100 kWh against roughly 60 kWh usable, 843 kg more hardware. The entry-to-entry framing used here is therefore the **ET5's best possible case** — against any bigger Model 3, the price gap in this article only widens. Second, 740 km CLTC and 534 km WLTP are different measuring sticks; the Chinese cycle flatters, and the two cannot be subtracted from each other — see [CLTC vs WLTP](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/).

## The tariff wall, in one table

Both cars are China-built, and the EU's countervailing regime catches both — at very different rates:

| Layer | NIO ET5 into Germany | Tesla Model 3 for Europe |
|---|---|---|
| Standard import duty | 10% | 10% (embedded in retail) |
| Countervailing duty | **20.7%** (NIO, "other cooperating") | **7.8%** (Tesla; applies to its China-built exports) |
| VAT | 19% compounded | 19% (inside retail) |

The Model 3 volume sold in Europe is sourced from Giga Shanghai and sits inside the same anti-subsidy measure as every other China-built BEV — just at Tesla's individually assessed 7.8%, as our [BYD Seal vs Model 3 analysis](/blog/byd-seal-vs-tesla-model-3/) sets out. NIO is not a sampled company in the investigation and carries the 20.7% "other cooperating" default — the same rate our [NIO/XPeng/Leapmotor write-up](/blog/nio-xpeng-leapmotor-countervailing-duty/) documents. No market outside the EU levies a countervailing duty at all; the UK, UAE, Australia and Thailand stacks stop at standard duty and VAT/GST.

## The landed-cost matrix: seven markets, both cars

Seven markets, both columns labelled, gaps computed against each market's local Model 3 entry price:

| Market | ET5 landed (model) | Model 3 local price | Converted | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | $72,721 (+73.8%) | €36,990 | ≈ $42,454 | **+$30,267** |
| France | $73,177 (+74.9%) | €36,990 | ≈ $42,454 | **+$30,723** |
| United Kingdom | $61,492 (+46.9%) | £37,990 | ≈ $50,793 | **+$10,699** |
| Norway | $59,563 (+42.3%) | NOK 330,056 | ≈ $35,036 | **+$24,527** |
| Australia | $51,885 (+24.0%) | A$54,900* | ≈ $39,077 | **+$12,808** |
| United Arab Emirates | $50,590 (+20.9%) | AED 144,990 | ≈ $39,480 | **+$11,110** |
| Thailand | $49,430 (+18.1%) | THB 1,149,000 | ≈ $34,446 | **+$14,983** |

*Sticker before on-road costs; Australia's drive-away basis runs higher, which would widen the gap further.

Remember what each column is. The ET5 side is an import cost — no importer margin, no warranty provisioning, no dealer spread. The Model 3 side is a finished retail product with all of Tesla's costs and margin inside it. The ET5 therefore needs its landed number to sit *meaningfully below* the retail number to give an importer room; in all seven markets above, it does not sit below at all. Norway deserves a footnote: it is the one European market with zero duty and zero countervailing duty, yet the gap stays $24,527 — because the Model 3's Norwegian price (NOK 330,056) is one of the cheapest Model 3 entries measured anywhere, and 25% VAT still compounds over the ET5's larger base.

## Worked example: the ET5 through German customs

| Line (Germany) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $41,850.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $4,185.00 |
| Countervailing duty (NIO, 20.7%) | $9,529.25 |
| VAT (19%) | $10,557.21 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$72,721.45** |

Formula: `41,850 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 72,721.45`. Tax and duty alone — the three percentage lines — add $24,271.46 to a $41,850 car before freight or certification. The countervailing-duty line ($9,529.25) is more than twice the standard-duty line ($4,185) and 22.8% of the factory price by itself.

One counterfactual, clearly labelled as arithmetic and not a shopping option: rebuild the same car at Tesla's 7.8% countervailing rate and it lands at `41,850 × 1.10 × 1.078 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 65,654.62` — about **$65,655**, which is *still* $23,200 above the Model 3's German retail price. The rate difference is a $7,067 swing on this car, and it does not change the conclusion: the gap here is structural — factory price plus battery scale — with the countervailing rate as an amplifier.

## Why the ET5 cannot match the Model 3 on price — anywhere

- **The arbitrage was never there.** At $41,850 ex-factory, the ET5 starts within 1.5% of the Model 3's fully taxed German retail price. No tariff regime can price that into a win; the duty and VAT stack only decides how large the loss gets.
- **The EU amplifies.** Germany and France are the two widest gaps (+$30,267 and +$30,723) because both the base price and the 12.9-point rate spread against Tesla's 7.8% compound through duty and VAT.
- **No-CVD markets are the least-bad lanes.** The UK (+$10,699), the UAE (+$11,110) and Australia (+$12,808) are where an importer's position is most defensible — no countervailing duty touches the ET5 there, and the comparison reduces to base price plus light tax stacks.
- **Spec is the sales pitch, not price.** The ET5 out-powers and out-batteries the Model 3 entry by a wide margin (360 kW vs 208 kW; 100 kWh vs ~60 kWh usable). Whoever sells one against a Tesla is selling class — with a cost base that requires it.

## Where this comparison can go wrong

1. **Cost versus sticker.** The ET5 column carries no margin; the Model 3 column carries Tesla's. Subtracting one from the other measures headroom, not value — [the explainer](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/) covers the error.
2. **The trim mismatch flatters the ET5.** Entry-to-entry is its best case; compare against a Model 3 Long Range and the gap grows. Do not read this table as "the same car, two prices."
3. **VAT is recoverable for registered importers.** It changes the border cash requirement, not the underlying duty cost. Duty and countervailing duty never come back.
4. **Fixed costs are central estimates.** Volume and lane sensitive; small orders land above these numbers, and per-unit costs fall at scale.
5. **CLTC is not WLTP.** 740 km CLTC is not a real-world distance and cannot be compared with a WLTP figure directly.
6. **Rates move, prices move.** The countervailing regime is under review and Tesla adjusts its price lists in both directions (the French Model Y entry rose €1,000 in April 2026). Re-check everything before pricing a deal.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, the ET5 is the clearest proof in this dataset that import economics, not product quality, decides cross-border comparisons: a car whose factory price alone is within 1.5% of the Model 3's finished German sticker cannot be imported to win on price anywhere in Europe, and every rate or tax in the chain only widens the loss. The honest case for landing one is positioning above Tesla in spec, not below it in price — and the honest market list for that case starts with the UK, the UAE and Australia, not the EU.

### Reasoning

First, the structural fact is unanswerable arithmetic: $41,850 at the Chinese gate against a $42,454 finished German retail price, before the ET5 pays a cent of duty — no tariff schedule prices that into an undercut, which is why every one of the seven gaps runs against the import. Second, the policy layer still matters because it sets the size of the loss: NIO's 20.7% rate against Tesla's 7.8% is a $7,067 swing once German VAT compounds, and even erasing the spread entirely (the counterfactual) leaves $23,200 to find. Third, geography is the only lever that moves the result in the right direction: strip the countervailing duty — the UK and Gulf lanes, and Australia — and the gap narrows to five figures, which tells an importer exactly which markets deserve the sales effort. Fourth, a caveat that cuts against my own table: the ET5 out-specs the Model 3 entry by 152 kW and roughly 40 kWh of usable battery, so the comparison that actually decides a purchase is against a far more expensive Model 3 — where the ET5's *relative* position improves. This article measures the import math, not the value argument; the two are not the same question, and an importer should be ready to argue the second.
