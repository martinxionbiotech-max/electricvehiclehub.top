## TL;DR

Germany runs the EU's full trade-defence stack — 10% standard duty, a brand-specific countervailing duty (BYD 17%, Geely 18.8%, SAIC 35.3%, most other producers 20.7%), then 19% VAT — which lands a [BYD Seal](/vehicles/byd-seal/) at **$44,413.48 (+79.9%)** on a $24,690 ex-factory price. Germany is the cheapest of the four EU markets priced below in seven of nine runs, but the four markets are never more than $1,515.99 apart on any model — while [Teslas built at Giga Berlin](/blog/byd-seal-vs-tesla-model-3/) sit outside the duty entirely, because the 7.8% Tesla rate covers Shanghai-built units.

## Key statistics

- **Standard import duty: 10%** — the EU's most-favoured-nation rate for battery-electric passenger cars (CN 8703 80 10), collected identically at every EU entry point
- **Countervailing duty: 17%–35.3%, by producer group** — BYD 17.0%, Geely group 18.8%, SAIC group 35.3%, other cooperating producers 20.7%, non-cooperating producers 35.3%, Tesla Shanghai 7.8%; battery-electric only, under a five-year measure in force since 30 October 2024
- **VAT: 19%** — charged on the duty-inclusive value, so it compounds on the whole stack; the lowest standard rate of the four markets compared here (France 20%, the Netherlands and Belgium 21%)
- **Registration: $500** in the model; the full fixed block is **$6,600** (freight $2,000, clearance $350, certification $3,250, inland transport $500, registration $500)
- **Worked example:** BYD Seal, $24,690 ex-factory → **$44,413.48 landed (+79.9%)**
- **Germany vs France / Netherlands / Belgium (nine model runs):** Germany lands cheapest in seven, with margins over the next-best market of **$10.88–$707.99**; Belgium wins the other two runs, by $119.89 and $61.57; the full best-to-worst spread per model runs $250.00–$1,515.99
- **Germany premium range (nine models):** +66.2% (Zeekr 009) to +145.2% (MG 4) over ex-factory
- **Berlin-built Teslas sit outside the duty** — the countervailing duty attaches to China-origin BEVs; Giga Berlin Model Ys are EU-produced

Price basis, stated once and meant everywhere below: every base price is a China ex-factory MSRP; every landed figure is EV Hub's cost-model output from records dated 7 September 2026, decomposed so you can rerun it with your own numbers.

## The German stack, layer by layer

Germany is the EU's largest automotive market — and for most Chinese EV importers, the natural first destination. What surprises first-timers is how little of what they pay at a German border is actually German. The duties are set in Brussels and collected at every EU entry point; Berlin's fingerprints are on exactly two numbers in the stack: the 19% VAT rate and the $500 registration line.

| Layer | Rate | Applied to (EV Hub model) | In the landed figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard import duty | **10%** | vehicle value — the ex-factory price, held as a simplified CIF | Yes |
| Countervailing duty | **17%–35.3%**, by producer group | value plus standard duty | Yes |
| VAT | **19%** | value plus both duties — the duty-inclusive stack | Yes |
| Registration fee | **$500** | flat | Yes |
| Freight · clearance · certification · inland | $2,000 · $350 · $3,250 · $500 | flat | Yes |

The order of operations matters, because nothing here adds — everything compounds. The standard duty lands first; the countervailing duty is charged on the duty-inclusive value, so BYD's 17% contributes $4,617 on the Seal below rather than the $4,197.30 a flat 17%-on-price reading would suggest; VAT is charged last, on everything above it. One scope note worth burning in early: the countervailing duty covers battery-electric cars only — a plug-in hybrid or range-extender import pays the 10% duty and VAT, and nothing else in the duty column. The regulatory background sits in the [EU import guide](/blog/import-chinese-ev-eu-guide/).

## Worked example: a BYD Seal lands in Germany

Take the BYD Seal, at a $24,690 China ex-factory price:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory MSRP) | $24,690.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $2,469.00 |
| Countervailing duty (17% — BYD) | $4,617.03 |
| VAT (19%, on the duty-inclusive value) | $6,037.45 |
| RoRo freight (China → North Europe port) | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 |
| Inland transport (port → buyer) | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$44,413.48 (+79.9%)** |

Formula: `24,690 × 1.10 × 1.17 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 44,413.48`. Every input above is from EV Hub's tariff and landed-cost records; the workings are in the [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).

Treat $44,413 as a floor, not a showroom estimate. It excludes dealer margin and currency moves between order and arrival, and it is built on one simplification: the model applies duty to the ex-factory price as a simplified CIF, while a customs assessor may value the car differently once freight and insurance join the declared value. Where the model's number is a floor, the assessor's can be a ceiling.

## Model by model: Germany vs France, the Netherlands and Belgium

Nine models, four destinations, drawn from the same records. All figures USD; bases are China ex-factory MSRPs; landed columns rounded to the nearest dollar:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [BYD Seagull](/vehicles/byd-seagull/) | $8,940 | 17.0% | $20,292 | $20,307 | $20,422 | $20,172 |
| [MG 4](/vehicles/mg-4/) | $9,690 | 35.3% | $23,762 | $23,806 | $23,950 | $23,700 |
| [BYD Dolphin](/vehicles/byd-dolphin/) | $14,020 | 17.0% | $28,072 | $28,152 | $28,333 | $28,083 |
| [BYD Atto 3](/vehicles/byd-atto-3/) | $16,260 | 17.0% | $31,503 | $31,612 | $31,821 | $31,571 |
| [BYD Seal](/vehicles/byd-seal/) | $24,690 | 17.0% | $44,413 | $44,631 | $44,949 | $44,699 |
| [XPENG G6](/vehicles/xpeng-g6/) | $24,830 | 20.7% | $45,830 | $46,060 | $46,390 | $46,140 |
| [Xiaomi SU7](/vehicles/xiaomi-su7/) | $30,320 | 20.7% | $54,504 | $54,807 | $55,210 | $54,960 |
| [Zeekr 001](/vehicles/zeekr-001/) | $38,000 | 18.8% | $65,694 | $66,090 | $66,587 | $66,337 |
| [Zeekr 009](/vehicles/zeekr-009/) | $61,830 | 18.8% | $102,751 | $103,459 | $104,267 | $104,017 |

Three patterns sit in that grid, and the first two pull against each other.

**Germany wins the title; the title is worth little.** Germany lands cheapest in seven of the nine runs — and then you look at the margins. The closest call in the whole grid is $10.88: the Dolphin, Germany over Belgium. Even Germany's widest winning margin, $707.99 on the Zeekr 009 against France, is 0.7% of that car's landed cost. The spread rewards shipping to the market where your buyers are, not routing containers through the lowest-VAT port — and the three-way Germany/France/Netherlands version of that argument is dissected in [its own post](/blog/germany-netherlands-france-import-comparison/).

**The two exceptions are fee arithmetic, not tax policy.** Belgium beats Germany on the two cheapest cars — by $119.89 on the Seagull and $61.57 on the MG 4 — because Belgium's registration line is $150 against Germany's $500, and on the cheapest cars that $350 concession outweighs two extra points of VAT. From the Dolphin upward the VAT gap compounds faster than the fee saving — $360.88 against $350 on the Dolphin, the closest call in the table — and Germany keeps the lead on every costlier model.

**The rate sheet ranks these cars more than the price list does.** The Seagull and the MG 4 leave the factory $750 apart — and land $3,469.86 apart. Of that gap, $750 is sticker price, $75 is standard duty, about $2,091 is the countervailing-duty difference between SAIC's 35.3% and BYD's 17%, and $554 is the VAT charged on all of the above. That is why the MG 4 carries the heaviest premium in this article (+145.2%) while the $61,830 Zeekr 009 carries the lightest (+66.2%): fixed compliance costs and percentage duties both bite hardest when the base is small. The brand-by-brand breakdowns live in the [BYD 17% math](/blog/byd-countervailing-duty-landed-cost/), the [SAIC 35.3% explainer](/blog/saic-35-percent-countervailing-duty/), the [Geely 18.8% guide](/blog/geely-188-countervailing-duty/), and the 20.7% articles on [XPeng/NIO/Leapmotor](/blog/nio-xpeng-leapmotor-countervailing-duty/) and [Xiaomi/Hongqi](/blog/xiaomi-hongqi-countervailing-duty/).

## What's German here, and what isn't

Of everything an import pays, four numbers are national — and each of the four markets sets them differently:

| Variable | Germany | France | Netherlands | Belgium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VAT (standard rate) | **19%** | 20% | 21% | 21% |
| Registration (model input) | $500 | $400 | $400 | $150 |
| Fixed block total | $6,600 | $6,500 | $6,500 | $6,250 |

That is the whole country-level story: a VAT rate, a fee, and a fee's second-order effect on the fixed block. Everything else — the 10% duty, the brand's countervailing rate, the compliance grind — is the EU customs union working as designed.

Within Germany, three practical items matter for a small-volume importer:

1. **Approval route.** An EU whole-vehicle type approval with a Certificate of Conformity is the fast lane. Without one, Germany's §21 StVZO single-vehicle approval (via TÜV, DEKRA or GTÜ) is the well-trodden path — budget 2–6 weeks and $1,500–$5,000 for certification, more if the car needs adaptations like lighting or speedometer conformity. The model holds $3,250. See [WVTA vs single-vehicle approval](/blog/wvta-vs-single-vehicle-approval/).
2. **Charging interface.** EU-spec cars use Type 2 / CCS2; China-market builds use GB/T. If the inlet is wrong for Germany's network, [the adapter path has a cost of its own](/blog/ccs2-vs-gbt-charging/).
3. **Running costs after registration.** Registration is in the landed figure; annual road tax and insurance are not. EVs currently benefit from reduced or zero annual road tax for the first years, per [EV Hub's Germany reference](/docs/germany-import/) — confirm the current terms for your model.

## The Tesla Berlin question

One piece of sourcing arithmetic comes up in every Germany conversation: didn't Tesla escape this stack by building in Berlin? Half right — and the half that is right matters.

The countervailing duty attaches to origin. It is levied on battery-electric cars built in China. Tesla's Giga Berlin plant in Grünheide builds Model Y for Europe; those cars never cross a customs border as imports and sit outside the measure entirely — no standard duty, no countervailing duty, no import line at all.

The 7.8% Tesla rate in the same schedule covers Shanghai-built units — including the Model 3 most European markets receive from Giga Shanghai. So Tesla doesn't skip the tariff so much as pay the lowest rate on it: 7.8% against BYD's 17%, a gap worth about $2,499 of countervailing duty on an identically priced $24,690 car before the VAT it attracts, as worked through in [BYD Seal vs Tesla Model 3](/blog/byd-seal-vs-tesla-model-3/).

For a Chinese-brand importer the lesson isn't loophole-spotting; it's that the duty has edges. Origin is one edge, powertrain the other. A China-built BEV pays it; a Berlin-built car and a China-built plug-in hybrid both sit outside it. If your product plan treats "Germany" or "the EU" as the variable that decides duty, it is aiming two levels too high — the factory and the powertrain decide, not the destination.

## What importers should check before committing

1. **Pin the producer group's rate first.** It follows the manufacturer, not the badge: SAIC's 35.3% covers [MG](/brands/mg/), Maxus, Roewe, Wuling, Baojun and IM; Geely's 18.8% covers [Zeekr](/brands/zeekr/), [Lynk & Co](/brands/lynk-co/), Galaxy, Livan and Geometry; BYD's 17% applies across [its brand family](/brands/byd/).
2. **Check the powertrain scope.** The countervailing duty is BEV-only; PHEVs and EREVs pay the 10% duty and VAT and nothing more in that column — a materially different table.
3. **Pick the homologation route deliberately.** WVTA/CoC if your model has it; §21 StVZO otherwise — [the cost difference is real](/blog/wvta-vs-single-vehicle-approval/).
4. **Import the EU-spec build where you can.** CCS2 inlet, not GB/T — [the adapter alternative costs more than it looks](/blog/ccs2-vs-gbt-charging/).
5. **Treat the model as a valuation floor.** The model applies duty to ex-factory as a simplified CIF; an assessor may add freight and insurance to the declared value. Get the treatment in writing through a German customs broker.
6. **Verify what you can't see in the table.** Registration is in; road tax, insurance and any current buyer incentive are out — and German purchase incentives were not verified in this research pass (UNKNOWN). Check before you budget around them.
7. **Businesses: plan the VAT cash flow.** Import VAT is paid at the border; Germany's VAT system generally allows input-VAT deduction for VAT-registered taxpayers, but confirm your position with a tax adviser.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view Germany is the right first EU market for most Chinese EV importers — the bloc's biggest car market, the lowest VAT of the four compared here, and a well-trodden approval path — but the destination is the least important variable in the economics. What the table above really shows is that a producer's countervailing-duty rate ranks every car on the page harder than its price does.

**Reasoning.** First, almost nothing in the stack is German: the 10% duty and the brand rate are EU-set, and the four-market margins are a rounding exercise — $10.88 to $707.99 across the nine model runs — which is why Belgium can take the two cheapest cars on a $350 fee difference and lose everything above them. Second, the producer variable is a different scale of decision: $750 of sticker turns into $3,469.86 of landed cost between two small hatchbacks, mostly because of the rate sheet. If you are a dealer choosing which brands to carry, the duty schedule — not the showroom price list — ranks your portfolio. Third, scope is where strategy lives: the BEV-only edge means plug-ins walk around the tariff, and origin is why Berlin-built Teslas sit outside it entirely; when Brussels revisits these lines, every number in this article moves at once. Fourth, cheap cars pay the tariff most painfully in percentage terms — a +145.2% MG 4 against a +66.2% Zeekr 009 — so anyone planning an entry-level channel should stress-test its duty exposure before its margins.
