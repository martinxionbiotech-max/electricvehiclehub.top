## TL;DR

The [Onvo L60](/vehicles/onvo-l60/) is NIO's value-brand answer to the Tesla Model Y — and at the factory gate it looks like the undercut of the decade: **$29,140 against €39,990 (~$45,745)**. The landed-cost math inverts it in the EU. In Germany the L60 lands at **$52,640.12 — $6,896 above the Model Y's entry price** — and only where no countervailing duty applies (the UK, Australia, the UAE) does the gap swing back to the import, by **$3,939 to $11,266**.

## Key statistics

- **L60 base price:** $29,140 China ex-factory — 60 kWh battery, 560 km CLTC, 240 kW / 305 Nm, 5.9 s to 100 km/h, 4,828 mm long, 1,885 kg
- **Model Y reference prices:** from €39,990 (Germany) · €40,990 (France, after the April 2026 +€1,000 rise) · €41,970 (Spain) · £41,990 (UK) · A$58,900 before on-road (Australia) · AED 174,900 (UAE) · Thailand: UNKNOWN in our sources
- **Converted at 22 Sep 2026 rates** (EUR/USD 1.1439 · GBP/USD 1.3332 · AUD/USD 0.7104 · USD/AED 3.6731): ≈$45,745 · ≈$46,888 · ≈$48,009 · ≈$55,981 · ≈$41,843 · ≈$47,616
- **L60 landed:** Germany $52,640 (+80.6%) · France $52,927 (+81.6%) · Spain $53,024 (+82.0%) · UK $44,715 (+53.4%) · Australia $37,904 (+30.1%) · UAE $36,577 (+25.5%) · Thailand $35,830 (+23.0%)
- **The gaps:** Germany +$6,896 · France +$6,039 · Spain +$5,014 (L60 above) → UK −$11,266 · Australia −$3,939 · UAE −$11,040 (L60 below)
- **The duty line:** the NIO-family 20.7% countervailing rate is $6,635.18 per car in Germany — 2.3× the standard-duty line
- **The fixed-cost block:** $6,600 per car (freight, clearance, certification, registration, inland) equals 22.6% of the base price before a single tax line
- **BaaS (China):** Onvo lists the L60 from ¥192,800 outright or ¥135,800 with battery rental (onvo.cn, 22 Sep 2026); the monthly battery fee is UNKNOWN in our sources

Two warnings before those numbers travel. First, the range row is not a subtraction: 560 km CLTC and 534 km WLTP are different measuring sticks, and the Chinese cycle flatters — [the explainer](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/) is the short honest read on why. Second, where a Model Y figure could not be verified from a listed source, the cell reads "—". That is deliberate: filling spec gaps with guesses is how bad comparisons get built. What is not in doubt is the size match — 4,828 mm against 4,797 mm, 2,950 mm of wheelbase against 2,890 mm — this is a genuine head-to-head, unlike the size-mismatch [Atto 3 cross-shop](/blog/byd-atto-3-vs-tesla-model-y/).

*Sticker before on-road costs — the least favourable basis for the L60.

Remember what each column is. The L60 column is a landed import cost — no importer margin, no warranty provisioning, no dealer spread. The Model Y column is a finished retail price that already contains Tesla's tax bill, logistics, service network and margin. Comparing them is legitimate for an importer asking "what headroom do I have against this competing shelf price?" It is not a like-for-like verdict on the cars; [why the sticker price is never the landed price](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/) covers the distinction. On that basis the EU rows are brutal: the L60 does not land below the Model Y's German entry price, it lands **$6,896 above it** — and $6,039 above in France.

## Worked example: $29,140 → $52,640 in Germany

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $29,140.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $2,914.00 |
| Countervailing duty (20.7%) | $6,635.18 |
| VAT (19%) | $7,350.94 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$52,640.12** |

Formula: `29,140 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 52,640.12`. Order matters — standard duty first, countervailing duty on the duty-paid value, VAT over everything above it, then the flat block. Full detail in [the methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/). The three tax lines alone add **$16,900.12 — 58% of the base price** — and the fixed block adds another 22.6%. A value car pays the same stack as a luxury car.

## Why the value story inverts at the EU border

This is the structural problem the L60 carries, and it has two parts.

**First, the fixed costs do not scale.** Freight, clearance, certification, registration and inland transport cost roughly the same whether the car costs $29,140 or $60,000. At $6,600, that block is 22.6% of the L60's base — versus 40.6% on a $16,260 [Atto 3](/blog/byd-atto-3-vs-tesla-model-y/) and far less on a premium import. The lower the base price, the harder the flat block hits the premium percentage. The L60's Germany premium of +80.6% is, in that sense, the arithmetic of being cheap at the gate.

**Second, the competitor does not pay the same stack.** The Model Y volume sold in Europe is built in Tesla's Berlin plant — inside the customs zone, no import duty, no countervailing duty — while the L60 crosses the full 10% + 20.7% + VAT wall. The [G6 comparison](/blog/xpeng-g6-vs-tesla-model-y/) walks through the same origin asymmetry in more detail. Onvo, as a NIO-family brand, carries the 20.7% "other cooperating" rate documented in our [NIO/XPeng/Leapmotor write-up](/blog/nio-xpeng-leapmotor-countervailing-duty/), against Tesla's individually assessed 7.8% for its China-built exports — a 12.9-point spread that compounds through duty and VAT.

One counterfactual, labelled as arithmetic rather than a shopping option: rebuild the German L60 at Tesla's 7.8% rate and it lands at `29,140 × 1.10 × 1.078 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 47,719.51` — roughly **$47,720**, still **$1,975 above** the Model Y's converted entry price. The inversion is not just the rate spread; it is base price plus fixed costs plus VAT meeting a locally built competitor. Outside the EU the map flips for the same reason: the UK (+53.4%) and Australia (+30.1%) apply no countervailing duty, and the Gulf stack is lighter still (+25.5%), so the L60 lands **$3,939 to $11,266 below** the Model Y's local price — real headroom, in the markets where the importer's margin is actually winnable. This is the same NIO-family pattern the [ET5 vs Model 3 analysis](/blog/nio-et5-vs-tesla-model-3/) found at the premium end; the L60 shows it applies at the value end too.

## The BaaS footnote

Onvo's Chinese site lists the L60 from ¥192,800 outright, or **¥135,800 with battery rental** — the NIO-group BaaS scheme in its value-brand form. The page we could access does not publish the monthly battery fee, so we mark that figure UNKNOWN rather than guess. Two things importers should carry anyway: the subscription is a China-domestic scheme that does not transfer to an export vehicle record, and a rented battery complicates resale, warranty and fleet financing in ways the landed-cost table cannot see. Price the deal on the $29,140 full-battery base; treat BaaS as a China-market footnote.

## Where this comparison can go wrong

1. **Cost versus sticker.** The L60 column carries no margin; the Model Y column carries Tesla's. Subtracting one from the other measures headroom, not value.
2. **Mixing range cycles.** 560 km CLTC is not comparable to 534 km WLTP; the two cycles must never be subtracted.
3. **The margin you must add.** $52,640 buys the car in Germany; it does not sell it. Every dollar of importer and dealer margin pushes the L60 further above the Model Y — where it is already above it.
4. **VAT is recoverable for registered importers.** It changes border cash, not the underlying duty cost. Duty and countervailing duty never come back.
5. **Rates and prices move.** The EU's countervailing regime is under review and legal challenge, and Tesla reprices in both directions. Model the deal at current rates and stress it at plus or minus a few points before committing volume.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, the L60 is the clearest demonstration in this catalogue that a value-price strategy does not survive the EU border: a car that undercuts the Model Y's German entry sticker by **$16,605 at the factory gate finishes $6,896 above it at the port**, before a dollar of importer margin. The value story is real — but only in the no-CVD lanes. An importer planning L60 volume in the EU is planning to sell spec against a cheaper incumbent; anywhere else, the same car is a genuine price weapon.

### Reasoning

First, the arithmetic is unanswerable: 58% of the base in three tax lines plus a $6,600 flat block that refuses to scale down with the sticker. A cheap car cannot buy its way past fixed costs, and the premium percentage proves it — +80.6% in Germany against +30.1% in Australia for the identical vehicle. Second, the counterfactual at Tesla's 7.8% rate shows the rate spread is not the whole story: even with the best-case rate, the L60 still lands ~$1,975 above the Model Y in Germany, which tells an importer the EU problem is structural, not an appeal away from being fixed. Third, the markets where the L60 wins on price — the UK at −$11,266, the UAE at −$11,040, Australia at −$3,939 — are the markets where an importer should be shipping first, because that is where the $29,140 base actually converts into a defensible margin. Fourth, a caution that cuts against my own headline: spec-for-spec the L60 matches or beats the entry Model Y in range-per-kWh (11.9 vs 13.4–16.2 kWh/100 km as quoted) and undercuts it in weight, so the product case outside price is stronger than the EU price rows suggest — but a product case is not a landed-cost case, and this article measures the second.
