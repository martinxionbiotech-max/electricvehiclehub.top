## TL;DR

The [NIO ES6](/vehicles/nio-es6/) — sold in Europe as the EL6 — is the premium mid-size SUV of the Chinese export wave, and it squares off against the electric SUV that outsells everything else on the road. On landed cost the matchup has one unambiguous outcome: **the ES6 lands at $81,585.04 in Germany against a Tesla Model Y listed from €39,990 (≈$45,873), a +$35,713 gap, and it lands above the Model Y's local price in all seven markets modelled** — from +$3,745 in Thailand to +$35,713 in Germany. The ES6 out-specs the entry Model Y everywhere that counts and out-prices it everywhere it lands. The question this comparison answers is how much of that premium is car, and how much is customs.

## Key statistics

- **ES6 base price:** $47,460 China ex-factory — 100 kWh battery, 650 km CLTC, 360 kW / 700 Nm, 4.5 s to 100 km/h, 2,843 kg
- **Model Y entry reference prices:** €39,990 (Germany, list) · £41,990 (UK) · NOK 422,560 (Norway) · A$58,900 before on-road (Australia) · AED 174,900 (UAE) · ฿1,719,000 (Thailand) · RM198,000 (Malaysia)
- **Converted at 22 Sep 2026 rates:** ≈$45,873 · ≈$56,155 · ≈$44,786 · ≈$41,942 · ≈$47,624 · ≈$51,687 · ≈$48,566
- **ES6 landed:** Germany $81,585.04 (+71.9%) · UK $68,897.20 (+45.2%) · Norway $66,575.00 (+40.3%) · Australia $58,056.00 (+22.3%) · UAE $56,774.65 (+19.6%) · Thailand $55,432.20 (+16.8%) · Malaysia $59,466.30 (+25.3%)
- **Gaps:** +$3,745 (Thailand, narrowest) to +$35,713 (Germany, widest) — the ES6's landed cost sits above the Model Y's local price in every market
- **The duty line:** NIO's 20.7% countervailing rate costs $10,806.64 per car in Germany, the largest single tax line in the stack
- **The anchor fact:** the ES6's $47,460 factory price is already ≈$1,587 above the Model Y's finished, VAT-paid German retail price
- **The counterfactual:** rebuilt at Tesla's 7.8% rate, the ES6 would land at ≈$73,571 in Germany — still ≈$27,698 above the Model Y's retail

## Two SUVs, one class apart on paper

Read the spec sheet before the prices, because entry-to-entry these two are not the same class of car:

| Specification | NIO ES6 | Tesla Model Y (entry RWD) |
|---|---|---|
| Length × width × height | 4,854 × 1,995 × 1,703 mm | 4,797 × 1,921 × 1,624 mm |
| Wheelbase | 2,915 mm | 2,890 mm |
| Curb weight | 2,843 kg | 1,878–1,906 kg |
| Battery | 100 kWh | 63 kWh (UK entry) |
| Range, as quoted | 650 km CLTC | 314 mi ≈ 505 km WLTP (UK entry) |
| Motor | 360 kW / 700 Nm | 220 kW / 350 Nm |
| 0–100 km/h | 4.5 s | — (not published in the sources verified) |
| Top speed | 200 km/h | — (not published in the sources verified) |
| Efficiency, as quoted | 16.1 kWh/100 km | 4.4–4.6 mi/kWh (≈13.5–14.1 kWh/100 km) |
| Fast charge, as quoted | 35 min (10–80%) | 25 min (20–80%, 175 kW) |

Two caveats before those numbers travel. First, the trim mismatch is real and it cuts in the ES6's favour on spec: 100 kWh against 63 kWh, 360 kW against 220 kW, roughly 940 kg more hardware. The German Model Y line runs from 79.0–84.7 kWh and 525–622 km WLTP across its trims, so the entry figure above is the cheapest Model Y configuration, not the meanest one. Second, 650 km CLTC and 505 km WLTP are different measuring sticks; the Chinese cycle flatters, and the two cannot be subtracted from each other — [CLTC vs WLTP](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/) is the short honest read on why. Where a Model Y figure could not be verified from a listed source, the table says so; we do not fill spec gaps with guesses.

One naming note for importers: the second-generation ES6 is sold in Europe as the **EL6**, so EU dealer quotes, homologation papers and price lists will carry that badge — same car, same tariff line.

## The tariff wall, in one table

Both nameplates are China-born, but they reach Europe through different doors:

| Layer | NIO ES6 into Germany | Tesla Model Y for Europe |
|---|---|---|
| Standard import duty | 10% | None — Berlin-built, no customs entry |
| Countervailing duty | **20.7%** (NIO, other cooperating) | None — outside the CVD regime |
| VAT | 19% compounded | 19% (inside retail) |

The European Model Y volume is built at Giga Berlin, inside the customs zone it sells into — no duty, no countervailing duty, as our [XPeng G6 vs Model Y analysis](/blog/xpeng-g6-vs-tesla-model-y/) sets out. When Tesla does export China-built cars, it sits inside the same anti-subsidy regime as everyone else, at its individually assessed 7.8% rate — the Australian Model Y RWD in this article is China-built, per CarExpert. NIO carries the 20.7% "other cooperating" default, documented in our [NIO/XPeng/Leapmotor write-up](/blog/nio-xpeng-leapmotor-countervailing-duty/). Outside the EU, no market in this matrix levies a countervailing duty at all — the UK, Norway, Australia, UAE, Thailand and Malaysia stacks stop at standard duty and VAT/GST.

| Australia | $58,056.00 (+22.3%) | A$58,900* | ≈ $41,942 | **+$16,114** |
| United Arab Emirates | $56,774.65 (+19.6%) | AED 174,900 | ≈ $47,624 | **+$9,150** |
| Thailand | $55,432.20 (+16.8%) | ฿1,719,000 | ≈ $51,687 | **+$3,745** |
| Malaysia | $59,466.30 (+25.3%) | RM198,000 | ≈ $48,566 | **+$10,900** |

*Sticker before on-road costs — the least favourable basis for the Model Y; Australian drive-away figures run higher, which would narrow the gap.

Remember what each column is. The ES6 side is an import cost — no importer margin, no warranty provisioning, no dealer spread. The Model Y side is a finished retail product with Tesla's entire cost structure and margin inside it. An import makes commercial sense when the landed number sits *meaningfully below* the competing shelf price; in all seven markets above, the ES6 sits above it. Norway deserves a footnote: it is the one European market with zero duty and zero countervailing duty, yet the gap stays +$21,789 — because 25% VAT compounds over the ES6's $47,460 base while Tesla prices its Norwegian entry (NOK 422,560) among the cheapest Model Ys measured anywhere.

## Worked example: the ES6 through German customs

| Line (Germany) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory) | $47,460.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $4,746.00 |
| Countervailing duty (NIO, 20.7%) | $10,806.64 |
| VAT (19%) | $11,972.40 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$81,585.04** |

Formula: `47,460 × 1.10 × 1.207 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 81,585.04`. The three tax lines alone add $27,525.04 to a $47,460 car, and the countervailing-duty line ($10,806.64) is more than twice the standard-duty line ($4,746) — 22.8% of the factory price by itself.

One counterfactual, clearly labelled as arithmetic and not a shopping option: rebuild the same car at Tesla's 7.8% rate and it lands at `47,460 × 1.10 × 1.078 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 73,570.90` — about **$73,571**, still ≈$27,698 above the Model Y's converted German entry price. The 12.9-point rate spread is worth ≈$8,014 on this car, and erasing it entirely does not change the conclusion: the gap is structural — factory price plus battery scale — with the countervailing rate as an amplifier, not the cause.

## Selling class, not price

- **The anchor kills the arbitrage.** At $47,460 ex-factory, the ES6 costs ≈$1,587 more at the Chinese gate than a Model Y costs finished and VAT-paid on a German forecourt. No tariff schedule prices that into an undercut; duty and VAT only decide how large the premium gets.
- **The EU is the worst lane.** Germany (+$35,713) is the widest gap because a 10% duty, a 20.7% countervailing duty and 19% VAT compound over a base price that was already above the competitor's retail.
- **Light-stack markets are the least-bad lanes.** Thailand (+$3,745), the UAE (+$9,150) and Malaysia (+$10,900) are where an importer's position is most defensible — zero or near-zero duty, no countervailing duty, and the comparison reduces to base price plus a light tax stack.
- **The spec gap is the sales pitch.** 100 kWh against 63 kWh, 360 kW against 220 kW, 650 km CLTC against 505 km WLTP. Whoever sells an ES6 against a Model Y is selling a class-above product — and the honest comparison target is a Model Y Premium or Long Range trim, where the ES6's *relative* price position improves (the German line runs to €62,990). [Our XPeng and Zeekr premium landed-cost analysis](/blog/xpeng-zeekr-premium-ev-landed-cost/) shows the same pattern for the rest of the premium export set.

## Where this comparison can go wrong

1. **Cost versus sticker.** The ES6 column carries no margin; the Model Y column carries Tesla's. Subtracting one from the other measures headroom, not value — [the explainer](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/) covers the error.
2. **Entry-to-entry shows the widest gap.** Against Model Y Long Range and Premium trims, the ES6's price disadvantage shrinks. Do not read this table as "the same car, two prices."
3. **VAT is recoverable for registered importers.** It changes the border cash requirement, not the underlying duty cost. Duty and countervailing duty never come back.
4. **Fixed costs are central estimates.** Volume- and lane-sensitive; small orders land above these numbers, per-unit costs fall at scale. The full stack is in the [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).
5. **CLTC is not WLTP.** 650 km CLTC is not a real-world distance and cannot be compared directly with a WLTP figure.
6. **Rates move, prices move.** The countervailing regime is under review, and Tesla adjusts price lists in both directions. Re-check everything before pricing a deal.

## The Author's Take

### Position

In my view, the ES6 is the purest "selling class, not price" case in this dataset: it out-specs the entry Model Y on every line that matters — battery, power, quoted range — and lands above it on price in all seven markets, from a $3,745 gap in Thailand to $35,713 in Germany. No importer lands an ES6 to undercut a Model Y; the only commercially honest frame is a premium product with a premium cost base, sold into the markets where the tax stack lets the product story breathe.

### Reasoning

First, the anchor fact is unanswerable arithmetic: a $47,460 factory price against a €39,990 finished German retail means the ES6 is behind before it pays a cent of duty, which is why every one of the seven gaps runs against the import. Second, the policy layer sets the size of the loss, not its existence: NIO's 20.7% rate against Tesla's 7.8% is worth ≈$8,014 landed in Germany, and the counterfactual at Tesla's rate still leaves ≈$27,698 to find. Third, geography is the only lever that moves the result meaningfully: the ASEAN and Gulf lanes (Thailand +$3,745, UAE +$9,150, Malaysia +$10,900) are where the arithmetic is least hostile, and the EU — with the EL6 badge, the 20.7% rate and a Berlin-built competitor — is where it is most hostile. Fourth, a caveat that cuts against my own table: entry-to-entry is the widest possible gap, and against a Model Y Long Range the ES6's relative position improves; this article measures import math against the cheapest Model Y, which is the comparison most favourable to Tesla.
