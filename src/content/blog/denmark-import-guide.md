## TL;DR

Importing a Chinese EV to Denmark means the full EU customs stack — **10% standard duty, a brand-specific countervailing duty of 17% to 35.3%, and 25% VAT** on the running total — plus **the heaviest registration block in our 30-market dataset at $3,500**. On our model a BYD Seal lands at about **$49,320 (+99.8%)**: **$3,430 more than the same car into Sweden**, and the steepest EU landing in our records for every model we checked.

## Key numbers

- **Import duty:** 10% — the EU's common external tariff on electric passenger cars, identical at every EU border (CN 8703 80 10)
- **Countervailing duty (CVD):** brand-specific — 17.0% BYD · 18.8% Geely, Zeekr and Lynk & Co · 20.7% most other cooperating producers (Neta, Xiaomi, Changan and others) · 35.3% SAIC brands (MG, Maxus, Roewe, Wuling) · 7.8% Tesla Shanghai
- **VAT:** 25% on the duty-paid value — among the highest rates in the EU, and, unlike Sweden, generally **not** deductible on passenger cars even for VAT-registered businesses; only vans and trucks qualify [Source: EAFO]
- **Registration:** $3,500 in our model — the largest registration block of the 30 markets we track ($70 in Sweden, $500 in Germany, $1,400 in Norway)
- **Danish registration tax (registreringsafgift):** share-based and still phasing in — BEVs pay 40% of the calculated rate, frozen for 2026, with a fixed deduction of DKK 161,300; the legislated ramp reaches 80% by 2030 and 100% by 2035 [Source: EAFO]
- **Worked example:** BYD Seal $24,690 ex-factory → **$49,320 landed (+99.8%)**; $3,430 above Sweden, $4,907 above Germany, and the highest EU premium we record for the model
- **Market context:** 131,759 new passenger cars registered January–August 2026, up from 116,351 a year earlier; electric and plug-in hybrid cars were about 86% of August 2026 registrations [Source: Mobility Denmark]
- **No offsetting subsidy:** Denmark offers no direct national purchase subsidies as of 2025 — nothing on the domestic side absorbs the cost stack [Source: EAFO]

## The terms that decide this market

- **Landed cost** — the importer-side cost to bring a China-market car to a Danish buyer, registered and compliant. Base price → import duty → countervailing duty → VAT → freight → clearance → certification → registration → inland transport. The full formula is documented in our [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).
- **MFN duty** — the EU's "most-favoured-nation" tariff of 10% that applies to cars from any country without a free trade agreement covering them. China has no EU car FTA, so 10% is the floor.
- **Countervailing duty (CVD)** — an anti-subsidy tariff layered on top of the MFN duty. For Chinese BEVs, the EU set definitive, brand-specific rates in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, effective 31 October 2024 for five years. It applies in full in Denmark — and, as everywhere in the EU, to battery-electric cars only.
- **CIF** — cost, insurance, freight; the customs value. In our model duty applies to the ex-factory value, with freight, clearance and certification carried as a fixed block per market.
- **Compounding, not adding** — the stack multiplies. Duty comes first, then the CVD is calculated on the duty-paid value, then VAT on the running total. Never sum 10 + 35.3 + 25 = 70.3%; the real number is different in both directions, and only the line-item math gets it right.
- **Registreringsafgift** — Denmark's vehicle registration tax and the country's signature car cost. It is calculated progressively on a car's taxable value: 25% up to DKK 72,900, 85% from DKK 72,900 to DKK 226,500, and 150% above that. BEVs pay only a share of the calculated tax — 40% since 2021, frozen at 40% for 2026 under the latest Finance Act agreement — with increases legislated toward 80% by 2030 and 100% by 2035. A fixed deduction applies to the tax base: DKK 161,300 for BEVs in 2026, against DKK 45,000 for low-emission combustion cars [Source: EAFO].
- **The VAT quirk** — Denmark's 25% is the standard rate, charged on the duty-inclusive value like every EU VAT, but it is generally **not** recoverable on passenger cars, even for businesses; only vans and trucks driven purely for business get full input-VAT deduction [Source: EAFO]. That is a material difference from Sweden, where VAT-registered businesses reclaim the 25% on commercial-use cars.
- **One customs territory** — Hamburg, Rotterdam or Aarhus produce the same customs bill, because the EU is one customs union. Inside it, the country-level variables are VAT and national registration taxes — and that is exactly where Denmark distinguishes itself.

## What a Chinese EV pays at the Danish border

| Layer | Rate for a China-origin BEV | Levied on | In our landed model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Import duty (MFN) | **10%** | CIF value | Yes |
| Countervailing duty | **17% BYD / 18.8% Geely group / 20.7% default / 35.3% SAIC / 7.8% Tesla Shanghai** | duty-paid value | Yes |
| VAT | **25%** | running total (value + duty + CVD) | Yes |
| Registration & plates | **$3,500 flat in our model** — the largest block we track; the real registreringsafgift is share-based and vehicle-specific (see above) | — | Yes |

Read the table top to bottom and the pattern is obvious: the customs layers are set in Brussels and identical across the EU; the VAT is the same 25% Sweden charges; the registration line is where Denmark diverges — and it is the single reason Denmark tops every EU peer in our landed-cost tables. The same logic runs through our [Germany vs Netherlands vs France comparison](/blog/germany-netherlands-france-import-comparison/): inside the EU, the duty bill is uniform and the spread is national tax policy.

## Landed cost by model (Denmark)

Figures from EV Hub's vehicle records: ex-factory China price, plus the brand's CVD rate, 10% duty, 25% VAT, and the fixed import block (freight, clearance, compliance, registration, inland).

| Model | Base (China) | Denmark landed | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wuling Hongguang MINI EV | $5,000 | $18,902 | +278.0% |
| Neta V | $8,320 | $23,408 | +181.3% |
| MG 4 | $9,690 | $27,627 | +185.1% |
| BYD Dolphin | $14,020 | $32,155 | +129.3% |
| BYD Atto 3 | $16,260 | $35,758 | +119.9% |
| BYD Seal | $24,690 | $49,320 | +99.8% |
| Xiaomi SU7 | $30,320 | $59,920 | +97.6% |
| Zeekr 009 | $61,830 | $110,599 | +78.9% |

Two patterns stand out, and both are structural.

First, **the brand's CVD tier is the biggest variable you can control.** The MG 4 and the Neta V sit $1,370 apart ex-factory, but the MG pays SAIC's 35.3% against Neta's 20.7%, so the MG lands **$4,219** higher. On a $25,000 car, the spread between the cheapest and most expensive CVD tier compounds to about **$6,290** in Denmark once VAT is applied. That spread is set in Brussels, not Copenhagen — Danish taxes just amplify whichever tier your brand sits in.

Second, **cheap cars wear the flat block worst.** A $5,000 Wuling pays the same $9,600 fixed block — freight, clearance, certification, the $3,500 registration, inland — as a $61,830 Zeekr, so its percentage premium runs to +278.0% while the Zeekr 009 "only" pays +78.9%. Percentage comparisons between a microcar and a luxury MPV tell you almost nothing about either import decision. Absolute landed cost and incremental tax do.

### Worked example: a BYD Seal in Denmark

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (ex-factory China) | $24,690 |
| Import duty (10% of base) | $2,469 |
| Countervailing duty (17% of base + duty) | $4,617 |
| VAT (25% of base + duty + CVD) | $7,944 |
| Freight, clearance, certification, registration, inland | $9,600 |
| **Total landed** | **$49,320** |

Formula: `base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs`. Every input comes from EV Hub's tariff and landed-cost records dated 2026-09-15.

One caution on the registration line: our model folds registration into a flat $3,500 for comparability, but Denmark's actual registreringsafgift is calculated per vehicle on taxable value with the BEV share and fixed deduction, so a specific car's true bill can land above or below our block — and for imports the taxable value itself is set at the border. Treat $49,320 as a model estimate, not a showroom quote, and confirm the registration computation for your exact car before committing.

## Denmark vs Sweden: same stack, one line apart

This is the comparison the Danish number is built for. Sweden and Denmark charge the same 25% VAT, apply the same EU duty and the same CVD schedule, and serve similar Nordic cost bases. The only difference in our model is the registration block: $3,500 against $70.

| Model | Denmark | Sweden | Gap | Norway (no duty/CVD) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wuling Hongguang MINI EV | $18,902 | $15,472 | +$3,430 | $13,500 |
| MG 4 | $27,627 | $24,197 | +$3,430 | $19,363 |
| BYD Atto 3 | $35,758 | $32,328 | +$3,430 | $27,575 |
| BYD Seal | $49,320 | $45,890 | +$3,430 | $38,112 |
| Zeekr 009 | $110,599 | $107,169 | +$3,430 | $84,538 |

The Denmark–Sweden gap is **exactly $3,430 on every model** — the difference between the two registration blocks, nothing else. Against Germany the gap is bigger: for the Seal, $4,907, of which $3,000 is the extra registration and $1,907 is the extra VAT charged at 25% instead of 19% on the same duty-paid base. And against Norway it mixes both stories: Norway sits outside the EU customs union and levies no duty and no CVD, so the Seal lands at $38,112 — $11,208 below Denmark, of which $7,778 is the customs stack and $3,430 is registration [Source: EU Implementing Regulation 2024/2754; EV Hub records]. Our [Norway import guide](/blog/norway-import-guide/) covers the Norwegian mechanics in detail, and the [Sweden guide](/blog/sweden-import-guide/) is the closest like-for-like comparison in Europe.

## Why Denmark still matters: real demand under the steepest stack

Denmark is not a fringe EV market. It registered 131,759 new passenger cars January–August 2026, up from 116,351 in the same period of 2025, and in August 2026 about 86% of new registrations were electric or plug-in hybrid cars; the fleet now holds 821,184 chargeable passenger cars [Source: Mobility Denmark]. Chinese brands are climbing into that mix: in the year to August 2026, Xpeng registered 3,698 cars (2.8% of the market, up from 1,594 a year earlier), MG 3,065, BYD 2,048 and Zeekr 1,944 — the last from just 122 in the same period last year [Source: Mobility Denmark].

What the state provides — and what it doesn't:

- **No purchase subsidy:** direct national purchase subsidies are gone — none available as of 2025. The support lives in the tax code, not at the till [Source: EAFO].
- **Registration tax relief, for now:** BEVs still pay only 40% of the calculated registration tax, frozen at 40% for 2026, and the fixed deduction for BEVs stands at DKK 161,300. Both are the only reasons the registration line is not higher — and the legislated ramp says which way it points [Source: EAFO].
- **Ownership:** BEVs pay DKK 920 in annual ownership tax in 2026 (up from DKK 840), against DKK 1,160 for a 100 g/km petrol car, and are exempt from the CO₂ surcharge [Source: EAFO].
- **Fleets and company cars:** plug-in hybrids carry a DKK 30,000 surcharge in the benefit-in-kind calculation, while BEVs get a DKK 15,000 deduction — a nudge toward pure electric [Source: EAFO].
- **Charging:** the electricity tax drops to the EU minimum for 2026–2027, and employer-provided charging is tax-free for employees [Source: EAFO].
- **What doesn't help:** the 25% VAT is generally not deductible on passenger cars even for businesses [Source: EAFO]. For a fleet buyer, that is the line that makes Denmark markedly harsher than Sweden, where VAT-registered businesses recover the 25%.

For an importer the reading is two-sided: demand is real and electric-leaning, but nothing discounts the border bill — and the registration system is legislated to get more expensive, not less, over the coming decade.

## What importers should check before committing

1. **Which CVD tier does your brand sit in?** The difference between 17% and 35.3% on a $25,000 car compounds to about $6,290 in Denmark once VAT is applied. Confirm the rate for the exact exporting producer, not just the brand on the badge — and note that the rates in this article reflect the regulation as in force on 15 September 2026; any later EU developments could not be verified for this article (UNKNOWN).
2. **Compute the registreringsafgift for your exact car.** Our $3,500 is a modeled block; the real tax is progressive (25%/85%/150%), scaled by the BEV share (40% for 2026), net of the fixed deduction (DKK 161,300 for BEVs), and based on a taxable value set at import. The step-by-step official computation could not be verified from primary sources at the time of writing — treat the per-vehicle figure as UNKNOWN and confirm with a Danish broker or the registration service of the Motorcentres.
3. **Assume no VAT recovery on cars.** Unlike Sweden, input VAT on passenger cars is generally non-deductible in Denmark; plan the 25% as a permanent cost unless the vehicle qualifies as a van or truck driven purely for business [Source: EAFO].
4. **Check the homologation route.** A car with EU whole-vehicle type approval can be approved without extra technical documentation; a China-only homologation needs single-vehicle approval, which is slower and costlier — see our [WVTA vs single-vehicle approval](/blog/wvta-vs-single-vehicle-approval/) explainer.
5. **Left-hand drive and CCS2.** Denmark is a left-hand-drive market with CCS2 charging; a China-market car needs the right port and charging configuration — our [CCS2 vs GB/T note](/blog/ccs2-vs-gbt-charging/) covers the compatibility cost.
6. **Confirm current customs practice locally.** The Danish import process and fee specifics at Toldstyrelsen could not be verified from primary sources at the time of writing — treat those details as UNKNOWN and confirm with a customs broker before wiring money.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view, Denmark is the most expensive place in the EU to land a Chinese EV in our records — and the reason has nothing to do with Brussels. Denmark's edge over its neighbours comes entirely from the national registration system: the same car that costs $45,890 in Sweden costs $49,320 in Denmark, purely because of a $3,430 registration gap. The customs stack is collective; the ceiling is national.

**Reasoning.** First, the pattern is consistent: in our records, Denmark carries the steepest landing premium of all 12 EU markets for every model we checked — the Seal at +99.8%, the MG 4 at +185.1%, the Wuling at +278.0%. Second, the mechanism is simple and durable: identical duty, identical CVD and the identical 25% VAT that Sweden charges, plus a registration block fifty times Sweden's ($3,500 against $70) — a difference that lands in every line-item calculation. Third, Denmark's own policy trajectory supports the premium rather than offsetting it: the full tax exemption ended in 2016, the 40% BEV share is a freeze rather than a rollback, and the legislated path reaches 100% by 2035; the only counterweight is the DKK 161,300 deduction, which has itself been shrinking (from DKK 170,000 in 2021). Fourth, demand suggests the market absorbs it: with about 86% of August registrations electric or plug-in and Chinese brands multiplying their volumes, the cost stack filters product choices rather than killing the segment. The buyers most exposed are private ones — fleet buyers in Sweden recover the VAT, but their Danish counterparts generally cannot.
