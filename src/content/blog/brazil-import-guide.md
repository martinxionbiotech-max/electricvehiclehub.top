## TL;DR

Brazil finished its three-year tariff ramp on 1 July 2026: fully assembled electric cars now pay a **35% import tax (Imposto de Importação)**, the ceiling allowed under Mercosur rules, after a schedule that ran 10% (2024) → 18% → 25% (2025) → 35% (2026). A BYD Seal priced at $24,690 ex-factory China lands at roughly **$45,568 (+84.6%)**. The threshold is not the 35% — it is the stack underneath it, where IPI, PIS/COFINS and ICMS compound, and where the CKD/SKD assembly-kit concession has now been withdrawn too.

## Key statistics

- **Import tax (II):** 35% for fully assembled BEVs from 1 July 2026 — the Mercosur ceiling
- **Ramp history:** 10% (January 2024) → 18% (July 2024) → 25% (July 2025) → 35% (July 2026)
- **ICMS:** state-level, about 17% in EV Hub's model, levied on a base that already includes the import tax and other federal charges
- **Other federal layers:** IPI (excise on manufactured goods), PIS-Import and COFINS-Import, plus AFRMM on sea freight — all charged before ICMS
- **Countervailing duty:** none
- **Assembly kits:** from 1 July 2026 the reduced CKD/SKD rate is being withdrawn, reaching parity with the 35% CBU rate by 1 January 2027
- **Worked example:** BYD Seal ($24,690 base) → **$45,568 landed in Brazil (+84.6%)**
- **The real hurdle:** cascading federal and state taxes, plus the end of the kit-assembly discount

## Why the 35% number is not the number

Brazil's import tax is the entry point of a chain, not a single line item. The order matters:

1. **Imposto de Importação (II)** on the customs value — now 35% for a fully assembled BEV.
2. **IPI** on the customs value plus the II. The taxable base grows before the rate is applied.
3. **PIS-Import and COFINS-Import** on the customs value, with ICMS included in their base to avoid double taxation.
4. **ICMS** — the state-level value-added tax — computed on a gross-up basis that includes II, IPI, PIS and COFINS. That last step is why Brazilian import math surprises people: ICMS is levied on a base that contains the other taxes.

The practical effect is that a 35% nominal import tax produces a far higher effective burden once the chain completes. Brazil's own tax-reform programme is designed to replace PIS, COFINS, IPI, ICMS and ISS with a dual VAT (CBS and IBS) by 2033, and 2026 is a testing year for the transition — which means the rules you price against today may change twice before they settle.

## The kit-assembly discount closed too

For most of the ramp, brands had an escape valve. Fully assembled EVs paid 25% by July 2025 while CKD and SKD kits still entered at a reduced rate, so importing a near-complete vehicle and finishing it locally was materially cheaper.

That changed. Since 1 July 2026 the CBU rate is 35%, and Brazil has ended the preferential kit rate, with reports indicating kits reach parity by 1 January 2027. A new zero-tariff quota for CKD and SKD electric and hybrid vehicles worth roughly USD 463 million was introduced for the second half of 2026, but a quota is capacity, not a strategy.

Read together, Brazil's policy says the same thing Mexico's does: assemble here, or pay at the border.

## Worked example: a BYD Seal in Brazil

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (ex-factory China) | $24,690 |
| Import tax, II (35% of base) | $8,642 |
| ICMS-equivalent layer (17% of base + II) | $5,666 |
| Freight, clearance, certification, registration, inland | $6,570 |
| **Total landed** | **$45,568** |

Formula: `base × (1 + II) × (1 + ICMS) + fixed costs`. All inputs from EV Hub records dated 2026-09-15.

**Scope note:** the model applies one VAT-equivalent layer. In a real Brazilian customs entry, IPI, PIS-Import and COFINS-Import sit between the II and the ICMS, and the ICMS base is grossed up. So the true landed cost of a CBU BEV in Brazil is higher than $45,568. Treat this figure as the floor of a range, not the ceiling.

## The real threshold

- **A credible assembly plan.** Without one, you are competing on a 35% tariff plus a cascading tax chain against locally built cars that dodge both.
- **ICMS state selection.** ICMS is a state tax and rates differ; where you clear and warehouse is a commercial decision with a line-item consequence.
- **Timing around the reform.** The CBS/IBS transition runs through 2026 and beyond. Contracts that hard-code today's tax mechanics will need renegotiation.

## What buyers should check before committing

- **Full NCM-level calculation, not a rate quote.** Ask your Brazilian broker for the complete entry simulation: II, IPI, PIS, COFINS, ICMS and AFRMM.
- **Is the zero-tariff kit quota accessible to you?** If so, does the volume justify an assembly arrangement?
- **Which state gives the best ICMS outcome** for your distribution footprint?
- **What is the five-year plan?** Brazil's tariff path terminated at the Mercosur ceiling, and the next policy step is local production, not a rate cut.

## The Author's Take

**Position:** Brazil's 35% is the least interesting number in this article, and in my view anyone negotiating an import programme around it is optimizing the wrong line.

**Reasoning:** First, the tariff is capped — 35% is the Mercosur ceiling, so it will not creep higher; the variable part is the tax chain beneath it, where the ICMS gross-up quietly does more damage than a couple of points of II. Second, the removal of the kit-assembly discount is the actual policy event of 2026, because it closes the only structural route to a competitive CBU price and pushes every serious brand toward investment decisions. Third, the 2026 tax-reform test year makes long-dated pricing hazardous: a landed-cost model built on today's IPI/PIS/COFINS/ICMS mechanics has a known expiry date, and I would rather see importers model both regimes than pretend the current one is stable.

**Disclosure:** This is my opinion based on published rate schedules and tariff reporting, not tax advice. Brazilian tax mechanics vary by NCM code and state; validate with a local broker before quoting a customer.
