## TL;DR

The range number on a Chinese EV listing is almost certainly **CLTC** — the China Light-duty Vehicle Test Cycle — and it will read higher than the **WLTP** figure Europe uses for the same car. The reason is the test itself: CLTC assumes a gentler, lower-speed driving profile than WLTP, so it flatters range.

As a rough rule of thumb, **a CLTC figure runs about 10–25% higher than the WLTP equivalent** — so a car quoted at 600 km CLTC is more like **480–545 km WLTP**, and real-world motorway range is lower still. If you're comparing a Chinese EV's range against a European rival, you must normalize the standards first, or you'll be comparing optimism to reality.

## Key statistics

- **CLTC:** China's official range test (since 2021) — gentler profile, higher figures
- **WLTP:** Europe's standard — more demanding, lower figures
- **Rough conversion:** CLTC × ~0.8 ≈ WLTP (a rule of thumb, not a law)
- **Real-world range:** lower than WLTP, especially at motorway speed and in cold weather
- **The lesson:** never compare CLTC to WLTP without normalizing

## Why the same car has two different range numbers

Range isn't a fixed property of a car — it's the output of a specific *test procedure*, and different procedures assume different driving. The three you'll most often see:

| Standard | Region | Optimism | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLTC | China | High | Gentle, low-speed, city-heavy profile |
| WLTP | EU/UK | Medium | More dynamic, includes higher speeds |
| EPA | USA | Low | Most conservative of the three |

CLTC's profile — lower average speed, less aggressive acceleration — lets a battery deliver more kilometres on paper than WLTP's more demanding cycle. That's not a trick; it's just a different ruler. But it means a Chinese car's headline range and a European car's headline range are *not* measured with the same ruler.

## The rule of thumb (with the honest caveat)

There is **no single exact conversion factor** — the CLTC-to-WLTP gap varies by vehicle, battery, and drivetrain. But as a planning estimate, **multiplying CLTC by roughly 0.8** gives you a defensible WLTP ballpark:

| CLTC (China listing) | Rough WLTP estimate |
|---|---|
| 400 km | ~320 km |
| 500 km | ~400 km |
| 600 km | ~480 km |
| 700 km | ~560 km |

Treat this as a *planning* number, not a specification. And remember the second drop: **WLTP is itself optimistic relative to real-world driving.** Motorway cruising, cold weather, and HVAC use all pull real-world range below WLTP — often by another 10–25%.

So the honest chain is: **CLTC (flattering) → WLTP (realistic-ish) → real world (lower).** A 600 km CLTC claim can easily translate to ~400 km of real motorway range in winter.

## Why this matters for your landed-cost decision

Our catalog lists range in **CLTC** (the figure Chinese manufacturers publish), which is why you'll see numbers like 710 km for a Zeekr 001. When you're weighing that against a European EV quoted in WLTP, you're not comparing like with like. Normalize first — otherwise a "longer-range" Chinese EV may actually have *less* usable range than the WLTP-quoted rival it's being benchmarked against.

This is also a real-world buying risk: range affects resale value, fleet suitability, and whether the car works for a buyer's actual driving pattern. Over-estimating range at the purchase stage is one of the most common sources of post-import disappointment.

## What buyers should ask before relying on a range figure

1. **"Which standard is this range quoted in?"** — CLTC, WLTP, or something else? Get the standard, not just the number.
2. **"Is there a WLTP figure for the export version?"** — export variants sold into Europe often carry an official WLTP figure; use it if available.
3. **"What's the realistic motorway range?"** — for long-distance buyers, ask about the high-speed figure specifically.
4. **"How does cold weather affect it?"** — battery range drops in cold; ask about winter performance if relevant.
5. **"Are you normalizing when comparing?"** — a CLTC figure and a WLTP figure are different rulers. Apply the ~0.8 rule before comparing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is CLTC always higher than WLTP?**
Almost always, yes — CLTC's gentler test profile produces higher range figures than WLTP for the same vehicle. The gap is typically 10–25%.

**How do I convert CLTC to WLTP?**
There's no exact universal factor, but multiplying CLTC by roughly 0.8 is a reasonable planning estimate. Real-world range is lower than both.

**Why does my Chinese EV's real-world range fall short of the listing?**
Because the listing is CLTC, which is optimistic — and even WLTP overstates real-world motorway and winter range. The drop from CLTC to real world can be 20–40%.

**Which standard is most realistic?**
The US EPA cycle is the most conservative and closest to real-world motorway driving, followed by WLTP, then CLTC.
