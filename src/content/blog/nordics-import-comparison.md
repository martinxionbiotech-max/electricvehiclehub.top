## TL;DR

Norway is the cheapest of the three Nordic markets to land a Chinese EV — on the customs side it isn't close: **0% import duty, no countervailing duty, and only VAT to consider**, with the first NOK 300,000 of an electric car's price still exempt in 2026. Sweden and Denmark pay the full EU stack instead — **10% duty, a brand-specific countervailing duty of 17% to 35.3%, then 25% VAT** — and Denmark adds the heaviest registration block in our 30-market records at **$3,500**. On our model a BYD Seal lands at **$38,112 in Norway, $45,890 in Sweden and $49,320 in Denmark**. The Sweden−Norway gap is the customs stack (minus a small fixed-cost offset); the Denmark−Sweden gap is a flat **$3,430 on every model we checked** — national registration policy, not Brussels.

## Key statistics

- **Import duty:** Norway **0%** · Sweden **10%** · Denmark **10%** (the EU's common external tariff, identical at every EU border)
- **Countervailing duty:** none in Norway; brand-specific in both EU members — **17.0% BYD · 18.8% Geely/Zeekr · 20.7% most other producing groups · 35.3% SAIC brands (MG, Wuling)**
- **VAT:** **25% in all three** — Norway's now applies only above the first **NOK 300,000** of price (2026 rule), with full VAT signalled from 2027
- **Registration block in our records:** Norway **$1,400** · Sweden **$70** · Denmark **$3,500** — the largest of the 30 markets we track
- **Worked example:** BYD Seal $24,690 ex-factory → **$38,112 (+54.4%) Norway · $45,890 (+85.9%) Sweden · $49,320 (+99.8%) Denmark**
- **The gaps, quantified:** Sweden−Norway ranges from **$1,972 to $22,632** across our seven-model grid (it scales with price and brand CVD tier); Denmark−Sweden is a flat **$3,430**
- **The trajectory:** Norway's VAT exemption is phasing out (NOK 500,000 → 300,000, with 150,000 signalled and full VAT from 2027); Denmark's registreringsafgift is legislated to climb from a 40% BEV share to 80% by 2030 and 100% by 2035

## The terms that decide these three markets

- **Landed cost** — the buyer-side cost to bring a China-market car into a market, customs-paid and registered: base price → import duty → countervailing duty → VAT → freight → clearance → certification → registration → inland transport. The full formula is documented in our [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).
- **EU customs union vs the EEA** — Sweden and Denmark sit inside both the EU and its customs union, so a Chinese car pays EU duties at any of their borders. Norway is in the EEA and the single market but **outside the customs union**, so no EU duty applies. This single structural fact is most of the Norway story.
- **MFN duty** — the EU's "most-favoured-nation" tariff of 10% on passenger cars, the floor for China-origin cars since no EU–China car FTA exists.
- **Countervailing duty (CVD)** — the EU's anti-subsidy tariff under [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754](/blog/china-ev-brand-cvd-master-table/): producer-specific, BEV-only, and in force for five years from 31 October 2024.
- **Compounding, not adding** — the stack multiplies in sequence (duty → CVD → VAT), which is why the effective load is not the sum of the headline rates.
- **One-off registration tax (Norway)** — a domestic purchase tax built around CO₂ and NOₓ; BEVs zero out those components but not a weight-based component above 500 kg.
- **Registreringsafgift (Denmark)** — Denmark's progressive registration tax (25%/85%/150% brackets); BEVs currently pay only a **40% share** of the calculated amount, frozen for 2026, against a fixed deduction of DKK 161,300.
- **VAT recovery** — Sweden generally allows VAT-registered businesses to reclaim the 25% on commercial-use cars; Denmark generally does not, even for businesses (only vans and trucks qualify). That single difference changes the real cost for fleet buyers.

## The three stacks, side by side

| Layer | Norway | Sweden | Denmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Import duty | **0%** | **10%** | **10%** |
| Countervailing duty | **None** — not an EU market | Brand-specific **17–35.3%** | Brand-specific **17–35.3%** |
| VAT | **25%** (first NOK 300,000 exempt in 2026) | **25%** (recoverable for businesses) | **25%** (generally not recoverable on cars) |
| Registration block (our model) | $1,400 | $70 | **$3,500** |
| Domestic purchase tax | BEV components zeroed; weight component applies | None beyond VAT | registreringsafgift at a 40% BEV share (2026) |

Read the table top to bottom and the structure is simple: the two EU members share an identical customs bill set in Brussels, so their only differences are national — VAT treatment and the registration block. Norway differs at the customs line itself, and that is where the biggest spread lives.

Two patterns stand out. First, **Norway wins every row** — the only market in the set without a duty or an anti-subsidy tariff. Second, **the premium percentages invert the ranking as prices fall**: Norway runs from +170.0% on the Wuling microcar down to +36.7% on the Zeekr 009, while Denmark runs from +278.0% to +78.9%. That inversion is arithmetic, not tax policy — the fixed import block ($6,170 in Sweden, $9,600 in Denmark, about $7,250 in Norway) weighs on cheap cars proportionally harder, as it does in every market we track. Compare absolute dollars, not percentages.

One caveat, stated in full in our [Norway guide](/blog/norway-import-guide/): the Norway column applies the headline 25% VAT to the full value as a deliberately conservative convention. The actual 2026 rule exempts the first NOK 300,000, so for cars priced below that line the true cost can be materially lower than modelled.

## What the gaps actually contain

The interesting result of running all three columns is that the two gaps decompose cleanly — and each belongs to a different decision-maker.

**Denmark − Sweden = $3,430, always.** Identical duty, identical CVD, identical 25% VAT, and the only difference is the registration block: $3,500 against $70. The gap does not move with the car's price or brand — it is national registration policy, and it is why Denmark is the most expensive EU landing in our records for every model we checked.

**Sweden − Norway = the customs stack ± a $1,080 fixed-cost offset.** For a given car the gap is:

`gap = base × [(1.10 × (1 + CVD) × 1.25) − 1.25] − $1,080`

The bracket is the customs multiplier by brand tier — **35.875%** of base for BYD (17%), **38.35%** for Geely group (18.8%), **40.96%** for other cooperating producers (20.7%), **61.04%** for SAIC brands (35.3%). The $1,080 subtraction is our fixed-block quirk: Norway's block is $7,250 against Sweden's $6,170, so the steeper fixed costs actually shade against Norway. Check it against the grid:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wuling Hongguang MINI EV | SAIC 35.3% | $1,972 | $3,430 | $5,402 |
| Neta V | 20.7% | $2,328 | $3,430 | $5,758 |
| MG 4 | SAIC 35.3% | $4,835 | $3,430 | $8,265 |
| BYD Atto 3 | BYD 17% | $4,753 | $3,430 | $8,183 |
| BYD Seal | BYD 17% | $7,778 | $3,430 | $11,208 |

| Zeekr 009 | Geely 18.8% | $22,632 | $3,430 | $26,062 |

The brand's CVD tier matters more than any choice among the three countries could: on a $25,000 car, the spread between the 17% and 35.3% tiers compounds to roughly **$6,290** once 25% VAT is applied, while the whole Denmark−Sweden registration difference is $3,430 regardless of badge. If you are optimizing cost, pick the export producer before you pick the country.

## Beyond the border: the rules that reward reading fine print

Customs decides the ranking, but three national rules decide the bill.

**Norway — the threshold is the whole story.** The 25% VAT exemption now covers only the first NOK 300,000 (roughly USD 28,000) of an electric car's price, and it applies to vehicles **delivered** in 2026, not ordered — a 2025 order delivered after 1 January 2026 falls under the new rule. The trajectory is one-way: the threshold has already fallen from NOK 500,000 to 300,000, with 150,000 signalled before full VAT from 2027. On top of VAT, the one-off registration tax still carries its weight component on BEVs — NOK 12.71 per kg above 500 kg — a real line item our model folds into the fixed block rather than itemises.

**Sweden — no purchase bonus, business-friendly VAT.** Sweden scrapped its *klimatbonus* in November 2022 and never replaced it, yet electric cars took **45% of new registrations in August 2026** (42% year-to-date) on the strength of ownership rules: BEVs pay SEK 360 a year in vehicle tax and a company car gets a SEK 350,000 discount on taxable benefit value (capped at 50% of price). For VAT-registered buyers the 25% VAT is recoverable, which removes the single heaviest line, and the registration step is administrative — SEK 1,240 paid in advance to the Transport Agency for verification of origin, plus the inspection. On our model, though, none of it discounts the border bill: the same Seal that costs $38,112 in Norway costs $45,890 in Gothenburg, — $1,477 above Germany, where a $1,907 VAT increment (25% against 19% on the same duty-paid base) is only partly offset by Sweden's $430-cheaper registration line.

**Denmark — the steepest landing, still absorbing it.** Denmark charges the identical customs stack and the same 25% VAT as Sweden, then adds the $3,500 registration block — plus a registreringsafgift that is progressive and legislated to get heavier (40% BEV share now, 80% by 2030, 100% by 2035, against a shrinking DKK 161,300 deduction). The market has absorbed it anyway: 131,759 new passenger cars registered January–August 2026, up from 116,351 a year earlier, with about 86% of August registrations electric or plug-in — and Chinese brands multiplying through that mix (Xpeng 3,698 units this year to August, MG 3,065, BYD 2,048, Zeekr 1,944 from just 122 a year earlier). The catch for import economics: the 25% VAT is generally **not** deductible on passenger cars even for businesses, so Denmark is harsher than Sweden precisely where fleets are concerned.

## What buyers should ask before choosing a Nordic market

1. **"Is the car priced under or over the Norwegian threshold?"** — below NOK 300,000, Norway's real VAT bill can be zero, and the gap over the EU widens; above it, the conservative model is close to reality.
2. **"Which CVD tier is my exporter in?"** — the difference between 17% and 35.3% is worth about $6,290 on a $25,000 car in both Sweden and Denmark. Confirm the rate for the exact producing entity.
3. **"Am I a private buyer or a VAT-registered business?"** — in Sweden the 25% VAT is generally recoverable on commercial use; in Denmark it generally is not. The same car can have two different effective costs.
4. **"What does the registration step really cost?"** — our model uses flat blocks ($70 / $3,500 / $1,400) for cross-market comparability; the real registreringsafgift is vehicle-specific and computed on assessed value, so budget it separately.
5. **"When will the car be delivered?"** — delivery date decides which Norwegian VAT rule applies, and both Norway (2027) and Denmark (2030–2035) have legislated changes ahead. A month can matter more than a discount.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Norway still the cheapest Nordic market for a Chinese EV?**
Yes, on customs: 0% duty and no countervailing duty, leaving VAT as the only border-stage charge. On our conservative model a BYD Seal lands $7,778 below its Swedish figure, and across our seven-model grid the gap runs from $1,972 to $22,632 depending on price and brand tier; for cars priced below the NOK 300,000 VAT line, the real gap is larger still.

**Does the EU countervailing duty apply in Norway?**
No. The CVD is an EU measure under Regulation 2024/2754. Norway is outside the EU customs union, so Chinese EVs enter duty-free with no anti-subsidy tariff — the same structural advantage the UK and Switzerland enjoy without being EU members.

**Why is Denmark more expensive than Sweden for the same car?**
One line: registration. Duty, CVD and VAT are identical, and the Denmark−Sweden gap is exactly $3,430 on every model we checked — the difference between our $3,500 Danish registration block and Sweden's $70 one.

**Can I recover the VAT in any of the three?**
In Sweden, generally yes for VAT-registered businesses on commercial-use cars. In Denmark, generally no — input VAT on passenger cars is not deductible even for businesses. Norway's deduction rules for passenger cars were not verified for this comparison — treat them as UNKNOWN and confirm with a Norwegian adviser.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view the three Nordics sort into a clear order — Norway first, Sweden second, Denmark third — and the instructive part is that only one of the two gaps is Brussels' doing. Sweden-versus-Denmark is 100% national registration policy; Norway-versus-Sweden is 100% customs geometry. Read together, they show that for a Chinese EV the decisive line is still whether the EU tariff stack applies at all, and that after that, national registration choices can add or subtract thousands in ways the tariff debate never mentions.

**Reasoning.** First, the decomposition is exact, not impressionistic: Denmark−Sweden is a flat $3,430 on every row of our seven-model grid, while Sweden−Norway tracks the brand's CVD tier and price — $1,972 for a $5,000 SAIC microcar, $7,778 for a BYD Seal, $22,632 for a Zeekr 009. Second, the brand lever inside the EU dwarfs the country lever between two EU members: choosing a 17%-tier producer over a 35.3%-tier one saves about $6,290 on a $25,000 car, nearly twice the registration spread. Third, all three trajectories point the same way — Norway's VAT exemption is shrinking on a published schedule, and Denmark's registration relief is legislated to unwind by 2035 — so anyone modelling these markets should model the direction of travel, not just this year's rates. Fourth, the demand side is not the constraint anywhere: 45% EV share in Sweden without a purchase bonus, roughly 86% electrified registrations in Denmark with the taxes, and Norway's world-leading adoption. The Nordics will keep buying; the question is only what the border and the registration office take first.

**Disclosure.** This is my analysis of published rules and EV Hub's landed-cost records — opinion, not legal, tax or customs advice, and not a quotation. Landed-cost figures are model estimates with dated inputs, not transaction prices; the Norway VAT lines are conservative ceilings by design. Confirm current rates, thresholds and procedures with the Norwegian, Swedish and Danish authorities or a licensed broker before committing capital.
