## TL;DR

Two compact hatchbacks, two very different tariff stories: the [MG4](/vehicles/mg-4/) carries the EU's highest countervailing duty at **35.3%** (SAIC group) while the [BYD Dolphin](/vehicles/byd-dolphin/) carries **17%** — and the cheaper car stays cheaper everywhere we priced. **The MG4 lands at $23,761.74 (+145.2%) in Germany against the Dolphin's $28,072.05 (+100.2%)**, and the gap holds from Qatar ($14,354.50 vs $18,901.00) to the UK ($19,040.80 vs $24,756.40). The German tax coincidence is worth staring at: the two cars pay **$7,471.74 and $7,452.05** in total duty plus VAT — $19.69 apart — on their way to a $4,310.31 landed gap.

## Key statistics

- **Base prices (China ex-factory MSRP):** MG4 $9,690 · Dolphin $14,020 — $4,330 apart
- **Countervailing duty rates:** SAIC group **35.3%** · BYD **17.0%** — both on top of the 10% standard EU duty, both BEV-only, both EU-only
- **Germany landed:** MG4 $23,761.74 · Dolphin $28,072.05 → MG4 cheaper by **$4,310.31**
- **Germany total tax line:** $7,471.74 vs $7,452.05 — a **$19.69** difference deciding a four-figure gap
- **Elsewhere:** Qatar $14,354.50 vs $18,901.00 · UK $19,040.80 vs $24,756.40 · Australia $16,509.00 vs $21,272.00 · Switzerland $16,943.89 vs $21,811.84 · Thailand $15,018.30 vs $19,651.40
- **Premiums:** MG4 +145.2% (Germany) and +48.1% (Qatar); Dolphin +100.2% (Germany) and +34.8% (Qatar)
- **On paper:** MG4 42.8 kWh / 437 km CLTC / 120 kW · Dolphin 45.12 kWh / 418 km CLTC / 70 kW

## The two tariff stories, side by side

| Layer | MG4 (SAIC group) | Dolphin (BYD) |
|---|---|---|
| Standard EU import duty | 10% | 10% |
| Countervailing duty | **35.3%** | **17.0%** |
| German VAT | 19% | 19% |
| Combined, pre-VAT | **45.3%** | **27.0%** |

The 35.3% is not a rounding artefact — it is the highest rate in the EU's producer table, the same ceiling the Commission applied to non-cooperating exporters, and SAIC disputes it. BYD's 17% reflects its assessed subsidy margin as a cooperating producer. Both are countervailing measures on battery-electric cars from China only, which means they exist in the EU and nowhere else — the UK, the Gulf and Australia treat both brands identically at the border. If the rate mechanics are new to you, the two explainers worth reading first are [why SAIC pays 35.3%](/blog/saic-35-percent-countervailing-duty/) and [how BYD's 17% was calculated](/blog/byd-countervailing-duty-landed-cost/).

Two notes against lazy reading of this table. And the biggest genuine difference in the row is the motor: 120 kW against 70 kW is a different driving proposition, not a trim detail. The MG4 is the quicker, more powerful car; the Dolphin counters with more battery capacity and its own software and interior story.

Read down the last column: the Dolphin pays more to land in every single market, by $4,310 to $5,716 — even though the MG4 hands the EU a duty rate more than twice as high. That sentence should not make sense until you check the bases.

## The German anomaly: taxes within $20, prices $4,330 apart

Here is the itemized reason the ranking survives the tariff split:

| Tax line (Germany) | MG4 | Dolphin |
|---|---|---|
| Standard duty (10%) | $969.00 | $1,402.00 |
| Countervailing duty | $3,762.63 (35.3%) | $2,621.74 (17.0%) |
| VAT (19%) | $2,740.11 | $3,428.31 |
| **Total tax** | **$7,471.74** | **$7,452.05** |

The full formulas: `9,690 × 1.10 × 1.353 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 23,761.74` and `14,020 × 1.10 × 1.17 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 28,072.05`.

The two cars hand almost identical amounts to the German state — $19.69 apart — despite paying headline rates of 45.3% and 27% before VAT. The higher rate lands on the cheaper car, the lower rate lands on the dearer one, and the two effects very nearly annihilate. That leaves the factory gate to decide the ranking: $4,330 of base price, minus $19.69 of tax, equals the $4,310.31 gap. Outside the EU there is no countervailing duty at all, so the same base gap compounds through lighter stacks and the Dolphin's disadvantage widens slightly ($4,546–$5,716 across the five non-EU markets above).

The practical lesson is the one this whole catalog keeps teaching: **rank imports by landed cost, never by tariff rate.** A 35.3% car can and does beat a 17% car; a 17% car stops looking cheap the moment its neighbour is $4,330 cheaper at the gate.

## What importers should check before committing

1. **Variant discipline** — both cars ship in multiple battery and trim configurations, and China-market specs do not always map to export specs. Confirm the exact variant before any price from this article is applied to it.
2. **Cycle discipline** — both range figures are CLTC; benchmark WLTP or local homologation numbers before you print a brochure.
3. **Rate risk, both directions** — SAIC has challenged its 35.3% classification in the EU courts and the regime carries periodic reviews. Budget the EU deal at 35.3% until a ruling says otherwise, and re-price quarterly rather than annually.
4. **Margin reality** — every landed number here excludes importer margin, warranty provisioning and dealer spread. Benchmark against local street pricing before assuming the gap is yours to keep.
5. **The service question** — both brands are still expanding networks in export markets. Whoever services and warranties the car in year three is part of the landed cost, even when they never appear in the table.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view, MG4 versus Dolphin is the cleanest proof in the budget segment that you compare landed costs, not tariff rates: SAIC's 35.3% is brutal on paper, and the MG4 still lands $4,310–$5,716 cheaper everywhere I priced it — but I would only commit EU volume to it with pricing that already assumes the 35.3% survives every legal challenge and review, because in Europe that rate, not the car, is the swing factor in the deal.

**Reasoning.** First, the arithmetic is unusually honest here: the German tax lines coincide within $19.69, which means the entire ranking is decided by base price — a $4,330 factory-gate advantage that no rate differential in the current table can overturn. Second, the same structure flatters the MG4 outside the EU even more cleanly, where no countervailing duty exists for either brand and the comparison reduces to light percentage stacks on two known bases. Third, the asymmetry that would keep me up at night is legal, not commercial: the MG4's European economics ride on a contested rate, so a court ruling or a review outcome reprices every unit in transit. Cheap imports are cheap until the footnote changes.
