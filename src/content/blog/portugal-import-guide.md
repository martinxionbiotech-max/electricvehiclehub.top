## TL;DR

Importing a Chinese EV to Portugal means paying the full EU customs stack: **10% standard duty plus a brand-specific countervailing duty of 17% to 35.3%**, and then **23% VAT** on the running total — one of the highest VAT rates in the bloc. A BYD Seal lands at about **$45,385 (+83.8%)**: $726 more than the same car into neighbouring Spain, and the third-costliest landing of the twelve EU markets we model, level with Ireland and behind only Denmark and Sweden. Portugal's national registration regime spares BEVs and its urban perks favour them, but the border bill is the EU's — and VAT is where Lisbon writes its own number.

## Key numbers

- **Standard import duty:** 10% — the EU's common external tariff on electric passenger cars, identical at every EU border
- **Countervailing duty (CVD):** brand-specific — 17.0% BYD · 18.8% Geely group (Geely, Zeekr, Lynk & Co) · 20.7% most other cooperating producers · 35.3% SAIC group (MG, Maxus, Roewe, Wuling) · 7.8% Tesla Shanghai
- **VAT:** 23% on the duty-paid value — level with Ireland and Poland, below only Denmark and Sweden in our twelve EU markets; recoverable for VAT-registered businesses, terminal for private buyers
- **Registration (ISV):** Portugal's national registration tax treats BEVs more favourably than any other powertrain; our landed model carries a $200 registration line for Portugal
- **Worked example:** BYD Seal $24,690 ex-factory → **$45,385 landed (+83.8%)**; the same car lands at $44,659 in Spain, $44,413 in Germany and $38,112 in Norway
- **Market context:** June 2026 set a monthly record with **12,045 BEVs registered**; July took **30.9% of new passenger-car registrations**, and year-to-date BEVs out-register petrol and diesel combined (26% vs 25%), rising to 41% with PHEVs [Source: UVE]
- **Incentive context:** the recorded package includes a **€4,000 BEV rebate** (up to €55,000, with a scrappage condition), a full exemption from the annual circulation tax, and 100% company VAT deductibility up to €62,500 + VAT [Source: European Commission / EAFO]

## The terms that decide this market

- **Landed cost** — the importer-side cost to bring a China-market car to a Portuguese buyer, registered and compliant. Base price → import duty → countervailing duty → VAT → freight → clearance → certification → registration → inland transport. The full formula is documented in our [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).
- **MFN duty** — the EU's "most-favoured-nation" tariff of 10% that applies to cars from any country without a free trade agreement covering them. China has no EU car FTA, so 10% is the floor.
- **Countervailing duty (CVD)** — an anti-subsidy tariff layered on top of the MFN duty. For Chinese BEVs, the EU set definitive, brand-specific rates in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, in force for five years from late October 2024.
- **CIF** — cost, insurance, freight; the customs value the standard duty is calculated on. In our model duty applies to the ex-factory value, with freight, clearance and certification carried as a fixed block per market.
- **Compounding, not adding** — the stack multiplies: duty first, then the CVD on the duty-paid value, then VAT on the running total. A BYD's multiplier in Portugal is 1.10 × 1.17 × 1.23 = **1.583**; a SAIC-built MG's is 1.10 × 1.353 × 1.23 = **1.831**. Summing 10 + 35.3 + 23 = 68.3% gets the answer wrong in both directions — only the line-item math reconciles with the invoice.
- **ISV (Imposto Sobre Veículos)** — Portugal's one-off national registration tax, levied when a vehicle is registered and charged separately from the EU customs stack. BEVs get the most favourable treatment in the code: the European Commission's EAFO records a full exemption for battery-electric cars as of its April 2025 update (qualifying PHEVs get 75% relief; HEVs 40%); our records describe the BEV treatment as a reduced ISV schedule and carry a $200 registration line in the landed model. The exact 2026 schedule could not be verified from primary Portuguese sources — treat it as UNKNOWN and confirm in writing before shipping. The annual circulation tax (IUC) is separately waived for BEVs [Source: EAFO].
- **VAT recoverability** — the 23% is charged at import for everyone, but VAT-registered businesses can generally recover it later; for private buyers it is permanent cost. Portugal also allows companies a 100% VAT deduction on qualifying BEVs up to €62,500 + VAT [Source: EAFO].
- **Iberian gateway** — Portugal shares the EU customs territory with Spain, so clearing a car in Lisbon or Madrid produces the same duty and CVD bill. The only route-planning variables on the peninsula are VAT and registration — which is exactly what the Portugal-vs-Spain run below isolates.

## What a Chinese EV pays at the Portuguese border

| Layer | Rate for a China-origin BEV | Levied on | In our landed model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Import duty (MFN) | **10%** | CIF value | Yes |
| Countervailing duty | **17% BYD / 18.8% Geely group / 20.7% default / 35.3% SAIC / 7.8% Tesla Shanghai** | duty-paid value | Yes |
| VAT | **23%** | running total (value + duty + CVD) | Yes |
| Registration & plates | ~$200 flat in our records; the underlying ISV regime is BEV-favourable | — | Yes |
| Domestic purchase rebate | **€4,000** for BEVs ≤ €55,000 (scrappage condition) | purchase, not import | No — upside only |

Read the table top to bottom and the Portuguese signature is clear. The customs layers are the EU default — a Seal clears on identical duty and CVD terms whether it enters through Lisbon, Sines or Leixões, or through Rotterdam or Hamburg, because the EU is one customs territory. VAT is where Portugal diverges upward: 23% is the highest rate in our twelve EU markets after the Nordic two, tied with Ireland and Poland. And the national layer is the one piece of the stack that actively favours electric cars: BEVs get Portugal's gentlest registration-tax treatment. The practical result is a market that is expensive to land a car in and relatively cheap to own one in.

For the mechanics of why 10 + 17 + 23 does not sum to 50 on the invoice, see the [EU import guide](/blog/import-chinese-ev-eu-guide/).

## Landed cost by model (Portugal)

Figures from EV Hub's vehicle records: ex-factory China price, plus 10% duty, the brand CVD, 23% VAT, and the fixed import block (freight, clearance, compliance, registration, inland transport — $6,300 in our Portuguese records).

| Model | Base (China) | Portugal landed | Premium | CVD rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wuling Hongguang MINI EV | $5,000 | $15,453 | +209.1% | 35.3% |
| Neta V | $8,320 | $19,887 | +139.0% | 20.7% |
| Leapmotor T03 | $8,440 | $20,083 | +138.0% | 20.7% |
| MG 4 | $9,690 | $24,039 | +148.1% | 35.3% |
| BYD Dolphin | $14,020 | $28,494 | +103.2% | 17% |
| BYD Atto 3 | $16,260 | $32,040 | +97.0% | 17% |
| BYD Seal | $24,690 | $45,385 | +83.8% | 17% |
| Xiaomi SU7 | $30,320 | $55,815 | +84.1% | 20.7% |
| Zeekr 009 | $61,830 | $105,683 | +70.9% | 18.8% |

Three patterns jump out, and all three change how you should price an import.

First, **the premium runs inverse to price.** The Wuling MINI EV more than triples (+209.1%) because duty and taxes are percentages while the $6,300 fixed block is not — on a $5,000 car, fixed costs alone equal 126% of the ex-factory price. At the other end, the $61,830 Zeekr 009 carries the lowest premium in the group (+70.9%). Percentage comparisons between a microcar and a luxury MPV tell you almost nothing about either import decision; absolute landed cost and incremental tax do.

Second, **the badge decides more than the price does.** The MG 4 and the Leapmotor T03 sit about $1,250 apart ex-factory, but the MG pays SAIC's 35.3% against Leapmotor's 20.7%, and the gap widens on the way in. On a $25,000 car, the spread between the cheapest and most expensive CVD tier is worth roughly **$6,190 in Portugal** once VAT compounds on top — a number set in Brussels, paid in Lisbon. The [SAIC position](/blog/saic-35-percent-countervailing-duty/) and the [BYD rate](/blog/byd-countervailing-duty-landed-cost/) sit at opposite ends of that spread.

Third, **do not rank models by sticker.** The Xiaomi SU7 (+84.1%) lands proportionally worse than the cheaper BYD Seal (+83.8%) because Xiaomi sits at the 20.7% default rate while BYD pays 17%. Sort by CVD tier and landed column, not by ex-factory price — the same lesson that runs through our [sticker-vs-landed-price breakdown](/blog/sticker-vs-landed-price/). For how the fixed block is built, see [How our landed-cost model works](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/).

### Worked example: a BYD Seal in Portugal

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (ex-factory China) | $24,690 |
| Import duty (10% of base) | $2,469 |
| Countervailing duty (17% of base + duty) | $4,617 |
| VAT (23% of base + duty + CVD) | $7,308 |
| Freight, clearance, certification, registration, inland | $6,300 |
| **Total landed** | **$45,385** |

Formula: `base × (1 + duty) × (1 + CVD) × (1 + VAT) + fixed costs`. Every input comes from EV Hub's tariff and landed-cost records dated 2026-09-15.

One caution on the registration line: $200 is the smallest item in the fixed block and the least certain — the ISV position for BEVs could not be confirmed beyond EAFO's April 2025 snapshot (flagged UNKNOWN above). Treat $45,385 as the modeled floor for the tax stack, not a showroom estimate, and verify the registration-tax position with a Portuguese broker before wiring money.

## Portugal vs Spain: one peninsula, two tax offices

This is the comparison Iberian importers run first, and the [Spain import guide](/blog/spain-import-guide/) runs the other side of it. The same BYD Seal, side by side:

| Market | VAT | Seal landed | Premium | vs Portugal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Portugal | 23% | $45,385 | +83.8% | — |
| Ireland | 23% | $45,385 | +83.8% | $0 |
| Sweden | 25% | $45,890 | +85.9% | +$506 |
| Denmark | 25% | $49,320 | +99.8% | +$3,935 |
| Poland | 23% | $45,240 | +83.2% | −$145 |
| Spain | 21% | $44,659 | +80.9% | −$726 |
| Germany | 19% | $44,413 | +79.9% | −$971 |
| Norway (non-EU) | 25% | $38,112 | +54.4% | −$7,272 |
| United Kingdom (non-EU) | 20% | $38,841 | +57.3% | −$6,544 |

Two structural facts explain almost every row. Inside the EU, the duty and CVD are identical everywhere — the only country-level variables are VAT and registration. Outside the customs union, the countervailing duty disappears entirely, which is why Norway and the UK undercut the peninsula despite high headline VAT.

Take the Iberian gap first. Duty and CVD lift the Seal's $24,690 base to $31,776 before VAT. Spain's 21% adds $6,673 on that number; Portugal's 23% adds $7,308 — a $636 gap — and Spain's cheaper registration line ($110 versus $200 in our records) adds $90 more. Total: **$726, and every dollar of it is tax policy, not freight or customs.** There is no logistics case for clearing through one Iberian country rather than the other; there is a tax case, and it runs Spain's way.

Against Germany the gap is $971: a $1,271 VAT difference (19% versus 23%) partly clawed back by Germany's more expensive registration line, which costs $300 more than Portugal's in our records. Against Denmark — the bloc's most expensive landing for this car at $49,320 — the $3,935 gap is a 25% VAT plus a $3,500 registration line in our records. And the $7,272 gap with Norway is what the customs union is worth: outside it, the same car sheds the entire duty-plus-CVD layer, of which roughly $7,086 sits on this invoice. (Our model applies Norway's full 25% VAT to the import value; the real rules include a threshold exemption for cheaper cars, so treat the Norwegian figure as a ceiling — the [Norway guide](/blog/norway-import-guide/) walks through the mechanics.)

The Irish row is not a typo, by the way: same 23% VAT, same modeled registration, identical $45,385 landing. Ireland and Portugal are the same import problem wearing different flags.

## Why Portugal still matters: a market that imports anyway

None of the arithmetic above has slowed the demand side down. Portugal registered **12,045 fully electric vehicles in June 2026** — a monthly record, up 75% year on year, split between 8,907 new cars and 3,138 imported used ones [Source: UVE]. July added another **10,229**, the third consecutive month above 10,000 units; among new passenger cars, BEVs took a **30.9% share** in July against 20.7% a year earlier, and 26% year-to-date — now more than petrol and diesel combined (25%), or 41% once plug-in hybrids are included [Source: UVE]. The light-commercial side is electrifying faster still: 4,281 new electric vans and light commercials in January–July, up 138% year on year.

Two details in that data matter for importers specifically. First, **the import channel is already part of the market's habit** — roughly a quarter to a third of monthly BEV registrations are imported used cars (3,138 in June, 3,144 in July), which means registration logistics, appraisals and buyer acceptance for foreign-sourced cars are a normal feature of the Portuguese trade, not an obstacle. Second, **Chinese brands are already winning here**: BYD is the third-best-selling BEV brand year-to-date, behind Tesla and BMW, and the models carrying the full tariff stack are competing head-on with European-built product [Source: UVE].

The policy layer leans the same way — for owners, if not for importers. The recorded 2025 package offers a **€4,000 rebate** for new BEVs priced up to €55,000 including VAT, conditional on scrapping a vehicle over ten years old, on a first-come, first-served budget of €13.5 million; BEVs are exempt from the annual circulation tax and autonomous taxation; companies can deduct 100% of VAT on qualifying BEVs up to €62,500 + VAT; and cities offer free or discounted BEV parking, unrestricted low-emission-zone access in more than 200 municipalities and bus-lane use in selected areas [Source: European Commission / EAFO]. What none of that touches is the customs bill — the rebate is smaller than the VAT line it nominally competes with on a mid-size import, and it comes with conditions. Count it as upside, never as a subsidy against the stack.

So read the "Iberian gateway" label precisely. If it means tariff arbitrage, Portugal is not one: there is no customs advantage to clearing in Lisbon, and none versus Madrid. If it means market access — an Atlantic-facing, left-hand-drive market inside the single market, already importing thousands of BEVs a month, sharing a land border and distribution infrastructure with Spain — then Portugal is the peninsula's launch market, and that is the useful way to read it.

## What importers should check before committing

1. **Confirm your brand's CVD tier.** The spread between 17% and 35.3% on a $25,000 car is about $6,190 in Portugal once VAT compounds. Check the rate for the exact exporting producer against Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, not just the badge.
2. **Run the VAT scenario both ways.** The 23% is recoverable for a VAT-registered business and permanent for a private buyer; on a Seal that swing is the single biggest variable in the build.
3. **Get the ISV position in writing.** EAFO's snapshot records BEVs as ISV-exempt; our records carry a $200 line; the 2026 schedule is UNKNOWN. One email to a Portuguese customs broker settles it before, not after, you ship.
4. **Budget the fixed block honestly.** $6,300 in our Portuguese model, and $3,250 of it is compliance and certification. It does not scale down, which is why cheap cars wear the premium worst.
5. **Check the homologation route before purchase** — existing EU whole-vehicle type approval or single-vehicle approval for a China-only homologation. The [WVTA vs single-vehicle approval](/blog/wvta-vs-single-vehicle-approval/) explainer covers the cost and timing difference.
6. **LHD and CCS2.** Portugal is a left-hand-drive market on CCS2 charging, so a China-market car needs the right port and charging configuration rather than a steering conversion — the [CCS2 vs GB/T note](/blog/ccs2-vs-gbt-charging/) covers the compatibility cost.
7. **Watch the scope.** Our model applies CVD to BEVs only. If your plan involves a plug-in hybrid, confirm the current scope before committing.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view, Portugal is the clearest current test of whether a market can be genuinely EV-friendly and still brutally expensive to import into — and so far the answer is yes, the two are not in tension. The state has done nearly everything within its own tax code to make ownership attractive: ISV relief for BEVs, IUC exemption, a purchase rebate, parking and charging perks. What it cannot do, as an EU member, is change the border bill. The result is a market where a BYD Seal lands third-costliest in the bloc, level with Ireland — and still sells well enough for BYD to be the third-ranked EV brand year-to-date.

**Reasoning.** First, the arithmetic is structural, not seasonal: VAT is now the single biggest tax line after the car itself, the Iberian gap of $726 versus Spain is entirely a VAT-and-registration story, and no domestic incentive is sized to neutralise it — the €4,000 rebate is ownership policy, not import policy. Second, the demand is real and accelerating: three consecutive 10,000-plus months through July, a 30.9% monthly BEV share in a market where BEVs now outsell petrol and diesel combined year-to-date, and an active used-import channel that proves buyers here are already comfortable with foreign-sourced cars. Third, the gateway logic only works in one direction: for a brand already distributing in Iberia, Portugal is a registration-office extension of Spain; for an importer choosing where to land a single container, the tax codes point to Spain, and the honest answer is that Portugal's case rests on the market itself — its demand, its charging and parking privileges, its import habit — not on tariff arbitrage. The one unresolved item is the registration-tax detail: EAFO's April 2025 snapshot and our September 2026 records describe the BEV treatment differently in the fine print, and for a mid-four-figure tax once possible, that is worth one broker's email.
