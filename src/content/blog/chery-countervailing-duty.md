## TL;DR

Chery Group sits on the EU's default countervailing-duty line: **20.7%**, the rate the Commission assigns to "other cooperating companies" — Chinese BEV exporters that cooperated with the anti-subsidy investigation but were not individually sampled like BYD (17.0%), Geely (18.8%), or SAIC (35.3%).

The catch for importers is that Chery's 20.7% almost never shows up under the "Chery" badge. In this catalog, the core Chery brand is **100% plug-in hybrid** — zero BEVs — while the group's battery-electric volume travels under **Chery Fulwin**, **Chery New Energy**, **Exeed**, and **Cowin**. A Chery Fulwin A9 priced at $16,100 ex-factory China lands in Germany at **$32,037 (+99%)**. The cheapest Chery-group BEV, the $5,200 Chery New Energy QQ Ice Cream, lands at **$14,816 — a +185% premium**, because the 20.7% duty and fixed import costs crush a tiny base price.

## Key statistics

- **Chery Group countervailing duty:** 20.7% (on top of 10% standard duty = **30.7% combined** before VAT)
- **Rate type:** the "other cooperating companies" line — applies to every Chery-group BEV not individually sampled
- **BYD:** 17.0% · **Geely/Zeekr:** 18.8% · **SAIC/MG:** 35.3% · **Other cooperating:** 20.7%
- **In force since:** 31 October 2024, for 5 years (EU Implementing Regulation 2024/2754)
- **Core "Chery" badge:** 6 models in this catalog, all PHEV — no CVD applies to them
- **Chery-group BEV count:** 16 models (Fulwin 4, Chery New Energy 7, Exeed 2, Cowin 3)
- **Worked example:** Chery Fulwin A9, $16,100 China → **$32,037 landed in Germany (+99.0%)**

## Why Chery pays 20.7% — and why the badge doesn't say "Chery"

The EU's definitive countervailing duty on Chinese BEVs, set by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, has a three-tier structure for cooperating producers. Three companies were individually sampled and got individual rates: BYD (17.0%), Geely (18.8%), and SAIC (35.3%). Everyone else who cooperated — which includes Chery — was assigned the weighted-average **20.7%** "other cooperating" rate. Tesla's Shanghai exports, sampled separately, carry 7.8%.

The 20.7% travels with the **legal exporting entity**, not with whatever badge is on the tailgate. Chery Group's BEV exports flow through a family of sub-brands that share Chery's platforms and supply chain:

- **Chery Fulwin** — the group's newer EV sub-brand (Fulwin X3, T7, X3 Plus, A9)
- **Chery New Energy** — the legacy EV line (QQ Ice Cream, EQ1, QQ3 EV, QQ Duomi, Wujie Pro, Arrizo e, Shuxiangjia)
- **Exeed** — Chery's premium export brand (Exlantix ES, Exlantix ET)
- **Cowin** — the budget-oriented brand (Kaiyi Shiyue Max, Showjet Pro EV, Xuandu EV)

The core "Chery" badge itself is the exception that proves the rule: its six catalog models are all plug-in hybrids, and PHEVs currently pay **only the 10% standard duty**, with no countervailing duty. The EU's CVD applies to battery-electric vehicles specifically — a PHEV workaround is not available to the BEV sub-brands, but it does mean Chery's PHEV exports face a very different bill.

## The full tariff stack, worked example: Chery Fulwin A9 → Germany

Here is the itemized math for a Chery Fulwin A9 (base $16,100 ex-factory China) imported into Germany, using the same [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/) as the rest of the catalog:

| Cost item | Amount | How it's calculated |
|---|---|---|
| Base price (CIF) | $16,100 | Fulwin A9, China MSRP |
| Standard import duty | $1,610 | 10% of CIF |
| Countervailing duty | $3,666 | 20.7% of (CIF + duty) |
| VAT | $4,061 | 19% of (CIF + duty + CVD) |
| RoRo freight | $2,000 | — |
| Customs clearance | $350 | — |
| Certification | $3,250 | Homologation |
| Registration | $500 | — |
| Inland transport | $500 | — |
| **Total landed** | **$32,037** | **+99.0% over base** |

The countervailing duty ($3,666) is more than double the standard duty ($1,610). On this mid-priced car the CVD is the largest single tariff line, and the $6,600 flat import stack still adds another 41 points of premium on top of the tariffs.

The same math punishes cheap Chery EVs even harder. On the $5,200 QQ Ice Cream, the CVD is only about $1,184, but the $6,600 fixed stack is 127% of the base price on its own — so the landed premium hits **+185%**. That is why Chery's budget micro-EVs are effectively absent from European showrooms: the tariff-plus-fixed-cost math does not survive the journey.

## Same car, different market: where the 20.7% disappears

The countervailing duty is an EU measure. The same Chery Fulwin A9 lands very differently outside the bloc:

| Market | Region | Fulwin A9 landed cost | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | EU | $32,037 | +99% |
| United Kingdom | Non-EU Europe | $27,502 | +71% |
| United Arab Emirates | Middle East | $22,200 | +38% |

A **$9,800 difference on a $16,100 car** between Germany and the UAE — almost entirely the 20.7% countervailing duty plus EU VAT. The UK's post-Brexit 10% duty with no CVD cuts the premium to +71%; the UAE's 5% duty and 5% VAT collapse it to +38%. If your buyer is not in the EU, Chery's tariff problem largely evaporates.

## What buyers should ask before importing a Chery-group EV

1. **"Which badge is actually on the car?"** — Chery Fulwin, Chery New Energy, Exeed, and Cowin BEVs all carry 20.7%; a Chery PHEV pays only the 10% standard duty.
2. **"Is this a BEV or a PHEV?"** — the 20.7% applies to BEVs only. Chery PHEVs currently pay no countervailing duty.
3. **"What's the absolute duty bill, not the rate?"** — 20.7% on a $26,730 Exlantix ET is ~$6,100. Always ask for the dollar figure.
4. **"Am I registering in the EU?"** — if not, the 20.7% may not apply. Get the landed cost for your actual destination.
5. **"How does this compare to BYD at the same price point?"** — Chery's 20.7% is 3.7 points above BYD's 17%. On a $16,000 car that is roughly $650 of extra duty — real but not decisive; base price and specification usually matter more.

## The Author's Take

**Position:** In my view, Chery's 20.7% is less a handicap than a measurement error waiting to happen — most importers price the wrong badge, because the group's BEVs almost never say "Chery" on them.

**Reasoning:** First, the rate is the EU's default "other cooperating" line, so it is not a judgment on Chery specifically — it is the same 20.7% that Changan, NIO, XPeng, Leapmotor, Xiaomi, and Hongqi all pay, and the real gap to BYD is only 3.7 points. Second, the genuine Chery-brand risk is confusion: six Chery-badged catalog models are PHEVs that skip the CVD entirely, while the BEV duty lands on Fulwin, Chery New Energy, Exeed, and Cowin — a buyer who quotes "Chery = 20.7%" without checking the badge gets it wrong in both directions. Third, the budget micro-EVs in the group are economically dead on arrival in the EU regardless of the rate, because a $6,600 fixed stack overwhelms a $5,200 car; the tariff is not the binding constraint there, the fixed costs are.

**Disclosure:** This is my interpretation of published EU tariff law and the EV Hub landed-cost records, not legal or tax advice. Countervailing-duty rates and the list of covered exporters are set by EU regulation and can change; verify the current schedule with a customs broker before pricing anything.
