## TL;DR

As of 19 September 2026, China-made plug-in hybrids still enter the EU paying only the standard 10% import duty, with no countervailing duty in force — but the EU has asked Beijing to cap hybrid exports at around 15% of the market and says it will act if China does not [6][8][11]. If Brussels follows its BEV playbook, brand-specific duties of 17% to 35.3% could be added on top, and BYD, Chery and SAIC are the producers most directly named in the reporting so far [2][4][5].

## What is a PHEV — and how do anti-subsidy duties differ from the BEV regime?

A plug-in hybrid electric vehicle (PHEV) combines a combustion engine with an electric motor and a battery that recharges from the grid. The plug is what separates a PHEV from a conventional hybrid (HEV), which cannot be charged externally and relies on the engine and regenerative braking. A related category, the extended-range electric vehicle (EREV), drives its wheels electrically and uses a combustion engine mainly as an on-board generator.

A countervailing duty (CVD) — the formal name for an anti-subsidy duty — is an extra import charge imposed after an investigation concludes that foreign producers benefit from subsidies that distort competition in the importing market. It differs from the ordinary import duty in both purpose and process: the ordinary rate (10% for cars in the EU) applies to everyone; a CVD is a trade-defence measure aimed at specific producers, follows a WTO-consistent investigation, and is calculated producer by producer. In the EU it is imposed by an implementing regulation published after the case concludes [1][2].

That is how the EU approached China-made battery electric vehicles (BEVs). After an investigation opened in October 2023, definitive duties took effect at the end of October 2024 for five years, expressed as producer-specific rates and added on top of the standard 10% duty [1][2][12]. Plug-in hybrids were left outside the scope — they still pay only the 10% [5]. That exclusion is the whole reason this topic exists: a BEV from China can face a combined duty layer of up to 45.3% before VAT, while a PHEV from the same group, built in the same plants, pays less than a quarter of that.

## Where things stand in September 2026

- **No PHEV measure is in force.** As of 17 September, no additional duties on hybrids had materialised, despite repeated reports this year; plug-in hybrids pay the standard 10% duty only [8][9].
- **No confirmed investigation.** In January 2026 the Commission said on the record that there was "no ongoing investigation into exports of hybrid vehicles" from China [12]. In June 2026, Handelsblatt reported the Commission was preparing countervailing duties on PHEVs and that a case was "believed to be already under way" [4][5]. Whether a formal investigation was ever opened remains UNKNOWN.
- **The live track is negotiation.** On 17 September, the Financial Times reported the EU had asked China to voluntarily cap hybrid exports at around 15% of the EU market — down from more than a third — and would act if China did not. An EU official framed it bluntly: "If they will not limit their exports to our market then we will. This is about stopping deindustrialisation." [6][11]
- **China has pushed back.** On 18 September, China's Ministry of Commerce said voluntary export restrictions "seriously violate WTO rules," that China firmly opposes them, and that any solution must comply with WTO rules and both sides' domestic laws [9][10]. Reuters reported that China will monitor EU moves [7].
- **The next milestone is early October**, when Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič is expected to visit Beijing as Brussels' deadline for "tangible results" under the EU–China Trade and Investment Consultations [8][11].

The pressure behind all of this shows up in the numbers: hybrid imports from China rose from 3,800 units in October 2024 to about 50,000 in July 2026, while average prices fell [6][8][11]. Chinese hybrid exports to the EU grew 155% in 2025 against 12% for BEVs — Euractiv data reported by CnEVPost [12]. Add Europe's industrial politics — Volkswagen's planned reductions of up to 100,000 jobs, a goods deficit with China of €360.6bn last year (almost €1bn a day), and a Commission president saying the relationship has "reached a tipping point" — and the direction of travel is clear even though the instrument is not [8][11].

## What duties could apply — and how they'd stack

No PHEV rates have been published, so the only hard numbers are the BEV precedent. These are the definitive countervailing duty rates under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, which remain in force [2]:

| Exporter group | Countervailing duty | Combined with the 10% standard duty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BYD Group | 17.0% | 27.0% |
| Geely Group | 18.8% | 28.8% |
| SAIC Group | 35.3% | 45.3% |
| Other cooperating companies (Annex) | 20.7% | 30.7% |
| All other companies | 35.3% | 45.3% |
| Tesla (Shanghai), individual examination | 7.8% | 17.8% |

Three mechanics matter for anyone modeling a future PHEV duty:

1. **Duties stack on the customs value.** The standard 10% applies to the customs value, and the regulation expresses the countervailing rates "on the CIF Union border price, customs duty unpaid" — both layers are calculated on the same import value [2].
2. **VAT then applies on the duty-inclusive amount.** The EU's VAT directive says the taxable amount on importation includes duties due by reason of the importation [3]. A duty increase therefore compounds once, at the VAT step: 19% in Germany, up to 25% in some member states [15].
3. **Illustrative math (using BEV rates, on a €30,000 customs value):** 10% duty = €3,000; a 17% BYD-tier countervailing duty = €5,100; VAT at 19% on €38,100 = €7,239. Total added: about €15,339, or 51% above the customs value. At the top-rate tier (45.3% combined duty), the same car would carry roughly 73% in duty plus VAT [2][3].

For a PHEV today the same math runs at just the 10% duty plus VAT — that gap is the "window." The alternative to duties is the undertaking route: minimum import prices agreed between exporters and the Commission, the mechanism the two sides agreed to operationalise for BEVs in January 2026 [13]. What any hybrid measure's rates, scope (plug-ins only, or all hybrids including HEVs and EREVs) and timing would be is UNKNOWN as of this writing.

Read that as a map of line-up breadth, not a sales forecast: it counts China-market configurations in our catalog, and not every model is homologated or sold in Europe. On top of the 154 PHEVs sit 58 EREV models (led by Changan, Chery, Dongfeng, Leapmotor and Seres) — an adjacent plug-in category whose inclusion in any "hybrid" measure is one of the open scope questions [15].

On the second layer — named exposure — the June reporting pointed at BYD, Chery and SAIC as the manufacturers most likely to be targeted [4][5]. Groups like Geely and Great Wall show comparable catalog depth without appearing in the reported target list, which is exactly why watching the scope of any future notice matters more than reacting to headlines.

## What importers and buyers should watch

- **The October milestone.** If Šefčovič's Beijing visit produces a negotiated framework, the near-term risk shifts from duties to volume or price restraint; if it does not, expect a formal move [8][11].
- **A notice of initiation in the EU's official journal.** A formal anti-subsidy case is the prerequisite for duties under WTO rules; in the BEV case, the gap from initiation (October 2023) to definitive duties (October 2024) was about twelve months [1][2][12].
- **Scope wording.** Watch whether a measure targets "hybrids" broadly or plug-in hybrids specifically. Current reporting uses "hybrid," which could pull HEVs and EREVs into the conversation [6][11].
- **The undertaking option.** Minimum-price undertakings can replace duties case by case; the January 2026 BEV consensus created the procedural template [13].
- **Contract terms.** If you are pricing a PHEV import today, the 10%-only duty treatment is a policy variable, not a settled cost [1][2][5]; duty-change clauses and short-dated fixed prices are the pragmatic hedges while scope is unresolved.
- **Plan before the notice, not after.** The BEV precedent shows duties land producer by producer and run for five years [2]; the cheapest moment to re-plan a plug-in line-up is while the rules are still being negotiated.

## The Author's Take

### Position

My read is that some form of restriction — negotiated or imposed — reaches China-built plug-in hybrids within this policy cycle, and the practical question for importers is no longer whether the 10%-only window closes, but how abruptly and through which instrument. I would not underwrite a 2027 import plan that assumes plug-in hybrids keep their BEV-free duty treatment.

### Reasoning

Three things drive my view. First, the two sides are already trading instruments, not just rhetoric: China rejected voluntary export restraints on WTO grounds [9][10], which narrows the realistic menu to a defensible negotiated mechanism — the undertaking route the two sides built for BEVs [13] — or a formal trade-defence case. Second, the economic pressure is concentrated and visible: imports up more than tenfold to about 50,000 units a month, falling average prices, and an October deadline Brussels has set for itself [6][8][11]. The BEV case demonstrated the Commission will run producer-specific duties through to definitive measures even amid retaliation risk [1][2]. Third, exposure is highly concentrated: BYD, Chery and SAIC are the groups named so far [4][5], and BYD's German PHEV leadership means duties would hit precisely the segment that drove its recent growth [5].
