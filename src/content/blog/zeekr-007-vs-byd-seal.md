## TL;DR

The [Zeekr 007](/vehicles/zeekr-007/) and the [BYD Seal](/vehicles/byd-seal/) answer one question — what does a mid-size Chinese electric sedan cost delivered — with a $4,870 gap at the factory gate and anything from $5,114 to $8,155 at the customs house, depending on where you point the ship. **In Germany the 007 lands at $52,568.52 (+77.8%) against the Seal's $44,413.48 (+79.9%): an $8,155.04 spread. In Qatar the same pair sits $5,113.50 apart.** The question importers should run is not which sedan is better — it is which tax stack you are selling into, because that is what turns one gap into eight different ones.

## Key statistics

- **Base prices (China ex-factory MSRP):** 007 $29,560 · Seal $24,690 — $4,870 apart
- **Germany:** $52,568.52 vs $44,413.48 → **$8,155.04** spread; every $1 of factory-gate difference lands as ≈$1.67
- **Qatar:** $35,218.00 vs $30,104.50 → **$5,113.50** spread; ≈$1.05 per $1
- **The full range of eight markets:** UK $6,428.40 · Saudi Arabia $5,880.52 · Switzerland $5,475.04 · UAE $5,369.17 · Australia $5,357.00 · Thailand $5,210.90
- **Countervailing duty:** Zeekr (Geely group attribution) 18.8% · BYD 17.0% — EU-only, BEV-only; both cars are BEVs
- **On paper:** 007 — 75 kWh, 688 km CLTC, 310 kW, 4,865 mm · Seal — 61.44 kWh, 450 km CLTC, 170 kW, 4,800 mm
- **What the premium buys:** +22% battery capacity, +53% quoted range, +82% motor power, for 16.5% more ex-factory money

Both ranges are CLTC — the optimistic Chinese cycle — and neither should be subtracted from a WLTP figure; our [cycle explainer](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/) covers why. What the table does show cleanly: the footprint difference between these cars is 65 mm of length and 8 mm of wheelbase. The 007's premium is not paying for size. It is paying for battery, power and charge speed — and then the taxman gets his turn.

Read the multiplier column, because it is the article. The same specification upgrade — the thing the 007 brochure sells — costs $5,113.50 to land in Doha and $8,155.04 to land in Hamburg. Nothing about the cars changes between those rows. Only the tax code does.

## Where the multiplier comes from

Outside the EU, both cars face identical rate structures (no countervailing duty applies to either, and the standard duty and consumption tax are the same for both), so the spread is pure arithmetic:

`spread = ($29,560 − $24,690) × (1 + duty) × (1 + VAT)`

Qatar: `4,870 × 1.05 × 1.00 = 5,113.50`. The UK: `4,870 × 1.10 × 1.20 = 6,428.40`. Switzerland: `4,870 × 1.04 × 1.081 ≈ 5,475`. A fourth of the spread can be tax; in Qatar it is almost none.

Germany is the one mixed case among these eight, because the two brands do not share a countervailing-duty rate — 18.8% for Zeekr under [Geely group attribution](/blog/zeekr-countervailing-duty/) versus [17.0% for BYD](/blog/byd-countervailing-duty-landed-cost/):

`spread = $29,560 × 1.10 × 1.188 × 1.19 − $24,690 × 1.10 × 1.17 × 1.19 = $52,568.52 − $44,413.48 = $8,155.04`

In euros at the 17 September 2026 rate of 1.1473: ≈€45,819 versus ≈€38,711 — a €7,108 spread, the largest of the eight markets.

## Worked example: Zeekr 007 → Germany, line by line

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base price (China ex-factory MSRP) | $29,560.00 |
| Standard import duty (10%) | $2,956.00 |
| Countervailing duty (18.8%) | $6,113.01 |
| VAT (19%) | $7,339.51 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000.00 |
| Customs clearance | $350.00 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250.00 |
| Registration | $500.00 |
| Inland transport | $500.00 |
| **Total landed** | **$52,568.52** |

Formula: `29,560 × 1.10 × 1.188 × 1.19 + 6,600 = 52,568.52`. Note that the percentage stack adds $16,408.52 before the flat block — more than half the car's own price — of which the countervailing duty alone is $6,113.01. The Seal's equivalent stack (17% instead of 18.8%) adds $15,123.48. The rate gap of 1.8 points is worth about $1,285 between these two otherwise analogous imports; the other $6,870 of the German spread is base price and the compounding that follows it.

## What the gap buys — and what it doesn't

The 007's case on paper is straightforward: 238 km more claimed range, 13.56 kWh more battery, 140 kW more power, a charge claim that is 15 minutes quicker. For a buyer who values drivetrain over everything, that is a coherent story, and in flat-tax markets like Qatar or Thailand the arithmetic behind it stays close to the brochure.

Three things it does not buy, in my reading. First, cycle truth: both ranges are CLTC, and the [real-world gap](/blog/cltc-vs-wltp-range/) between the two cars will be narrower than 238 km, though probably still real. Second, service: both brands are still building their European networks, and neither the 007's service plan nor the Seal's appears anywhere in a landed-cost table. Third, and most practically, chargers do not always deliver the ideal; a 10-minute claim and a 25-minute claim both assume conditions you will not always get. The comparison here prices the import; it does not pretend to road-test either car.

## What importers should check before committing

1. **The exact pack and trim.** The 007 ships in multiple battery configurations in China; this analysis uses the 75 kWh reference. Confirm the variant, its homologation status and its curb weight before quoting.
2. **Distribution and parts for the 007 specifically.** Zeekr's European rollout is still filling out — a September 2026 check of German marketplace listings showed the X, 001 and 7X among its models, with no 007 among them. Confirm your channel, homologation and parts pipeline before committing volume, wherever you plan to sell.
3. **Current duty treatment, both brands.** Rates have been under review and legal challenge; verify the regulation's current state and your brand's attribution before budgeting. A group-attribution nuance decides which rate an individual model pays.
4. **Cycle and charging discipline.** Quote WLTP (or local homologation) numbers in front of buyers, and validate charge claims against the infrastructure your customers will actually use.
5. **Margin from the correct base.** Every figure in this article is a landed cost, not a retail price. In Germany the spread is $8,155 before a cent of importer margin; in Qatar it is $5,114. Price your deal from the destination, not the brochure.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** In my view, the 007 is the better car and the Seal the better EU import: the same $4,870 ex-factory gap lands as $5,113.50 in Qatar but $8,155.04 in Germany, and until both brands' European service networks mature, I would buy the 007 where taxes are flat and let the Seal carry the load where they compound.

**Reasoning.** First, the multiplier column is the honest specification sheet of this matchup: 1.05× in Qatar against 1.67× in Germany means the upgrade's price is set in the destination's tax code, and an importer who ignores that is negotiating against their own spreadsheet. Second, what the premium buys is real but narrow — battery and power, not space — which makes it easiest to justify where the landed spread is smallest and hardest where it is largest; the graph points one way. Third, the rate structures are close enough (18.8% against 17%) that neither car enjoys a hidden tariff advantage in Europe; the tiebreaker there is execution — service network, resale, parts — and on execution risk in Europe today, the Seal's more established position is worth more than the 007's spec sheet in my book. That ranking flips the moment you move outside the EU, and I would act on it accordingly.
