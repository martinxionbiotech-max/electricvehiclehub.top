## TL;DR

Xiaomi and Hongqi both pay the EU's **20.7%** countervailing duty — the "other cooperating companies" line on top of the 10% standard import duty, a **30.7% combined tariff before VAT**. Neither was individually sampled in the EU's anti-subsidy investigation.

They are an odd pairing. Xiaomi is a three-year-old tech challenger whose cheapest BEV, the $30,320 SU7, lands in Germany at **$54,504 (+79.8%)**. Hongqi is China's oldest luxury marque — the state limousine brand — whose range runs from the $12,650 E QM5 to the $83,070 E HS9. The E QM5 lands at **$26,587 (+110%)** because its low base price collides with the $6,600 fixed stack; the E HS9 lands at **$137,848 (+66%)**, where the same 20.7% is the largest single dollar line.

## Key statistics

- **Countervailing duty for both:** 20.7% (on top of 10% standard duty = **30.7% combined** before VAT)
- **Rate type:** the "other cooperating companies" line — neither was individually sampled
- **BYD:** 17.0% · **Geely/Zeekr:** 18.8% · **SAIC/MG:** 35.3%
- **In force since:** 31 October 2024, for 5 years (EU Implementing Regulation 2024/2754)
- **BEV count:** Xiaomi 4 (SU7, YU7, YU7 GT, SU7 Ultra), Hongqi 8 (E QM5 through E HS9)
- **Worked examples:** Xiaomi SU7 $30,320 → **$54,504 (+79.8%)** · Hongqi EH7 $29,410 → **$53,067 (+80.4%)**

## Why Xiaomi and Hongqi both pay 20.7% — and why the pairing is misleading

Under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, individual rates went only to BYD (17.0%), Geely (18.8%), and SAIC (35.3%), plus Tesla's Shanghai exports at 7.8%. Every other cooperating Chinese BEV exporter — including Xiaomi and Hongqi — was assigned the weighted-average **20.7%**.

The regulatory treatment flattens a gulf. Xiaomi entered the EV market in 2021 and ships a tightly focused four-model BEV lineup. Hongqi, founded in 1958 as China's ceremonial state-car maker and now a FAW brand, spans a broad luxury and near-luxury range. For tariff purposes the two are indistinguishable: both cooperate, neither was sampled, both pay 20.7%.

The useful distinction is positioning. Xiaomi's four BEVs cluster in the $30,000–$74,000 band. Hongqi spans $12,650 to $83,070, which means Hongqi's own internal spread — from a +110% premium on the E QM5 to +66% on the E HS9 — is wider than the gap between the two brands.

## The full tariff stack, worked example: Xiaomi SU7 → Germany

Here is the itemized math for a Xiaomi SU7 (base $30,320 ex-factory China) imported into Germany, using the same [landed-cost methodology](/docs/landed-cost-methodology/) as the rest of the catalog:

| Cost item | Amount | How it's calculated |
|---|---|---|
| Base price (CIF) | $30,320 | Xiaomi SU7, China MSRP |
| Standard import duty | $3,032 | 10% of CIF |
| Countervailing duty | $6,904 | 20.7% of (CIF + duty) |
| VAT | $7,649 | 19% of (CIF + duty + CVD) |
| RoRo freight | $2,000 | — |
| Customs clearance | $350 | — |
| Certification | $3,250 | Homologation |
| Registration | $500 | — |
| Inland transport | $500 | — |
| **Total landed** | **$54,504** | **+79.8% over base** |

The countervailing duty ($6,904) is more than double the standard duty ($3,032) and is 22.8% of the base price on its own. The $6,600 fixed stack adds another 22 points of premium.

The Hongqi EH7 at $29,410 produces a nearly identical bill — CVD $6,697, VAT $7,419, total **$53,067 (+80.4%)** — because at this price point the two cars are tariff twins.

## Same car, different market: where the 20.7% disappears

The countervailing duty is an EU measure. The same cars land very differently outside the bloc:

| Model | Germany (EU) | UK (Non-EU) | UAE (Middle East) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xiaomi SU7 | $54,504 (+80%) | $46,272 (+53%) | $37,878 (+25%) |
| Hongqi EH7 | $53,067 (+80%) | $45,071 (+53%) | $36,875 (+25%) |

A **$16,600 difference on the SU7** between Germany and the UAE — almost entirely the 20.7% countervailing duty plus EU VAT. The UK's post-Brexit 10% duty with no CVD cuts the premium to +53%; the UAE's 5% duty and 5% VAT collapse it to +25%.

## What buyers should ask before importing a Xiaomi or Hongqi EV

1. **"Which brand am I actually buying?"** — both carry 20.7%, so the tariff tells you nothing. The decision is base price and positioning, not duty.
2. **"Is this a BEV or a PHEV/EREV?"** — the 20.7% applies to BEVs only. Xiaomi's SkyNomad EREVs and Hongqi's PHEVs skip the CVD.
3. **"What's the absolute duty bill, not the rate?"** — 20.7% on an $83,070 E HS9 is ~$18,900. Always ask for the dollar figure.
4. **"Am I registering in the EU?"** — if not, the 20.7% may not apply. Get the landed cost for your actual destination.
5. **"Does the rate differ from BYD's?"** — Xiaomi and Hongqi pay 20.7%, 3.7 points above BYD's 17%. The gap is real but smaller than base-price differences between specific models.

## The Author's Take

**Position:** In my view, the Xiaomi–Hongqi pairing is a tariff coincidence that obscures two different import problems — Xiaomi is a mid-premium play that survives the 20.7% intact, while Hongqi's wide range means its own cheapest car is its worst import.

**Reasoning:** First, the 20.7% is the "other cooperating" default shared by both, so the rate carries no signal about either brand; comparing them by tariff is meaningless because they are identical on paper. Second, Xiaomi's tightly clustered four-model lineup sits entirely in the band where the 20.7% produces a manageable +67% to +80% premium, so the brand is coherently importable. Third, Hongqi's range is its own undoing — the $12,650 E QM5 suffers a +110% premium from the fixed-cost stack long before the tariff matters, while the $83,070 E HS9 generates the largest dollar duty bill; a single brand cannot be treated as a single tariff story.

**Disclosure:** This is my interpretation of published EU tariff law and the EV Hub landed-cost records, not legal or tax advice. Countervailing-duty rates and covered exporters are set by EU regulation and can change; verify the current schedule with a customs broker before pricing anything.
