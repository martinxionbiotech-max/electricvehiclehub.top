# Australia Import Guide

Australia is one of the most attractive destinations for Chinese EVs — on the tax side, at least for most models, the country applies **zero import duty** to electric vehicles, no countervailing duty, and a 10% GST. The real hurdle is compliance, not cost.

## The Australia cost stack

| Item | Rate |
|---|---|
| Import duty (EVs) | 0% |
| Countervailing duty | none |
| GST | 10% |

With zero duty and no countervailing duty, a Chinese EV lands in Australia at a minimal tax premium. A BYD Seal at $24,690 base lands around **$33,009** — a +33.7% premium, driven mostly by freight, certification, and fixed costs rather than tax.

## Why Australia is structurally cheap

- **Zero import duty on EVs.** Australia has removed the 5% duty on many EVs under the Luxury Car Tax (LCT) fuel-efficiency threshold, effectively making the standard import duty zero for the mainstream EV segment.
- **No countervailing duty.** Nothing equivalent to the EU's anti-subsidy tariff exists in Australia.
- **GST is moderate.** 10% is lower than EU VAT and recoverable for GST-registered businesses.

## The compliance hurdle: ADRs and RAV

Cost is not the main obstacle in Australia — compliance is. Imported vehicles must meet the **Australian Design Rules (ADRs)** and be entered on the **Register of Approved Vehicles (RAV)**.

Key points:

- A China-market EV generally does **not** automatically satisfy ADRs.
- Importers typically need a **concessional RAV entry** or specialist approval for vehicles not type-approved for Australia.
- This can require physical modifications (lighting, child-restraint anchors, speedometer calibration) and documentation proving conformity.
- Budget more time and cost for Australian compliance than for, say, the Gulf — the standards are stricter and the process more formal.

## Practical notes

- **GST recoverability:** registered importers can claim GST back, making the effective tax burden lower than the headline 10% suggests.
- **Market maturity:** Australia has a fast-growing EV market and an established appetite for Chinese brands, but the compliance process is the gating factor — plan for it from day one.
