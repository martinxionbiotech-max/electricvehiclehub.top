# Germany Import Guide

Germany is the EU's largest automotive market and the natural first destination for most Chinese EV importers. It shares the EU-wide tariff stack but has specific registration and approval realities worth understanding before you commit.

## The cost stack for Germany

Germany applies the standard EU framework:

- **Standard import duty:** 10% of CIF value.
- **Countervailing duty:** producer-specific, 7.8% to 35.3%.
- **VAT:** 19% (the lowest in Western Europe's major EV markets).

The fixed costs we use for Germany in our estimates:

| Cost item | Typical (USD) |
|---|---|
| RoRo freight (China → North Europe port) | $2,000 |
| Customs clearance | $350 |
| Certification / homologation | $3,250 |
| Registration | $500 |
| Inland transport (port → buyer) | $500 |

## A worked example: BYD Seal

Take a BYD Seal at a $24,690 China base price. The calculation compounds:

| Line item | Amount (USD) |
|---|---|
| Base price | $24,690 |
| Standard duty (10%) | $2,469 |
| Countervailing duty (17%, BYD) | $4,617 |
| VAT (19%) | $6,037 |
| RoRo freight | $2,000 |
| Customs clearance | $350 |
| Certification | $3,250 |
| Registration | $500 |
| Inland transport | $500 |
| **Total landed** | **$44,413** |

That's a **+79.9%** premium over the base price — before any dealer margin.

## German-specific approval: §21 StVZO

For individual or small-volume imports, Germany offers a practical route via **§21 StVZO single-vehicle approval**. This lets a non-type-approved vehicle be registered based on an individual examination, typically through a technical inspection organisation (TÜV, DEKRA, GTÜ).

Key points:

- The vehicle must meet relevant technical requirements (lighting, safety, emissions-adjacent noise and electrical standards).
- A China-market EV may need adaptations (e.g., lighting conformity, speedometer display) before passing.
- Budget 2–6 weeks and $1,500–$5,000 for certification, depending on the vehicle and the extent of adaptation.

## Registration and ongoing costs

Once certified, registration is straightforward: local plates, a road-tax assessment (EVs benefit from a reduced or zero annual tax in Germany for the first years), and mandatory liability insurance.

## Why Germany works as an entry point

Germany's 19% VAT is the lowest among the major EU EV import destinations, and its single-vehicle approval route is well-trodden for imported vehicles. The counterweight: the EU countervailing duty applies in full, and for high-CVD producers (SAIC at 35.3%), Germany is no cheaper on the duty side than anywhere else in the EU.
