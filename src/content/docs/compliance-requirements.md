# Compliance Requirements Across Markets

Every country has its own rules for what makes an imported vehicle road-legal. The tax number is only half the story — conformity is the other half, and it varies materially by market. This page consolidates the requirements so you can plan before you order.

## The four regimes at a glance

| Market | Approval regime | Typical vehicle types | Cost (USD) | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EU | WVTA / Small Series / national single-vehicle | all | $1,500–$5,000 | 2–6 weeks |
| UK | IVA (Individual Vehicle Approval) | all | $1,500–$4,000 | 2–5 weeks |
| UAE / Saudi | GCC conformity + national standards | all | market-specific | 1–4 weeks |
| Australia | ADR + RAV entry | all | higher; specialist | 2–8 weeks |

## Europe (EU)

The EU's framework is the most mature: a vehicle must satisfy the applicable UN/ECE regulations and, in practice, pass one of the approval routes — full WVTA, Small Series, or a national single-vehicle approval. See [EU Homologation](/docs/eu-homologation/) for detail.

## United Kingdom

Post-Brexit, the UK runs its own IVA scheme via the DVSA. A China-market EV generally needs a Basic IVA, with adaptations such as mph speedometer display and right-hand-drive considerations where relevant. See [UK Import](/docs/uk-import/).

## Gulf (UAE & Saudi Arabia)

The Gulf applies its own conformity framework. Vehicles typically need to demonstrate compliance with GCC standards and the relevant national authority's requirements (SFDA/SASO-adjacent in Saudi Arabia). The process is generally lighter than the EU's, but the requirements are not identical between the UAE and Saudi Arabia — verify the specific market before importing.

## Australia

Australia is the most demanding on documentation. Imported vehicles must meet ADRs and be entered on the Register of Approved Vehicles (RAV), usually via a concessional entry for vehicles not type-approved for Australia. Physical modifications (lighting, child-restraint anchors, speedometer) are frequently required. See [Australia Import](/docs/australia-import/).

## A practical checklist

Before committing to any import, confirm these five things:

1. **Does the specific vehicle have an existing approval in the target market?** (Some Chinese brands already type-approve models for export markets — this is the fastest path.)
2. **What is the required approval route for your volume?** Single vehicle, small series, or full type approval.
3. **What physical adaptations will the vehicle need?** Lighting, display, safety anchor points.
4. **Who will perform the conformity assessment?** An accredited technical service (TÜV, DVSA, RAV assessor, etc.).
5. **What documentation can the supplier provide up front?** COC, export license, and any existing type-approval certificates.
